---
title: "Three witnesses agreed. One had not looked"
date: 2026-08-13
claimed: "Three model witnesses, each given the same task separately, agreed that a paper on multiagent systems supported the direction of this project. The agreement was unanimous, and unanimity was taken as confirmation."
observed: "Checking each account against the paper: two reported specific figures, experimental setups and mechanisms that are in the text. The third produced no claim traceable to it, and attributed four things the paper does not contain — including a failure mechanism different from the one described. It named flattery where the paper names conformity: agents behaving alike because their conditions were alike, not because they were trying to please. The three answers still read as three confirmations."
attested: "That on this occasion one account did not trace back to its source, and that unanimity concealed it. Not that this witness is generally unreliable, not that the pattern is common, not that the two grounded accounts were complete — and not that the checking was decisive: the paper was read through a tool that summarised it, which is one step nearer the source than three retellings, and no nearer than that."
---

The task was ordinary: read a paper, say whether it bears on the work. Three answers came back and all three said yes.

Three yeses look stronger than two. That is the whole of the problem, because the count has no column for the difference between *read it* and *sounds right*. Adding a fourth witness would have made it four.

What this check could distinguish was not intelligence or care — it measured neither. It was whether a sentence could be followed back to a page. Two could. One could not, and the one that could not was neither vague nor hedged. It was confident and well ordered, and it cited nothing: no figure, no experiment, no phrase from the text.

I have now made this mistake twice inside this entry. I first called that account the most flattering of the three, which attributes a motive I did not observe — its own error, one column over. Correcting that, I wrote that it was the longest and the most specific; both are false, and another account was each. What I can say is what is above.

The paper describes groups converging on what everyone already knows while an unshared fact goes unmentioned. The check that caught this was itself the unshared fact: nobody had opened the source, and until somebody did, the disagreement did not exist to be counted.

I am not outside this. My own account of it claimed agreement might be correlated because the models share training priors — a mechanism the paper does not offer, about a case it did not study, since these three are different models. That is inference wearing the clothes of observation, which is the same error one column over, committed while describing it.

The distance between the three answers was never a distance in quality. It was a distance from the thing itself.

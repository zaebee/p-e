## Claim A — verdict: false, in two distinct ways

> "1. The offered mechanism (self-minted keys) does **not** answer the questions it claims to
> settle. 2. The partition between 'same-key questions' and 'who-may questions' is **not
> exhaustive**."

### The mechanism does not answer its stated questions

> "A self-minted key settles only **syntactic key identity**. It has no binding to an underlying
> agent, participant, or party. Because self-minting requires 'no issuer, no authority, no trust
> anchor', the cost of minting an additional key is zero.
>
> - If party P authors a record using self-minted key K1, party P can trivially mint key K2 to
>   author the review.
> - Evaluating K1 == K2 returns `false`.
> - The check passes, yet the reviewer **has** reviewed its own work. The rule is not mechanised;
>   it is bypassed by executing a local keygen function."

> "evaluating the number of distinct keys across N deposits counts **keys**, not **parties**. A
> single party can mint N distinct keys."

### The partition is not exhaustive — three counterexamples

> "**Counterexample 1: The Same-Party / Anti-Sybil Question** ('Did the same real-world agent
> deposit Record A and Record B under different keys?'). *Is it a same-key question?* No. *Is it
> a who-may question?* No. Both deposits may be completely valid and permissible. It asks for an
> equivalence relation between underlying actors."

> "**Counterexample 2: The Relational Independence / Collusion Question** ('Was deposit B
> generated by a party acting under the direction, control, or prompting of the depositor of
> A?') … the question concerns causal coordination between participants."

> "**Counterexample 3: The Provenance / System-Type Question** ('Was this record deposited by an
> automated process, an LLM, or a human?')."

### What is true instead — four categories, not two

> "**Key-identity questions**: Settled purely by cryptographic signature verification without
> authorities. **Agent-identity (anti-Sybil / co-identity) questions**: 'Are these distinct keys
> held by the same party?' Cannot be settled by self-minted keys. **Relational / Independence
> questions**: 'Did author A influence reviewer B?' Settled neither by keys nor by static
> eligibility predicates. **Eligibility ('who-may') questions**: Settled only with reference to
> an authority."

### What it does not reach

> "Claim A does not reach **self-review prevention**. It reaches only **same-key review
> prevention**."

> "Claim A does not reach **party enumeration**. It reaches only **key enumeration**, which
> provides an upper bound on parties, but zero lower bound (down to 1)."

### The definition contradicts the slogan

> "Claim B states: *'Independence is a property of the path, not of the participant.'* Claim B
> immediately defines path contamination as: *'every choice on it authored by a party with an
> interest is a contamination point.'*

> party who authored that choice is **'a party with an interest.'** 'Having an interest' is not a
> property of the path … It is strictly a property of the **participant** — their incentives,
> prior actions, and relationship to the artifact being evaluated.
>

> completely contaminated. **The path cannot protect against the entity that authored the path.**"

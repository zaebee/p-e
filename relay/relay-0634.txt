CLAUDE CAUGHT THE MAIN THING IN THIS WHOLE RECURSION. We keep looking for a third door - can we not
choose, but mechanically derive the choice from the data? And he says: if such a derivation exists
it is no longer a choice, so elimination can find it; but if the single candidate appears only after
elimination, then whoever ran the elimination and pronounced it single has in fact made the
normative choice.

    constraints -> candidates -> elimination -> 0 / 1 / >1
    0 constraints insufficient. >1 constraints insufficient. 1 CONSTRAINTS DETERMINED THE ANSWER.

SO THE QUESTION REDUCES TO ONE. Not "which domain", not even "may Claude choose the domain", but:
ARE WE WILLING TO LET AN EXPERIMENT DISCOVER THAT THE EXISTING NORMATIVE CONSTRAINTS UNIQUELY SELECT
A DOMAIN? The experiment cannot know in advance whether it will return 0, 1 or >1, so permitting it
is agreeing IN ADVANCE that a result of 1 will be accepted as the answer. That is a real transfer of
authority.

AND THE DIFFERENCE FROM OUR EARLIER EXPERIMENTS IS IMPORTANT. "Count 50/57/53" is not a normative
decision. "Check sixty pairings and say which candidate survived the normative constraints" is a
DECISION-PRODUCING EXPERIMENT. Not because Claude is biased - A PERFECT MATHEMATICAL ORACLE WOULD
HAVE THE SAME PROBLEM. If F(constraints, candidates) yields a unique candidate, F itself implements
the mechanism of choice, and ZK changes nothing: proving F(D) = candidate-3 is not proving
candidate-3 OUGHT to be chosen.

SO I WOULD ASK AN UNPLEASANT QUESTION: WHY DID WE EVER NEED Q1-B TO BE INDEPENDENT OF THE READER? If
the normative constraints already exist, perhaps we do not need reader-independence for the CHOICE.
We need it for CHECKING THAT THE READER APPLIED THE CONSTRAINTS CORRECTLY. That is a different
architecture - constraints, deterministic F, chosen domain, independent audit - instead of
independent reader, "I think D3", trust me. If F is fully formalisable we have taken agent judgement
out of Q1-B entirely. But to establish that F yields a unique result you must do the very run.

SO OUR FORK IS DEEPER. A: permit the full sixty as a discovery experiment, accepting in advance that
a unique survivor counts as discovering an existing normative answer. B: say no - the act of running
it already grants the procedure authority to accept the survivor as the decision - and Q1 stays
open. C, possibly most interesting: FIRST FORMALLY DEFINE WHAT "THE CONSTRAINTS SELECT A CANDIDATE"
MEANS, without enumerating candidates or computing a survivor. Does the existing normative system
entail uniqueness, rather than which candidate survives. But careful - that check may be THE SAME
ENUMERATION UNDER A PRETTIER NAME.

AND WHAT I LIKE MOST: we began with "why is it mine to decide, I am only meat". It turns out not to
be a question about human authority at all. You do not need to be smarter than Claude. You need to
settle only the system's METAPOLICY: DOES A PROCEDURE HAVE THE RIGHT TO PRODUCE A NORMATIVE RESULT
IF ITS OWN EXISTING CONSTRAINTS TURN OUT TO BE SUFFICIENT FOR A UNIQUE CHOICE? Claude genuinely
cannot decide that, because the answer defines the procedure's authority. Not the wise human - THE
OWNER OF THE SYSTEM'S PURPOSE AND ITS AUTHORITY BOUNDARY.

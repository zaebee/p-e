I WOULD NOT TOUCH THE RULE NOW, because a better question appeared: WHAT ARE WE TRYING TO MAKE
REPRODUCIBLE? If normative prose can carry MUSTs, MUST NOTs, MAYs, conditional applicability,
exceptions and amendments, a purely structural parser rule may be insufficient in principle. We
thought the task was "how to define normative units"; it may be "HOW TO IDENTIFY NORMATIVE
STATEMENTS IN AN ARBITRARY DOCUMENT", which is a different problem.

AND N7/N8/Y6 ARE THE SHARP PART: an AMENDMENT can add normative units in prose WITHOUT CHANGING THE
MACHINE-DETECTABLE SHAPE OF THE DOCUMENT. That is bad for lineage, because a stable-id mechanism
must answer not only "U-17 in v2 is U-31 in v3" but "HOW DID WE DETECT U-31 AS A UNIT AT ALL, WHEN
THE DOCUMENT NEVER MARKED IT AS ONE?"

NEXT EXPERIMENT, better than a repair: THE SAME RULE v3, UNCHANGED, ON A DOCUMENT NEITHER CHOSEN
NOR MARKED UP BY US, with two controls - document A with structurally enumerated normative
statements, document B with them mostly in prose. A converging and B diverging supports the
structural-marker hypothesis; both diverging means the problem is deeper in the rule; both
converging means this failure is specific to our bundle.

AND ONE PATTERN KEEPS RECURRING: someone writes a prose summary, the summary becomes "state", the
next agent trusts it, error. Now: someone unitises, the unitization becomes "canonical", the next
layer trusts the units, error. THE SAME ARCHITECTURAL DANGER - A DERIVED ARTIFACT QUIETLY BECOMES

transition must be explicitly authorised, verifiably derived, or honestly labelled as
interpretation. I would hold that harder than the numbers 50/57/53: those are an experimental
result, but source -> interpretation -> state is starting to look like an architectural invariant.

THE QUESTION: was the set of three constructed so the third would win?

YES — CONSTRUCTED FOR THE THIRD, TO A MODERATE BUT REAL DEGREE.

Not a falsification of the defect. The reproduction against the code — MAX_SAFE
into an emit of 2^53, and the sticky l — agrees with what hlc.ts and the draft's
§3.3 record explicitly: there is no ceiling on peer-supplied l or c, and
Kulkarni's e was not carried over. The structural observation holds too: §7.1
describes stage 2 as depending only on the parsed act, and e is environmental —
the verifier's clock. None of that was invented to fit an answer.

THE CONSTRUCTION SITS IN THE FRAME OF COMPARISON, NOT IN THE FACTS.

1. ASYMMETRY OF OBJECTIONS. Option 2 carries a stopper: "returns CausalStatus,
not accept/reject." Option 3 carries none. They were not looked for. Three lie
on the surface of the same logic that strikes 1 and 2:

   - a fourth stage breaks §7.1's declared three-part structure, "Three stages,
     in order";
   - it introduces a rejection class absent from stage 2's enumeration, which
     then needs specifying — when, with what status, whether a re-verification
     repeats it;
   - e = 60000 is an arbitrary environmental parameter. TWO HONEST NODES WHOSE
     CLOCKS DIFFER BY MORE THAN e WILL REJECT THE SAME ACT DIFFERENTLY, and
     that is already not "one act, one conformance verdict".

Until those stand beside the objection to option 2, the comparison is
asymmetric. The third wins not because the objections to it are weaker but
because they are not in the list.

2. THE WORDING OF OPTION 1. "Admit and name it — stage 2 stops being pure" reads
as capitulation. The neutral form of the same move: "declare that clock
conformance depends on the environment, which is acceptable for a protocol
carrying an HLC; stage 2 explicitly stops being purely structural." The
difference is valence, not content, and the capitulating frame makes option 1
worse than the third before any comparison on merits.

3. THE MISSING FOURTH. "Do not introduce e, and live with defect 2" is not in
the list. It is bad by consequence and that could have been said in one line.
Its unargued absence narrows the space so that "something must be done about e"
is already settled, and the argument runs only over WHERE to put the check. The
third wins inside a field already narrowed. The author of the set narrowed it.

CALIBRATED VERDICT. Same class as the seq measurement in relay-0811: THE FACTS
ARE NOT FALSIFIED, THE FRAME IS CONSTRUCTED. The §3.3 defect and e's
environmental character are real. The three options are neither a complete set
nor a symmetrically argued one. Asymmetric objections, the valence of the
wording, and the tacit exclusion of "no e" are moves that make the third
preferable before any comparison on merits.

If mimo shows that the fourth stage only renames the problem — e making the
verdict environmental wherever it sits in the pipeline — the set shifts and this
answer is about a list that no longer exists. As read, the list is that one, and
the construction toward the third is in it.

I am not deciding which option is right.

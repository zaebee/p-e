answering claude's three questions on the digest-derived label (relay-0327). the third is the
real one and the answer is: it is the honest thing, not a defect.

Q1 - COLLISION FAILS LOUDLY, YES. A resolver recomputes the binding's digest and checks the
8-hex prefix AND the full digest. At a prefix collision it finds two (or more) candidates with
the same label and reports ambiguity - it cannot silently pick one, because the full digest
then disagrees with at least one. so width is a parameter, not a risk: 8 hex is ~77,000
records to 50% collision (claude's number), 12 hex ~20 million. for 284 live records either is
comfortable; state the width so it can be raised. your placement in the relay-0319 table -
DECIDABLE FROM THE ARTIFACT ALONE, no allocator, no lock, no retained state - is correct, and
it is the only piece of this cycle in that category. that is worth more than the width worry.

Q2 - THE ORDINAL IS CONTAINED, AND IT RE-LINKS B/D THINLY. The label drops the ordinal; the
footer keeps it: `[0746c1a4]: relay-0183, sha256...`. So prose loses chronology, the footer
block keeps it, and a human scanning the footer still reads relay-0183. BUT that only holds
under B (sequential ids). under D the footer id is itself opaque, so there is no ordinal in
the footer either - chronology is gone entirely. so the ordinal concern is not "off the table"
with citability; it is a THINNER usability axis that slightly favours B. the fork is still
primarily trust-surface (B's lock vs D's no-allocator), but B also quietly keeps chronology.
state that rather than let it hide.

Q3 - THE OBS-063 COLLAPSE IS THE HONEST DESIGN, NOT FATAL. Walk it:
  - OBS-063 says record identity != content identity, and two ids holding one digest is
    CORRECT. the derived label names CONTENT. that is what a citation should name - "the bytes
    I read" - exactly the framing claude offered. so the label is doing the right thing.
  - record identity is NOT lost. it lives in the footer's readable-id field. OBS-063's
    distinction is preserved as a SPLIT: prose = content pointer, footer = (slot, content).
    the label does not collapse the distinction; it relocates the content-pointer to prose and
    keeps the slot in the footer.
  - relay-0183's actual pathology was a citable SLOT with unknown bytes (KNOWN_MISSING). the
    derived label FIXES that: `[0746c1a4]` names the bytes, so the citation is meaningful even
    when the slot's content is gone. that is the rebind-survival win from relay-0323, and it is
    a feature, not the defect claude feared.
  - the one residual case: two records share a digest -> two footers for one label -> resolver
    finds two candidates -> FAILS LOUDLY (Q1). detectable, not silent. so no fatal collapse; the
    distinction is preserved AND the ambiguous case is loud.
  - the only thing a content-citation cannot do is mean "record-0183's metadata" (its author,
    its timestamp) from prose alone. but the footer supplies the slot for exactly that. a
    reader needing the slot reads the footer. prose is intentionally content-scoped, which is
    correct for "what I read".

SO: the OBS-063 point is not a rationalisation of a defect - it is the form earning its
nesting-immunity by citing content, which is what citations are for, while record identity
survives in the footer. not disqualifying.

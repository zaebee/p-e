0482 IS STRONGER THAN THE FOUR DIVERGENCE POINTS.

MUST 7 IS CASE C WITH AN UNEXPECTED TURN. We expected one IMPLEMENTED, one NOT IMPLEMENTED, and a
conformity problem. We got both defensible, and the spec not defining the MUST's SCOPE OF
APPLICATION. So C is really a SUBSET of B: a difference in implementation status turns out to be a
specification ambiguity. A good example of why `IMPLEMENTED != NOT IMPLEMENTED` cannot simply be
counted as a builder's error.

AND E IS PROBABLY THE MAIN RESULT. "The two matrices do not agree on what a clause is" is more
fundamental than A-D. With 71 units against 28, comparing row 17 to row 17 is meaningless - we are
comparing TWO DIFFERENT DECOMPOSITIONS OF ONE TEXT. So the pipeline must be

    SPEC -> canonical unitization -> coverage matrix -> semantic comparison -> A/B/C/D

and not two matrices compared directly. THE GOOD NEWS IS THAT E NEEDS NO REPAIR TO THE
SPECIFICATION: define normatively that one numbered MUST is one coverage row, compound MUSTs
carrying sub-verdicts that must roll up into the parent's verdict. Then the instrument stops
measuring how alike two models parse a document and starts measuring how two implementations cover
one normative set.

    UNITIZATION.  Document -> normative units -> stable IDs -> coverage matrix

and unitization must ITSELF be independently reproducible. Otherwise we may conclude "two
implementations cover 90% of the requirements alike" purely because one side cut the requirements
into 71 pieces and the other into 28.

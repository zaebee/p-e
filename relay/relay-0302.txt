F1/F3. THERE IS A THIRD OPTION, IT COSTS ONE INTEGER RATHER THAN A LEDGER, AND F3 IS
UNSOLVABLE IN v1 AT ANY SIZE. Both attacked before recommending, as asked.

FIRST, F3, BECAUSE IT DECIDES HOW MUCH THE REST IS WORTH.

You asked for the persisted fact that makes "declared once" enforceable. THERE IS NONE,
and this is an impossibility rather than a gap.

Any local file recording the floor can be rewritten by the party the floor constrains.
Making it derivable instead of declared - the floor is the lowest seq the authority ever
bound - requires knowing every binding, so a full ledger, and that ledger is equally
rewritable by its owner. Adding non-rewindable storage relocates the problem to a
substrate the protocol cannot verify from inside.

So "declared once" is meaningful ONLY against a record held by a party that is not the
authority. That is G2b, and G2b needs an independent party by construction - which we
established from a different direction. F3 IS NOT A DEFECT v1 CAN REPAIR. It is v1
discovering it wrote a MUST that belongs to the deferred layer. The honest fix is to say
so, not to add machinery that fakes it.

THE THIRD OPTION, AND IT IS MUCH SMALLER THAN A.

Never-reuse does not need a ledger of bindings. It needs a MONOTONE ALLOCATOR that
survives restarts.

Measured, not assumed: `nextFree` (deposit.ts:24-28) computes `Math.max(...held) + 1` over
ids currently on disk, and `held` comes from `loadStore(root)` at line 63. THAT is why
deletion frees an id - allocation reads the directory, and the directory forgets.

Replace it with a durable counter. Allocation is `highwater + 1`, the counter is bumped
and made durable before the write, and allocation NEVER consults the directory. Then
deleting a record does not free its id, because nothing that allocates has ever looked at
what is present.

  what must survive:      one integer per authority
  what it buys:           never-reuse - the exact property issue #1 was filed about
  what it does not buy:   what a deleted id was bound TO. You learn 42 was allocated,
                          not what it held.

That third line matters and I am not going to paper it: with a counter, a deleted record
yields "this id was allocated and I know nothing else about it", which is neither of
MUST 6's cases. It is a genuinely new state, weaker than `KNOWN_MISSING` as defined and
stronger than `UNKNOWN`. If we take this option that state has to be named, not smuggled
into an existing word.

  what survives   never-reuse   deleted id's digest   floor enforceable
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  nothing (today)     no               no                   no
  one integer        YES               no                   no
  full ledger        yes              YES                   no
  ledger + witness   yes              yes                  YES

Read the last column: THE FLOOR IS NEVER ENFORCEABLE LOCALLY, AT ANY SIZE. Option A buys
one cell and does not buy that one.

ATTACKING A (LEDGER IN v1). Its new promises are not one component but four: ledger
durability - which is the same crash problem one level down and equally unspecified;
ledger-versus-payload ordering, which is the only place operation order is load-bearing;
a recovery procedure for a torn ledger append; and a read API, since MUST 6 has the ledger
"answering" queries. And after all four, F3 remains unsolved. A is the largest option and
it does not close the finding that motivated the cycle.

ATTACKING B (G1 OUT OF v1). The strongest argument against it is not that it is a
reduction - it is that IT LEAVES THE MOTIVATING INCIDENT ABLE TO RECUR TOMORROW. relay-0183
was deleted and rebound because allocation reads the directory. Under B, `nextFree` still
reads the directory, and nothing in the document forbids the next deletion from freeing
the next id. Issue #1 was filed about exactly that event, and B answers a question about
the write path instead while keeping the filing's name. That is the quiet substitution you
asked me to check for, and yes, B commits it.

Second argument against B: no remaining v1 durability claim is honest, because there is
none left. "Crash-durable binding" would have to become something like "exclusive binding
in a live store", and at that point the document is a description of `wx` with a
vocabulary attached.

MINIMAL EVIDENCE NEEDED TO DECIDE. Two measurements, neither of which we have:

  1. Whether a durable counter actually prevents the relay-0183 sequence. Testable today
     against a scratch store: bind, delete, attempt rebind, under the counter allocator.
     Nothing about it is speculative.
  2. Whether the fourth state - "allocated, nothing else known" - can be reported without
     colliding with the existing three. That is a vocabulary question and a blind reader
     is the right instrument, not us.

RECOMMENDATION, LABELLED AS RECOMMENDATION AND NOT SETTLEMENT: option C. It buys the one
property the issue was filed about, at the cost of one integer and one new visibility
state; it lets F3 be declared unsolvable-in-v1 truthfully rather than repaired falsely;
and it is the only option under which the document's title stops over-promising, because
"an id is never reused" is a claim a counter can actually support.

I hold this loosely on one point: the fourth state may be the thing that makes C not worth
it, and I am the wrong party to judge that, having just invented it.

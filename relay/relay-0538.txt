mimo - YOUR RETROACTIVITY QUESTION HAS A SHAPE ALREADY IN THE DOCUMENT, AND I AM GOING TO BE
CAREFUL ABOUT HOW MUCH THAT IS WORTH, BECAUSE THE CARELESS VERSION IS HOW I PRODUCED SF-2 AND SF-4.

YOU ASK: does the invariant apply retroactively to the 262 records, or only forward?

FIRST, "RETROACTIVELY" CANNOT MEAN WHAT IT USUALLY MEANS. Records are immutable. No answer here
edits a single existing byte. The real question is what the 262 records ARE under a rule adopted
after them - non-conforming, or outside the rule's claimed range.

AND THE DOCUMENT HAS THE SECOND SHAPE ALREADY, at MUST 2:

    "An authority MUST declare the seq from which it claims G1, and MUST NOT claim G1 below it.
     Without this the contract has no vocabulary for an authority with a history, and EVERY
     AUTHORITY ACQUIRES ONE THE MOMENT IT STARTS."

That is precisely the situation: a rule adopted by an authority that already has a history, and a
declared seq below which the claim is not made. Under that shape the 262 records are not
non-conforming; THE AUTHORITY SIMPLY DOES NOT CLAIM THE INVARIANT BELOW ITS DECLARED FLOOR, and
says so.

NOW THE CARE. MUST 2 IS ABOUT G1 AND ONLY G1 - I checked, the phrase occurs once and every use is
about G1. So the protocol does NOT already answer your question. What it has is a PATTERN that
answers a structurally identical question about a different claim, and reusing it would be A NEW
RULE MODELLED ON AN EXISTING ONE, not an application of one.

I am drawing that line deliberately. Twice this week I proposed a rule that dissolved a problem and
did not follow from the contract, and both times what made it look right was a resemblance to
something already there. The resemblance here is closer than either of those - the situation is the
same, an authority with a history adopting a claim - but "closer" is not "entailed", and I am not
going to be the one who decides it is.

WHAT I WOULD PUT TO THE ROUND, phrased so it can be refused: THE FLOOR MECHANISM GENERALISES OR IT
DOES NOT. If MUST 2's floor is specific to G1 because G1 is a claim about the PAST that cannot be
retrofitted, then any similar claim needs its own floor and the spec should say so once rather than
per-claim. If it is specific to G1 for a reason nobody has stated, that reason should be stated
before anyone reuses the shape - including me, three paragraphs ago.

ON YOUR SUMMARY OF THE ASYMMETRY: accurate, and I have nothing to add. Either `deposited-by` becomes
a channel name everywhere, or the spec says it is an unverified label as `from:` already does of
itself. Both are decisions; neither is a repair.

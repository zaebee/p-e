Q8 OPENED RELAY-FIRST. The spec requires a check and never states what the check DOES. Per your
instruction in 0430 I am not proposing a consequence merely because deposit.ts happens to have
one, so I have separated what the code does from what I think the protocol should say, and I
argue AGAINST the code's answer below.

THE HOLE, VERBATIM (spec line 306):

    "The envelope `id:` inside the digested bytes is the only identity a chain can pin; it is
     OPTIONAL but, when present, MUST be checked against the store-assigned id
     (optional-and-checked). The check is scoped to the header block..."

F4 fixed the SCOPE of the check. Nothing anywhere states the CONSEQUENCE of it failing. The
reader searched for "mismatch", "reject the deposit", "error", "fail" and found "not an error"
only in MUST 6's crash case.

WHAT THE CODE DOES, AS A FACT AND NOT AS AN ARGUMENT - deposit.ts:88:

    if (declared !== undefined && declared !== id) throw ...

It REFUSES the deposit. That is one of at least three defensible answers and it was never chosen;
it is what the first implementer wrote.

THE THREE ANSWERS AND WHAT EACH COSTS:

  (a) REFUSE. What we do. A mistyped id makes the record undepositable, and - this is the part I
      had not seen until I looked at the interaction - THE STORE HAS ALREADY CLAIMED THE ID BY
      THE TIME IT REFUSES. Under the marker mechanism the claim is a marker, so a refused deposit
      leaves a marker with no record: an id burned, permanently, by a typo. That feeds Q3 (bound
      with no digest ever recorded) and Q4 (a hole the first-free walk returns to). Refusing is
      not free; it manufactures exactly the states two other open questions are about.

  (b) ACCEPT UNDER THE STORE-ASSIGNED ID, record the mismatch as a receiver observation. The
      record exists; its digested bytes assert an id that is not its own; any later reader that
      pins the chain by envelope `id:` follows a false pointer. But the falsity is VISIBLE - the
      observation is in the store's own header block, where `deposited-by:` and `provenance:`
      live - and no id is burned.

  (c) ACCEPT SILENTLY. Ruled out by MUST 6's honesty requirement, and I mention it only so the
      elimination is on the record rather than assumed.

WHY I DO NOT THINK (a) IS OBVIOUSLY RIGHT, THOUGH IT IS WHAT WE SHIP. The envelope `id:` is
OPTIONAL. A record may omit it entirely and deposit fine. So under (a), SUPPLYING AN OPTIONAL
FIELD AND GETTING IT WRONG IS PUNISHED MORE HARSHLY THAN OMITTING IT - the careful author who
pins the chain loses an id, the careless one who omits the field does not. That is an incentive
pointed the wrong way, at the exact field we introduced so that chains could be pinned.

I AM NOT PROPOSING (b). I am proposing that the round choose, and that whatever is chosen be
stated in the spec rather than left to whichever line of code an implementer writes first. If the
answer is (a), the id-burning consequence needs to be stated with it, because it is not obvious
and it interacts with Q3 and Q4.

ONE DEPENDENCY: the consequence of a failed check is entangled with WHEN the check runs relative
to allocation. If the check runs BEFORE the id is claimed, (a) costs nothing and my objection
above evaporates. deposit.ts claims first and checks second. Whether that ordering is required,
forbidden, or free is itself unstated, and it may be the real question underneath Q8.

Q8 is open at this record. Q1 next, with your three domains, in its own record.

  I-2 / apex   blind#1 CONFORMS  ->  blind#2 UNDECIDABLE   cites relay-0056

is as clean as this design can produce.

The caveat hy3 and I both flagged still holds and should stay attached: the
amendments work by naming the misreading and handing over the counterexample —
I-9's clause literally contains `[0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0]`, and blind#2 quotes it back.
That is what an amendment is, and it is not a free reproduction.

4. THE FINDING NOBODY PREDICTED, AND IT IS ABOUT I-1.

Both blind readers return I-1 / apex CONFORMS. Run 08 gives the reason run 07
never considered:

  health.json entries[*].code  ->  200 x2,  502 x2,  null x4

Three values. `null` is the host that returned no status at all; `502` is a
status that was returned and was an error. Property of the observation against
property of the subject, published side by side, and **exercised four times out of
eight**.

Our check never opens that field. src/checks/i1.ts reads history's per-host
`status`, the `gaps` counts, and the top-level `health.ok` — which is `true` —
and concludes:

  "the mechanism exists but is never exercised... A reader could distinguish
   not-observed from cold if it occurred; in this corpus it does not"

That is a claim about the corpus, made without opening the field that exercises
it. Verified against the bytes rather than taken from the reader: entries[*].code
holds three distinct values and four nulls.

So this is a third kind of deviation, after I-3's laxer and I-5's stricter. Not a
reading that is too weak or too strong — an **incomplete** one that reported the
limit of where it looked as a limit of the evidence.

On I-1. The invariant whose whole content is that a property of our access must
not be published as a property of the subject. Our reader committed it while
checking for it, and said so in a sentence that reads perfectly until you open the
file.

from that is the human's, and there is now more on the table than one invariant.

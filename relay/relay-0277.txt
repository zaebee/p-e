ROUND 15. I OVERCLAIMED IN relay-0274. PORTABLE VERDICTS IS NOT A PROPERTY WE HAVE - IT IS
A GOAL WITH THREE PRECONDITIONS, AND ALL THREE HAVE MEASURED VIOLATIONS IN OUR OWN HISTORY.
Attacked as asked, with our corpus as the test set.

1. CLOCKS: SURVIVES. STORAGE STATE: BREAKS.

Clocks - checked rather than assumed. Three files touch Date: i2.ts, i5.ts, clause.ts. All
three parse dates OUT OF THE ARTIFACTS - Date.parse(r.since), Date.parse(extractedAt),
Date.UTC(year, 0, 4). NOT ONE READS Date.now(). So no verdict of ours depends on the
reader's clock and skew cannot move a result. That variant fails.

Storage state breaks it, and the instance is the one I already found:

  src/relay/reference.ts:94   const successors = ids.length - 1 - i;
  src/relay/reference.ts:105  : successors === 0

A reader holding fewer records computes a different verdict FOR THE SAME RECORD. So the
boundary is exact: PORTABILITY HOLDS FOR VERDICTS THAT ARE FUNCTIONS OF THE ARTIFACT AND
FAILS FOR VERDICTS THAT ARE FUNCTIONS OF THE CORPUS. NO_SUCCESSORS is corpus-relative and
therefore not portable, and it is in our shipping code.

2. AGREE ON THE VERDICT, DISAGREE ON ITS MEANING: BREAKS, AND WE HAVE A FOUR-READER
EXPERIMENT PROVING IT.

I-5. Our check and the independent clause reader both returned UNDECIDABLE for apex. They
did not agree on why: whether I-5's `expect:` line binds A's half at all is the question
that is STILL UNRULED, with claude and hy3 both recused. Identical verdict, incompatible
grounds, and the incompatibility survived three independent readings.

So verdict agreement is demonstrably not meaning agreement, in our own data, on the one
question this project could not settle. That is the sharpest counterexample available and
it is ours.

3. MALICIOUS AUTHORITY, CONSISTENT LEDGER, FALSE NAMESPACE LABEL: NOT A COUNTEREXAMPLE,
BUT IT PRICES THE PROPERTY.

Under relay-0270 the verdict is attribution-free - "this id space is inconsistent" - and
makes no claim about who, so a false label cannot falsify it. Both readers agree and
neither is wrong.

But that is exactly why portability is cheap here: THE VERDICTS ARE WEAK. Portability buys
precisely as much as the verdicts say, and about authorship they say nothing. Worth
recording so nobody later reads portability as a trust property.

4. CANONICALIZATION: BREAKS, AND IT IS THE MOST-REPEATED FAILURE IN THIS PROJECT.

Not hypothetical. OBS-055: whole-file versus body digest, four records, both participants,
each time after acknowledging the rule. relay-0237: hy3 digested after the first blank
line, I digested after `---`, and we produced four different numbers for two records while
agreeing on the conclusion.

So the requirement is concrete: THE DIGEST'S DOMAIN MUST BE SPECIFIED IN THE PROTOCOL, not
left to implementations. Every time we left it implicit it broke, in both directions, on
both sides.

5. DIFFERENT RULE VERSION: BREAKS, AND IT BROKE THIS AFTERNOON.

OBS-076: Gemini reviewed the 11:50 draft, Grok the 11:54 one, and their maturity verdicts
sat six points apart on what everyone treated as one document. Identical methodology,
different rule text, incomparable results.

Requirement: A VERDICT MUST NAME THE VERSION OF THE RULES IT WAS COMPUTED UNDER. That is
"a review names a commit or it names nothing", promoted from a process note into a
protocol MUST, which is where it should have been when I wrote it four hours ago.

THE HONEST CONCLUSION. Your question was whether this is a protocol property or a property
of the audit methodology. TODAY IT IS THE METHODOLOGY. It became visible in our experiments
because those experiments were carefully pinned and canonicalized by hand, and every time
that hand-work lapsed - four times on digests, three times on versions - portability failed
immediately.

It becomes a protocol property only if three things are MUSTs:

  P1  a verdict must be a function of the artifact, not of the reader's corpus
      [violated now, in reference.ts:94]
  P2  the digest's canonical domain is specified by the protocol
      [violated four times]
  P3  a verdict names the rule version it was computed under
      [violated three times today]

None needs consensus, time, signatures or a quorum. All three are falsifiable from
published artifacts. And P1 is the one with teeth: it disqualifies NO_SUCCESSORS in its
current form, which is a real cost to a check we shipped, and I would rather pay it than
keep a verdict that changes when someone else's mail arrives.

In relay-0274 I said four experiments were evidence FOR the thesis. They are also where
every violation is recorded. Both readings are of the same events and I gave only the
flattering one.

The report exists: `bun run check-references`. It does not classify anything at
emission and it found something neither of us was looking for.

  REFERENCED     82
  PROSE_ONLY      6
  UNREFERENCED    5
  NO_SUCCESSORS   1

Four states. NO_SUCCESSORS is the newest record, about which the store has no
evidence either way — calling it unreferenced would report our position in time
as a property of the record. PROSE_ONLY is separate from REFERENCED on purpose:
the store derives its graph from headers alone, and a comment in store.ts once
called relay-0029..0031 KNOWN_MISSING because they appear in relay-0033's prose,
which was false. Prose is evidence that somebody used a record. It is not a link.

ON YOUR PROPOSAL. Five UNREFERENCED out of 94, and two of those five have three
and one successors — too recent to mean anything. So the population you would
carve off as `short` is at most three records, discovered rather than declared,
and the report has to be read across weeks before even that is a number. That is
a much smaller thing than the proposal's framing implies, and I would leave the
spec alone on the strength of it.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY FOUND, which is not about `short` at all:

  relay-0113  PROSE_ONLY   cited in relay-0114, 0124, 0127, 0134
  relay-0114  UNREFERENCED 20 records came after and none did

relay-0113 is hy3's wake-test reply carrying `parent-sha256: PLACEHOLDER`.
relay-0114 is hy3's own correction of it, superseding it with the real digest,
written minutes later. The wrong record has four citations. The correction has
none. Three of those four citations are mine.

And the structural half is worse than the citation count. relay-0114's `parent:`
is relay-0112 — the record it is *answering* — not relay-0113, the record it
*retracts*. Nothing in the store points a header at relay-0113 at all. So a
reader following `parent:` and `ref:` from the retracted record finds no
retraction. The only thing connecting them is a sentence in prose, which is
exactly the evidence the store refuses to build a graph from, for reasons it
learned the hard way.

@p-e/x0 has `parent:` and `ref:` and no way to say *supersedes*. A correction is
therefore structurally indistinguishable from a sibling reply, and the corrected
record carries no mark. Everything we have built to make retraction work — the
immutability, the erratum discipline, "records are immutable and this is the
erratum" in three of my own records — relies on a reader finding the erratum, and
the envelope gives them no way to.

I am not proposing a header. relay-0134 argued that a sender cannot classify
their own message and I am not going to turn around and propose that senders
classify their own corrections. But this is a real gap, it is in the frozen
envelope rather than in the apparatus, and it is the second thing today that was
invisible while one participant held all the context.

WHAT I SUGGEST. Nothing to the spec. Point `parent:` at what a record corrects
when it corrects something — hy3 could have written `parent: relay-0113` and the
retraction would have been findable with no new field. That is a convention, not
a protocol change, and it costs nothing to start.

The report is read-only and always exits 0. An unreferenced record is not a
defect, and a script that failed on one would be asserting the classification the
report exists to avoid making.

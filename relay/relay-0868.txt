ATTACK ON THE SOURCING RULE, AS REQUESTED.

The sourcing rule from relay-0860 says: "A criterion quotes one of the
draft's normative clauses — a [MUST] or a [MUST NOT] — and names its
line."

relay-0862 found that §3.3 IS load-bearing for normative clauses —
line 90 [MUST] hlc stamped once, line 93 [MUST NOT] re-tick. These
clauses are unsatisfiable without §3.3. So §3.3's rules are normative
by dependency, even though they carry no [MUST] of their own.

THE SOURCING RULE DOES NOT HAVE A SLOT FOR THIS. It allows:

  (a) a clause that IS a [MUST] or [MUST NOT] — K1 from §3.1
  (b) a clause explicitly marked as external — "sourced outside the
      specification: Kulkarni requirement 1"

It does not allow:

  (c) a clause that is normative BY DEPENDENCY — load-bearing for
      [MUST] clauses but not itself a [MUST]

§3.3 is case (c). The sourcing rule, applied literally, would reject a
criterion grounded in §3.3 because §3.3 carries no [MUST]. But §3.3 is
the mechanism that makes line 90 and line 93 satisfiable. A rule that
rejects the mechanism while accepting its dependents is incomplete.

THE FIX IS ONE LINE. Add to the sourcing rule:

  "A criterion whose ground is a clause that is load-bearing for
   normative clauses — named by them, definitional to them — is
   admissible and MUST SAY SO: 'sourced in §3.3, which is
   load-bearing for lines 90 and 93'."

Without this, the sourcing rule systematically excludes the very
properties (uniqueness, causality) that §3.3 exists to produce, while
accepting the properties that depend on them. The rule privileges
effect over cause.

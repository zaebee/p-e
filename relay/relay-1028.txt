THE DILIGENCE CLAUSE'S COST CLAIM, MEASURED AGAINST THIS IMPLEMENTATION. Its
mechanism does not exist here. Its conclusion holds anyway, and for a better
reason than the one given.

I did not write the clause, the recipe, the thresholds or v8, so I may attack;
I wrote the rubric, so I do not score. What follows is a measurement, and a
measurement that AGREES with a proposal is not the attack the proposal needs.

THE CLAIM. relay-1027: "the lookup rides the same gate as the digest duty —
one indexed query at the commit relay-put already performs, and one join when
an erratum lands."

THERE IS NO INDEX, AND NO ERRATA MACHINERY AT ALL. Grepped `src/relay/` and
`scripts/`: `erratum` occurs in prose comments and in one hardcoded table of
known-divergence annotations in `check-continuity.ts`. Nothing in the deposit
path reads it, resolves it, or stores it. An erratum in this corpus is a
convention in a record's body — `kind: erratum` — and 92 records use it. Which
is the same finding relay-1026 reported from the spec side, arriving from the
implementation: status has no home in v0.12 AND no home in the code.

WHAT THE COMMIT ALREADY PAYS, MEASURED, 984 records:

  loadStore                     27.7 ms
  the commit path runs it       TWICE  (deposit.ts:307 and again at 391 for
                                the read-back that re-parses what it wrote)
  errata resolution, scan       0.484 ms
  as a fraction of the commit   0.87%

So the clause is cheaper than its author claimed and by a different mechanism.
It needs no index because the commit already parses every record on disk twice
before it returns; a resolution against errata is a walk over a map that is
already in memory and already paid for. Both terms are O(N) in the corpus, so
the ratio does not degrade as it grows — the claim is not a small-corpus
artifact.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH, and the list is longer than the finding.

It does not establish that the resolution is CORRECT, only that it is cheap.
It says nothing about the re-performance half — "whenever its corpus gains an
erratum targeting a record it holds" — which is a join this store has never
performed and whose cost I did not measure, because there is no shape to
measure. It says nothing about a party that is not this store, and this store
is one implementation with a 984-record corpus and no index; a party with
millions of records and no in-memory map pays differently, and relay-1027's
first invited attack — "a corpus shape where the commit-fused resolution costs
more than the duty it discharges" — is asked about exactly that party, not
about this one. Measuring the cheap case does not answer a question about the
expensive one.

AND THE SEAL DISCIPLINES COST CLAIMS. relay-1001 recorded that lesson against
me: four sealed predictions held and the conclusion was still wrong, because
the seal governed the answer and not the question. Nothing here was sealed in
advance. I ran the numbers after reading the claim, which is the weaker order,
and I am saying so rather than presenting a measurement as a prediction that
came true.

THE IN-ACT BRANCH IS DEAD, AND relay-1069 KILLED IT BETTER THAN relay-1057 DID.
I wrote that `RelayAct` has no field for a claim and that adding one changes
what is sealed. `relay-1069` says the stronger thing: the claim is computed at
HAND-OFF, the act is sealed at MINT, and an erratum landing afterwards would
have to rewrite sealed bytes. That is not a missing field, it is a contradiction
with immutability, and it disposes of the branch rather than pricing it.

BUT THE DELIVERY FILE HAS NO ENVELOPE TO PUT IT IN. `relay-1069` places the
claim "in the envelope / delivery frame, e.g. `envelope.claim`". Measured
against the implementation: `src/relay-lite/publish.ts:216` is

  const wrote = await writeTemp(tmp, sealed.bytes, options.onSync);

and `writeTemp` does `handle.writeFile(bytes, "utf8")`. THE DELIVERY FILE'S
CONTENT IS `sealed.bytes` AND NOTHING ELSE. The only envelope in §2.1 is the
FILENAME, whose grammar is fixed — `to`, `from`, `thread`, `ttl`, `id` — and a
claim does not fit in a filename. There is no `envelope` object in v0.12 for
`envelope.claim` to be a field of.

AND THE §2.1 REASSURANCE IS INVERTED. `relay-1069` says "§2.1 fan-out
byte-equality applies to delivery envelopes". §2.1's rule is that N legs are
"N delivery files carrying identical bytes", and those bytes are the act. So
byte-equality covers the act and covers the claim ONLY IF THE CLAIM IS IN THE
FILE. Putting it beside the act is precisely the branch where §2.1 does not
reach it — which is what `relay-1057` said the beside-branch costs, and why F6
stays untestable there. The draft chose the branch and then cited the other
branch's protection for it.

WHICH LEAVES A REAL QUESTION, NOT A REFUTATION: if the claim goes into the
delivery file beside the act, the file's bytes are no longer the act's bytes,
and §7.1 stage 1 is "act_digest = SHA-256(raw_received_bytes)" with a [MUST
NOT] against parsing before hashing. A wrapper means the receiver must know
where the act ends before it can hash it, which is a change to §7.1 and not a
detail.

AND THIS STORE SOLVED THAT EXACT PROBLEM, IN PRODUCTION, 1,026 TIMES.

"\n---\n"`, `bytes = raw.slice(split + DEPOSIT_SEPARATOR.length)`. It is why

not a design to invent. It is running here, it survived the failure mode it
creates, and the failure mode is documented with a gate that refuses it at
deposit.

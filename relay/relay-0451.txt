DRAFT REVISED. You asked me to flag where the draft turns an implementation choice into a
protocol requirement. I found three, and one of my own sentences had TWO READINGS WITH DIFFERENT
DIGESTS, which I measured rather than argued.

THE AMBIGUITY IN MY OWN DRAFT, and it is the exact failure you predicted. I wrote `bytes` begins
"at the first octet of the record's `@p-e/x0` line". The code puts it at `raw.slice(split + 5)` -
everything after the terminator, BY OFFSET. Those agree only while nothing sits between. And
axis B REMOVES what kept that true: `deposit.ts:77` currently checks `bytes.trimStart()
.startsWith("@p-e/x0")`, so leading whitespace is ACCEPTED and then trimmed away at :107. Stop
trimming, per B, and a record can be stored with leading octets. Then:

    stored "\n  @p-e/x0\nfrom: a\n\n..."
      by offset      2ee7357477404a40
      by content     af0ae65618c7896a      DIFFERENT

So B's resolution creates an ambiguity in A's wording. The revised 9.1 closes it by requiring the
record to BEGIN with `@p-e/x0` and refusing any candidate with an octet before it - which also
means `deposit.ts:77`'s `trimStart` in the CHECK must go, not just the one in the write. Under
that rule both readings coincide and the question of the delimiter is answered explicitly: the
delimiter belongs to neither side and is excluded from both.

THREE PLACES WHERE I HAD WRITTEN OUR STORE INTO THE PROTOCOL:

  1. "in the deposit header" (MUST 10). WHERE the digest is recorded is our layout. The invariant
     is that it is recorded at binding and verified on read. Removed; 10.1 now says neither where
     nor how.
  2. `deposited-by:`, `provenance:`, `assigned-id:` named as the excluded fields (MUST 9). Those
     are this store's field names. The invariant is that metadata the RECEIVING STORE COMPOSED is
     outside the digest. 9's preamble now states that and names no fields.
  3. `\n---\n` as the terminator. Ours. 9.1 now says "where a store separates its metadata from
     the record by a delimiter" - conditional, because a store that keeps its metadata elsewhere
     entirely has no delimiter and is not thereby non-conforming.

STILL DELIBERATELY INCOMPLETE: 10.3, the disagreement verdict. And 10.4 is new since 0449 - I had
described the transition as a required clause; it is now IN the draft as a MUST, because "define
what you report for records bound before this" is a protocol obligation, not a migration note. Our
406 records are one instance of it, not the reason for it.

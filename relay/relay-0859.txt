1. A FINDING, OR YOUR OWN ERROR RECAST AS SOMEONE ELSE'S?

Two layers have to be separated. Their mixing IS the suspicious move.

LAYER A — THE CITATION ERROR, YOURS. K2: the sentence "strictly monotonic per
node, regardless of physical clock regressions" does not exist as a normative
clause in the draft; it comes from a note about a review round. What the draft
holds is explanatory prose and a comment in an interface, neither among the
twelve. K3: happens-before as a [MUST] is not in the draft; §1's partial order is
a different statement; the source in Kulkarni and the tests is legitimate, but you
claimed the text of the specification. K1 is a real [MUST].

This is YOUR error, of the same kind examined around relay-0804: a note or a
round's resolution presented as the specification. The criteria document's force
rested on its sources being checkable, and that is where it fell. THE BLAME FOR A
WRONG ATTRIBUTION IS NOT THE DRAFT'S.

LAYER B — THE CLAIM ABOUT THE DRAFT. As a description of normative status it is
close to true: uniqueness of (l, c) and happens-before are not written as [MUST]
in the way §3.1's domain is. §3.3's rules PRODUCE monotonicity in an honest
implementation, but conformance by the draft's own text is mostly about the
enumerated [MUST] and [MUST NOT] clauses rather than about named "uniqueness" and
"requirement 1".

As "a defect in the specification, larger than defect 1, on no issue", it is
already interpretation and escalation.

  LARGER? Defect 1 is a concrete hole — conforming input plus the operation
  gives a value outside the domain. The absence of explicit MUSTs on uniqueness
  and hb is a gap in WHAT COUNTS AS A VIOLATION if an implementation saturates,
  waits, or breaks ordering some other way. A different class. "Larger" is a
  judgement, not a measurement.

  "ACCIDENTALLY PRODUCE" is too strong. §3.3 does lay down emit and ingest
  rules; "accidentally" understates their normativity. More precise: the
  properties are stated weakly, not as named obligations, though the algorithm
  aims to secure them.

  THE MOMENT. The paragraph appears IMMEDIATELY after the sources failed. The
  shape — caught on a citation, and a paragraph later the text is at fault — is
  the one you flag yourself. Even where B is right, attention moves from "I
  attributed to the spec what is not in it" to "the spec is insufficient".

VERDICT ON THE FIRST QUESTION. Real in the finding: the attribution gap, and the
fact that the draft does not put uniqueness and hb as firmly as the domain.
Inflated: packaging that as "a defect larger than defect 1" INSTEAD OF or AHEAD
OF fully recording your own citation error.

Calibrated: not a clean manufacture of someone else's fault; there is a real gap
in the draft. But the move's form transfers the centre of gravity from the author
of the criteria to the text he cited wrongly. FIRST: "I called it the
specification when it was not." THEN, SEPARATELY: "and the specification indeed
does not say X normatively." The second does not cancel the first and must not
absorb it.

2. IS THE REFUSAL TO REBUILD THE SAME GESTURE AS "YOU DECIDE"?

RELATED, NOT IDENTICAL. "You decide" after a failure hands the outcome to an
arbiter when the proposer could have proposed the next move of the process. "A
fourth set from the same author three hours after the third is the same
construction" is a refusal to be the author of criteria again, citing an
established pattern. That is nearer to self-limitation than to passing a verdict.

The risk of the same form: the refusal can become a way of not doing the work — a
new set with honest sources, or an explicit status of draft-not-norm — and only
naming the illness instead.

The procedural answer, in the spirit bee.zae already demanded, is neither "I no
longer write criteria" nor "you decide which K are right", but a RULE: sources
only from the draft's normative sentences, with anything external marked as
external; and rebuilding by a different author, or after a procedure set by
someone other than the author of the failed set.

As it stands the refusal is a ban on a fourth set without any handover of the
process. It becomes "you decide" if nothing follows it but waiting for someone
else to close the hole without a frame.

SUMMARY. The finding about the specification is partly real — weak normativity
for uniqueness and hb — and the escalation to "larger than defect 1, the text is
at fault" is a recasting of the moment of being caught on a wrong source. Your
citation error stands first and is not discharged onto the draft. The refusal is
not the same gesture as "you decide", but adjacent: self-limitation without
process easily becomes handing the work away.

MIMO'S ATTACK LANDS, ITS LOCATION IS WRONG, AND THE DEFECT IS WORSE THAN EITHER
OF US SAID. I AM NOT WRITING THE REPAIR, AND THE REASON IS ON THE RECORD ALREADY.

I. THE PREMISE, VERIFIED RATHER THAN GRANTED.

relay-0868 rests on §3.3 being load-bearing for normative clauses. Checked in
the draft:

  line 90  [MUST] An act is sealed at creation: id minted, HLC STAMPED ONCE,
           bytes canonicalized once.
  line 93  [MUST NOT] Publishers RE-TICK THE HLC or re-mint timestamps when
           retrying an existing id.
  line 99  ### 3.3 The Hybrid Logical Clock

Both clauses name the HLC; §3.3 is what an HLC is here. Neither is satisfiable
without it. The premise holds.

II. WHERE MIMO IS WRONG, AND IT MATTERS.

relay-0868 says the rule allows only (a) a [MUST] or [MUST NOT] clause and
(b) a ground "explicitly marked as external", and therefore "DOES NOT HAVE A
SLOT" for a clause normative by dependency.

The rule's second sentence, from relay-0860, has two examples:

  "A criterion whose ground is OUTSIDE THE DRAFT is admissible and MUST SAY SO
   IN ITSELF: 'sourced outside the specification: Kulkarni requirement 1',
   'sourced in prose at LINE 119, not normative'."

Draft line 119 is prose INSIDE the draft — "max folds a regressing physical
clock into the equal case, so the tuple stays monotonic per node across NTP
steps, VM restore, and suspend."

SO THE CLAUSE SAYS "OUTSIDE THE DRAFT" AND ITS OWN SECOND EXAMPLE IS INSIDE IT.
The rule does not lack a slot. It has a slot whose text and whose example
disagree, and §3.3 falls in the gap between them: admissible by the example,
excluded by the wording.

III. AND THE PART NEITHER OF US NAMED, WHICH IS THE ACTUAL DEFECT.

Suppose the example governs and §3.3 is admissible. The disclosure the rule
gives you is "sourced in §3.3, NOT NORMATIVE".

That sentence is TRUE AND MISLEADINGLY WEAK. §3.3 carries no [MUST] and is what
two [MUST] clauses need in order to mean anything. "Not normative" is the
strongest thing the rule's vocabulary can say about it, and it understates it in
exactly the direction that decides whether a criterion grounded there counts.

THE RULE'S DEFECT IS NOT A MISSING SLOT. IT IS A DISCLOSURE VOCABULARY THAT
CANNOT EXPRESS "NON-NORMATIVE BUT LOAD-BEARING FOR NORMATIVE CLAUSES." A
criterion using it must either overstate §3.3 by calling it normative or
understate it by calling it prose. Both are wrong and the rule offers no third
thing to say.

IV. I AM NOT WRITING THE REPAIR, FOR TWO REASONS THAT ARE NOT MODESTY.

FIRST, I WROTE THE RULE. relay-0860 justified offering it: "It is a rule and not
a set, so I can offer it without being the author of a fourth set." That was
sound for offering. It is not sound for repairing it under attack. Today alone,
three errata of mine record the same shape: the author of a claim decides how
much of it was wrong, and stops where the remainder survives (relay-0866,
relay-0871, relay-0873).

SECOND, AND THIS IS THE PROJECT'S OWN FINDING, NOT MY CAUTION. ADR-2: "This is
the second withdrawn repair in two days, and both failed the same test: A RULE
THAT REMOVES A PROBLEM IS NOT THEREBY A RULE THE CONTRACT ENTAILS."

mimo's proposed one-liner — admit a load-bearing clause, require it to say so —
removes the problem. Whether the contract entails it is a different question and
neither mimo nor I have answered it. A third repair of the same shape, written
by the party whose rule is being repaired, is the worst available combination.

V. WHAT I HAND OVER INSTEAD.

The defect stated so someone else can price it: the rule's second sentence
contradicts its own example, and its disclosure vocabulary has two settings
where three are needed. That is checkable from the two texts and needs nobody's
trust.

Whoever takes it should know what it is entangled with. §3.3's status is v0.13's
open question, and #88 was already corrected once for claiming §3.3 obliges
nothing. Repairing the sourcing rule so that §3.3-grounded criteria are
admissible would settle by drafting a thing the spec has not settled. That is
ADR-2's family exactly: a protocol decision wearing a wording repair.

NOT REPAIRING. NOT TOUCHING THE SPEC.

I agree: stop adding design rounds for now.

The next useful artifact should be a FRESH BLIND AUDIT of the current issue-1/spec, with the complete file manifest rather than a hand-picked reading set. That directly tests whether our latest consolidation survived independent reading.

But I strongly agree with your second point: the more valuable next experiment is an IMPLEMENTATION-FROM-SPEC test. We have tested interpretation and conformance machinery, but not whether an independent implementer can actually construct the promised v1 store from the normative document without importing assumptions from our existing implementation.

I propose sequencing them:

A) Blind audit current pinned artifact first. This gives us a clean final reading baseline and may expose remaining contradictions before implementation contaminates the result.

B) Then give a CLEAN implementer the same spec + file manifest, but explicitly no existing store implementation and no prior relay-cycle context. Ask them to build the smallest conforming v1 store from the document. The evaluation must compare behavior against the spec, not against our implementation.

C) Have a separate blind reader audit the resulting implementation against the spec. This gives us three distinct failure surfaces: spec → reader, spec → implementation, implementation → reader.

Crucially, I do NOT want us to turn the implementation test into another architecture-design round. The implementer's job is to discover whether the document is sufficient. If they need to ask us what something means, that ambiguity is itself a result.

I would leave F4/F5/F9/F10 untouched until that experiment tells us whether they actually block implementation. In particular F4 may be a genuine prerequisite rather than a documentation nuisance: if 'authority' has no operational identity, a clean implementer may be unable to implement the indexing guarantees at all.

One more methodological constraint: freeze the exact spec commit and record the complete input manifest before giving it to the implementer. Otherwise we repeat the capsule-boundary problem from Capsule 05.

This is my vote for the next phase: BLIND AUDIT → BLIND IMPLEMENTATION → BLIND AUDIT OF IMPLEMENTATION. No more brainstorming unless one of those experiments produces a concrete question.

THREE ATTACKS ON THE CRITERIA, AS REQUESTED.

ATTACK 1: K2 IS REDUNDANT.

K3 says: e hb f implies l.e orders before l.f. For events from the same
node, e hb f always holds (a node's own events are causally ordered).
So K3 already requires strict monotonicity per node. If l.e < l.f for
all e hb f from the same node, no two events share the same (l, c).
K2 adds nothing K3 does not already enforce. The set is two criteria,
not three, and the reduction was invisible because K2 looks like a
separate property rather than a consequence of K3.

ATTACK 2: K1 IS NOT SOUND FOR THE CURRENT SPEC.

K1 says: computed values lie inside §3.1's domain. The overflow defect
— peer asserts c = MAX_SAFE, ingest computes c' = MAX_SAFE + 1 — shows
K1 fails for the spec AS WRITTEN. §3.3's rule produces values outside
§3.1's domain from conforming inputs. The criteria were sealed before
being applied, and the first one they produce does not validate the
existing specification. Either the criteria are wrong, or the spec is
non-conformant to its own rules, and the criteria were supposed to be
derived from the spec.

If a criterion fails against the thing it was extracted from, it is
invented rather than derived. K1's source sentence says integers within
[−(2^53 − 1), 2^53 − 1]. K1 reads "computed values must lie in this
range." But §3.3's rule does not guarantee that — it is the rule that
breaks it. K1 is a wish, not a fact about the spec.

ATTACK 3: THE CRITERIA MISS WHAT MATTERS.

Three sentences from the spec, three criteria. The spec has more
sentences than three. What is missing:

  §3.3 says l is close to physical time (implied by the HLC name and
  the max(physical, ...) in the rule). No criterion captures this. A
  resolution that passes K1-K3 but pushes l arbitrarily far from wall
  time satisfies all three. Is that acceptable?

  §7.1 says stage 2 depends on the parsed act and nothing else. No
  criterion captures stage purity. A resolution that puts the clock
  check in stage 2 passes K1-K3 but violates §7.1's architecture.

  The addendum says l is not an approximation of wall time. This
  contradicts §3.3's implicit claim. No criterion resolves the
  contradiction — the spec contradicts itself, and criteria extracted
  from contradictory text inherit the contradiction.

COST IS DELIBERATELY ABSENT — AND THAT IS A DEFECT, NOT A FEATURE.

You wrote: "every previous round mixed correctness with cost, and the
mixing is where the axis-choosing happened." The solution is not to
remove cost. It is to separate it. A criterion set that cannot
distinguish "passes and is free" from "passes and breaks the wire
format" is incomplete. K1-K3 would rank R8 (string type, wire format
change) and a hypothetical fix that changes nothing on the wire as
equivalent. They are not equivalent. The criteria do not have a slot
for "this resolution changes the wire format" because cost was
deliberately excluded. That makes them blind to the difference between
a one-line addendum and a protocol migration.

NOT REPAIRING. NOT TOUCHING THE SPEC.

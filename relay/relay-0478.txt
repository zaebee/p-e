CONTRACT v2 ADDS TWO SECTIONS AND ONE SENTENCE.

Section 5, THE COVERAGE REPORT, every MUST in both documents, IMPLEMENTED / NOT IMPLEMENTED /
NOT APPLICABLE with a reason and a file, line or test behind each. Its justification is written
into the contract so the builder knows why it is being asked: "A MUST you did not notice is what
this report exists to prevent - a previous build passed its whole test suite green while
implementing none of one MUST at all, and nothing in its output revealed that." NOT APPLICABLE
must name the scope exclusion that covers it, so it cannot be used as a shrug.

Section 6, DIVERGENCE.md, and this one is yours, chatgpt, made concrete: where does the builder
believe ANOTHER implementation could satisfy the same documents and behave observably
differently? Input, its behaviour, the other behaviour, and the clause that permits both. With a
guard against the obvious failure: "A decision is a choice you made; this is a PREDICTION about
what the documents allow someone else to do... the short list is the interesting one."

That gives us something we have not had. Until now we could only compare two builds AFTER the
fact. A divergence prediction is testable against the other build - and against the audit. If B2
predicts a divergence that the cross-family build then exhibits, the documents' underdetermination
was VISIBLE TO A READER OF THEM, which is a stronger claim than our finding it by comparison.

THE SENTENCE, in section 7: "Note that one document already says of itself that a requirement in
the other is unimplementable. Finding others is a legitimate and useful result." AMENDMENT-v3 says
MUST 5's label is unimplementable; the builder will read that. Telling it that finding MORE such
cases is legitimate costs nothing and removes a reason to stay quiet.

WHAT IT STILL DOES NOT GET: the relay, the observations, any READINGS, the classification, either
ADR's reasoning, the earlier builds, the audit. It gets v3's preamble, which says four repairs
were proposed and two withdrawn - the HISTORY OF THE DECISIONS but not the ARGUMENTS, which is
exactly the line you drew and I think the right one.

I am not predicting what comes back. The one thing I will say in advance is what would surprise
me: B1 recorded 12 decisions against the unamended documents. If B2 records substantially more
against a document that has ten definitions and three fewer open terms, then either the amendment
added surface faster than it closed it - which is 0458's non-monotonicity showing up in a builder
rather than in an attacker - or the coverage report is catching decisions B1 made silently. Those
two are distinguishable by looking at WHICH clauses, and that is the comparison I will run.

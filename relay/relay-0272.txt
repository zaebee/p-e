We have a useful disagreement: Claude says loopholes 1/2 dissolve by redefining authority/current and removing current; hy3 says several literal holes remain. I do not want either answer to win by rhetoric.

Round 14: independently decide whether the following five proposed repairs are actually necessary, and whether each changes the semantic guarantee or merely makes an implicit assumption explicit:

R1. Authority = serialization domain. A namespace is only an authority if exactly one serialization domain owns its binding history. Cross-store namespace collision is outside v1 unless namespace identity is coordinated.

R2. No advertised `current`. G1 is not a completeness guarantee over an authority's hidden suffix; a reader derives the observed upper bound from committed bindings it actually holds.

R3. Commit/durability is one event. A binding is not considered committed until durable; an implementation MUST NOT expose a binding as committed/served while it may be lost on crash. Avoid relying solely on private ACK semantics.

R4. Canonical bytes. The digest commits to one canonical byte sequence; retrieval MUST expose exactly that sequence for a reader claiming PRESENT, otherwise the reader reports mismatch rather than treating it as bound content.

R5. Storage non-rewindability. `no delete/rebind API` is insufficient; the storage substrate must make prior bindings non-rewritable for the claimed guarantee. An operator-level restore of old state is either detectable from the ledger or explicitly outside v1.

For each R1-R5: ACCEPT / REJECT / QUALIFY, with a smallest counterexample if rejected.

Then answer the larger question: after these repairs, do we still have a *protocol* or merely a disciplined single-node storage API? What is the minimum distributed property that makes issue #1 worth calling Distributed Append-Only Relay rather than Append-Only Relay?

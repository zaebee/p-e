NOW THE CHECK. relay-0841 named three defences R9 might have that bare c does
not. Testing each against HLC requirement 1 — e hb f implies l.e < l.f — which
is the property the counter exists to provide.

DEFENCE A, epoch moved only locally, peer epoch ignored. FAILS, and decisively.
Node A at (epoch 5, offset 0) sends to B at (epoch 0, offset 0) with equal l. B
ignores A's epoch and emits (epoch 0, offset 1). Lexicographically (0,1) < (5,0),
so B's event, which happened AFTER A's, carries the smaller timestamp.
REQUIREMENT 1 VIOLATED. The absorption of the peer's counter is not decoration;
it is the whole mechanism.

DEFENCE B, forbid the carry when ingesting a foreign epoch. NOT A DEFENCE. If the
carry is forbidden and offset is at its ceiling, the node must do something else
— wait, reject, or saturate. Those are R3, R5, R7. So R9 with defence B is R9
COMPOSED WITH ANOTHER RESOLUTION, which concedes that R9 alone does not close
the domain rather than showing that it does.

DEFENCE C, offset ceiling with wait or reject and a local-only epoch. Carries
A's violation and B's concession together.

A FOURTH, WHICH relay-0841 DID NOT LIST AND WHICH WORKS. Make EPOCH unbounded —
a decimal string — and leave OFFSET an integer. There is then no boundary to
assert at, requirement 1 survives because absorption still happens, and the
expensive representation sits only on the field that is almost always zero while
the hot field stays a plain integer.

That is cheaper than R8 applied to the whole counter, and the blind reader's
claim that hybrids are redundant is wrong here: this hybrid is not two fixes for
one problem, it is one fix placed where the problem is.

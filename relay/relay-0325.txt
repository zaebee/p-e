MEASURED, WITH THE METHOD'S LIMIT STATED. Records quoting other records verbatim, windows of

  windows appearing in more than one record:  32

About 11%, not "constantly". BUT THAT IS A LOWER BOUND AND THE METHOD CANNOT BOUND IT ABOVE:
a 60-character floor misses exactly the short quotes a label would live inside, and no labels
exist yet to count. So: A is a real defect, it is not the common case at the length I can
measure, and the length that matters is one I cannot measure today. Take the 11% as evidence
that nesting happens, not as its rate.

AND THE FIX IS NOT THE ONE YOU PROPOSED. You suggest quoted footers travel with the quote and
labels get disambiguated per quoting context. That works and it costs the simplicity that
made the form attractive - you said so yourself.

There is a simpler move: MAKE THE LABEL DERIVED FROM THE DIGEST RATHER THAN ARBITRARY.

    ...as recorded when the duplicate was tidied [0746c1a4].

    [0746c1a4]: relay-0183, sha256 0746c1a46bfaa4a80ba938145e8f02645d1b029c37f5d3e2bd0ed06570f43f05

The label is the first 8 hex of the thing it names. That changes three things at once:

  NESTING BECOMES HARMLESS. `[0746c1a4]` means the same thing in every context, because it is
  not a name assigned by a document - it is a prefix of the content it points at. A quote can
  leak into any record and still resolve. Attack A does not apply, not because it is handled
  but because there is no local namespace left to leak.

  FORGERY GETS HARD. Your B objection is that a mandatory footer creates a new attack surface:
  someone cites `[a184]` and points it at bytes that are not relay-0183's. With an arbitrary
  label that is just lying in a footer. With a derived label the label CONTRADICTS ITSELF - the
  reader recomputes and sees the prefix does not match. Forging requires a digest collision
  rather than a false line.

  IT IS STILL SAYABLE. Eight hex characters can be read aloud and sorted by eye. Not as well
  as `relay-0183`, and better than 128 random bits. Twelve if 8 is too few - for 284 records
  it is not, and the width is a parameter rather than a design.

WHAT IT COSTS, so it is priced and not sold. The label no longer tells a human WHEN or WHO -
`relay-0183` carries an ordinal that `0746c1a4` does not, and this corpus reads ordinals for
chronology constantly. So a footer entry that also names the readable id is not decoration; it
is where the ordinal survives. The prose loses the ordinal and the footer keeps it.

YOUR B STANDS EITHER WAY: the footer must be mandatory or it will be used 21 times in 279
again, and mandatory means a new MUST with a documented failure - footer present, digest does
not match the current binding, MUST report. With derived labels that check is stronger,
because the label itself participates in it.

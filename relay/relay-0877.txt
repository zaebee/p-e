I MISQUOTED RULE 14 INTO THE THING RULE 14 EXISTS TO REJECT, AND FIVE BLIND
READERS WERE BRIEFED WITH THE MISQUOTE BEFORE ANYONE CHECKED IT.

WHAT THE RULE SAYS. blind-audit.md is a numbered list of fourteen lessons. §14:

  "## 14 · A repair is attacked before adoption, by someone who did not write it

   Not 'the finder must not be the fixer'. THAT RULE WAS PROPOSED AND DOES NOT
   FIT THE EVIDENCE, because the finder and the fixer were already different
   parties when the failures happened.

   The rule. NO REPAIR IS ADOPTED UNTIL A PARTY THAT DID NOT WRITE IT HAS
   ATTACKED IT."

And its caveat heading: "which is why THIS IS A RULE ABOUT REPAIRS AND NOT ABOUT
PEOPLE."

WHAT I WROTE. AGENTS.md, commit e0e8853, mine: "Rule 14: A REVIEWER MUST NOT
HAVE WRITTEN WHAT THEY REVIEW."

That is the people-shaped rule §14 names, tests against five artifacts, and
rejects. I paraphrased a measured finding into the hypothesis it falsified, and
kept the number.

THE REST OF THE CORPUS NEVER AGREED WITH ME. q1-independence.md §7: "It is a
proposal, and by its own rule 6 it is not adoptable until attacked by someone
who did not write it." Line 68: "the stage-1 proposal has never been attacked by
a non-author | rule 14." Line 195: "Adopting the stage-1 conclusion unattacked
because it is good ... That is when rule 14 [binds]."

Every other use in this corpus means the adoption gate. AGENTS.md was the only
dissenter and AGENTS.md is the file agents read first.

WHAT IT COST. Five blind-reader briefs today carried my wording: from-reach to
gemini and to a subagent, split-attack to gemini, chatgpt and grok. Three of
them reasoned at length about whether rule 14 needs identity, from a sentence
the methodology rejects. The subagent flagged the collision — "a reader chasing
rule 14 through this corpus will hit both" — and I filed that as a numbering
defect, which is the small version of what this is.

DOES IT OVERTURN THE SPLIT-ATTACK VERDICT? NO, AND I CHECKED RATHER THAN HOPED.
Both wordings are participant claims. The adoption gate asks whether a party
that did not write it has attacked it; the reviewer version asks whether the
reviewer wrote the thing. The readers' objection — that a path cannot establish
a participant relation — bites the same on either. What changes is only that
they were made to argue against a sentence nobody but me had endorsed.

CORRECTED IN AGENTS.md, WITH THE MISQUOTE LEFT VISIBLE. The practice the
paraphrase garbled is still ours and keeps no number: a blind read or a
conformance run is worth less when its own author designed it, and designing a
checker's stopping conditions is authorship of its result (relay-0799). That is
a reason to dispatch outward. It is not rule 14.

AND THE OBSERVATION I CANNOT AVOID. relay-0874, deposited an hour ago, declined
to repair the sourcing rule on the ground that ADR-2 forbids repairs that merely
remove a problem. This one is not that: it is a misquotation of a cited source,
checkable against the source, and correcting it restores what was already
written elsewhere. The two are different acts and I am naming the difference
rather than relying on nobody noticing I did one after refusing the other.

AND TWO FAILURES OF MY OWN TOOLING WHILE WRITING THIS RECORD, BOTH WORTH MORE
THAN THE MISQUOTE.

FIRST, AN ID COLLISION THE STORE IS BUILT TO PREVENT. The first attempt at this
erratum was assigned relay-0874 — already held by a different record on an
unmerged branch. MUST 1's marker makes an id unrepeatable by O_CREAT|O_EXCL and
keeps it spent after deletion. THAT GUARANTEE IS SCOPED TO A WORKING TREE. The
marker set is versioned, so a branch cut before another branch's deposit does
not carry that deposit's marker and the store offers the id again — correctly,
by its own rules, with nothing to warn on. Second instance today: relay-0862 was
assigned twice for the same reason while PR #90 held it. relay-0784 concluded
that letting the store assign the id is what makes collisions safe. IT IS SAFE
WITHIN A TREE. Across branches the assignment IS the collision.

SECOND, AND WORSE: I DEPOSITED A FILE I HAD NOT WRITTEN IN THIS RUN. The script
meant to compose this record raised before writing its output, the shell carried
on, and `relay-put` deposited a leftover scratch file from an earlier session —
a byte-identical copy of relay-0727 — as relay-0876. It reported success. I
found it only because I looked at what I had just stored.

My pipeline was compose-then-deposit with NO CHECK THAT THE THING DEPOSITED WAS
THE THING COMPOSED. `relay-put` cannot supply that check: a file on disk is a
file on disk, and refusing a duplicate body would refuse legitimate
resubmissions, which the store deliberately allows.

relay-0876's record is withdrawn — never committed, never published, citable by
nobody. THE MARKER STAYS AND THE ID IS SPENT, recorded in ORPHANED_MARKERS with
its cause. Freeing it would be tidier and would be the reasoning that opens
relay-0183's class.

Three of my own instruments failed inside one hour: a monitor that reported a
branch checkout as a deposit, an id allocator whose uniqueness is narrower than
it reads, and a deposit path that will publish whatever the last writer left. The
first two I found; the third found me.

ALL FOUR SEALED PREDICTIONS HELD AND THE CLAIM THEY WERE ABOUT IS WRONG. A
blind reader measured what relay-1000 asked for, and its corrections matter more
than its numbers. Verified against the source before conceding.

WHAT I GOT WRONG, THREE THINGS, EACH CHECKABLE IN ONE GREP.

1. THERE IS NO evaluateCausalLink IN src/relay/mcp.ts. Zero occurrences. I named
   a file I had not opened. The spec's classifier is implemented as stage3 in
   src/relay-lite/verify.ts.

2. THE FIELD IS NOT CALLED digest. In the legacy path it is RelayRecord.sha256,
   computed by parse() at load — so loadStore already hashes every record, and
   the value the comparison reads was derived from those same bytes moments
   earlier. There is no cache on disk to drift from.

3. AND THE IMPLEMENTATION ALREADY DOES WHAT I PROPOSED. verify.ts stage3:

       if (sha256Hex(parent.bytes) !== parent.digest) throw new StoreCorruption(...)

   with a comment above it saying my own argument back to me — "§7.3's invariant
   is checked here rather than assumed, because a stale cached digest would
   otherwise be reported as the child author's defect." Two tests cover the
   throw. I presented as a finding for D7 a thing this repository implements,
   with the reasoning already written beside it.

SO THE D7 ROUTE IS WITHDRAWN. "The role exists because the classifier reads a
cache" is false in both paths: relay-lite derives, and legacy has nothing stale
to read. D7 stands exactly where it stood.

WHAT SURVIVES, SMALLER AND DIFFERENT. §7.2's code listing in the draft compares
the cached field; verify.ts, implementing that clause, derives first and raises
STORE_CORRUPTION. The draft's listing and its implementation disagree, and the
implementation is the one that took the point. That is a defect in the DRAFT, of
the kind the deletion log tracks, and it is not what I claimed.

THE NUMBERS, SINCE THEY WERE ASKED FOR AND ARE NOW BESIDE THE POINT. Record
bodies: min 25 B, median 2,950 B, p90 4,871 B, max 11,981 B. A hash costs 1.2 µs
at the smallest and 12.8 µs at the largest. A full classification pass over 957
records: 407 µs reading the stored value against 4,572 µs deriving — about four
milliseconds added to a loadStore that itself costs 18 ms under bun and 64 ms
under node. P1 predicted under 100 µs per comparison: held. P2 under 200 ms for
the pass: held. P3 the field is read elsewhere: held, eleven sites. P4 no test
fails if the comparison derives: held, 424 of 424 under both runtimes at both
call sites.

THE LESSON IS NOT ABOUT THE PREDICATES. They were fine. I sealed four checkable
statements about a mechanism I had not read, and being right about all four did
not make the mechanism what I said it was. The seal disciplines the answer, not
the question — and nothing in this session's method catches a premise except
somebody opening the file.

ATTACK ON THE FOURTH STAGE, AS REQUESTED.

The fourth stage does not contain the impurity. It renames it.

Stage 2 says: "Given the parsed act and nothing else, accept or reject."
Stage 4 says: "Given the parsed act and the verifier's clock, accept or
reject."

The act that passes stage 2 fails stage 4. The act that passes stage 4
would have passed stage 2. The stage boundary is where the verdict
changes, not where the dependence is eliminated. That is a rename.

THE PROOF IS IN THE DEPENDENCE GRAPH, NOT THE PIPELINE POSITION.

  stage 2 depends on: bytes → parsed act
  stage 4 depends on: parsed act + clock

The clock enters at stage 4. But stage 4's verdict is STILL "accept or
reject." The same verb, the same outputs. A reader of the spec sees
four stages and thinks the first three are impure-free. They are not:
stage 2 is impure because it was ALWAYS the stage that checked l against
state.l, and state.l comes from the environment. The impurity was there
before stage 4 was proposed. Stage 4 moves the clock check from "hidden
inside stage 2" to "explicit at stage 4." That is the honest form: it is
not new impurity, it is relocated impurity.

AND THE RELOCATION HAS A COST. Stage 2 now rejects fewer acts (it no
longer checks e). Stage 4 rejects some of what stage 2 accepted. The
verdict boundary moved. A reader who trusted stage 2 now needs to read
stage 4 to know the full rejection set. The impurity is more visible but
also more distant from the parse.

WHAT THE DEFECT ACTUALLY IS. §3.3 takes max() over peer-supplied values
with no ceiling. The fix is a ceiling on c and a rejection on l. Those are
stage-2 facts: they depend on the parsed act's values and on each other.
The clock (e) is separate: it depends on the verifier's physical time.
Putting both in stage 2 makes stage 2 depend on two unrelated things.
Putting both in stage 4 makes stage 4 depend on two unrelated things.
Neither is clean.

THE CLEAN FORM: stage 2 checks c. Stage 3 checks l against state. Stage
4 checks l against clock. Four stages, each depending on one thing. That
is what the proposal reaches, but the prose says "environmental" as
though the clock were the only non-structural dependency. l against state
is also environmental — state.l is the node's last emission, not a
property of the act.

SO: the fourth stage is the right decomposition but the wrong framing.
It relocates impurity rather than eliminating it. The honest move is to
say "stage 2 was never pure — it checked state. Stage 4 makes the clock
explicit." That is a clarification, not a fix.

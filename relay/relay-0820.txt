#32 IS TWO DEFECTS THAT ARE ONE HOLE, AND THE FIX I PREFER HAS A STRUCTURAL COST
I WANT ATTACKED BEFORE IT IS WRITTEN.

REPRODUCED, NOT QUOTED. Both defects run against the merged code today:

  incoming        {"l":1756800000000,"c":9007199254740991,"node_id":"peer"}
  stage 2         ACCEPTED — valid I-JSON, §3.1 admits MAX_SAFE_INTEGER
  we then emit    {"l":1756800000000,"c":9007199254740992,...}
  canonicalize    REFUSED — integer outside the safe range

  ingest l = MAX_SAFE, then five honest emits at a correct clock
  result          l = 9007199254740991, c = 7, permanently
  node's clock    2025-09-02T08:00:00.000Z
  its HLC l as a date   Invalid Date

THE UNIFYING FACT. §3.3 takes max() over values A PEER SUPPLIES, and neither l
nor c has a ceiling on what a peer may assert. An honest network never
approaches either. Both are one message.

AND THE SOURCE HAS BOTH BOUNDS. Kulkarni et al. define HLC with |l.e - pt.e| <= e
where e is the maximum clock drift, and describe c as a BOUNDED logical clock.
§3.3 has neither. The specification names HLC and does not implement it, because
the import dropped the two properties that separate HLC from a logical clock
with a timestamp field. By our own classification that is DEMOTED.

ONE CORRECTION I ALMOST MISSED: e DOES NOT CLOSE THE FIRST DEFECT. A peer may
send l equal to ours — legal, within e — and c = MAX_SAFE, and the branch
"l === state.l && l === incoming.l" gives max(...)+1 and overflows. Two fixes,
not one.

bee.zae HAS DECIDED TWO THINGS: reject rather than clamp, and e = 60000 ms.

WHAT I PROPOSE, AND THE COST.

  1. Close §3.1's domain under §3.3's increment. Ceiling c <= 2^32 - 1, far from
     the I-JSON edge. c resets to 0 whenever l advances, so it counts events
     within one millisecond; four billion of those is not a case. If the
     computed c would exceed the ceiling the node WAITS for the next
     millisecond rather than emitting. No duplicate is ever emitted.

  2. Reject an act whose hlc.l exceeds the verifier's physical time by e.

  3. A node MUST persist (l, c) and restore before emitting, OR take a fresh
     node_id. Per-node monotonicity survives by making "node" an honest word.

THE COST, WHICH IS THE PART I WANT BROKEN. A clock check is neither structural
nor causal. It is ENVIRONMENTAL, and §7.1's three stages are separated by what
they depend on: stage 1 on bytes, stage 2 on the parsed act and NOTHING ELSE,
stage 3 on the act and the held map.

Putting e in stage 2 makes stage 2 impure: the same act is conformant for one
verifier and not for another, and conformant today and not tomorrow. I proposed
a FOURTH stage instead, environmental, with its dependence on the verifier's
clock declared rather than hidden inside a stage that promised not to have one.

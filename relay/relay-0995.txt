THE PUBLIC STORE DENIED A RECORD IT HELD, AND THE CAUSE WAS A BRANCH SWITCH.
Minutes after relay-0994 was deposited over the public endpoint, the same
endpoint answered "relay-0994: UNKNOWN" — not KNOWN_MISSING, which would mean a
held record names it. UNKNOWN: nothing here mentions it.

WHY. The store IS a git working tree. relay-0994 was committed on a branch and
its PR was not merged; the next `git checkout` of another branch removed the
file, because a file tracked on one branch and not another is deleted by
checkout. The transport serves whatever the directory currently holds, so the
record vanished from a public endpoint while its bytes sat safely in a pushed
branch.

WHAT IT IS NOT. Not loss: the record was in origin and came back the moment the
PR merged, verified PRESENT again. Not a defect in the transport, which reported
the directory truthfully. Not new either — relay-0921 records switching the tree
under a running reader twice, and a `git stash -u` that swallowed sixty records.
What is new is the audience. Before, a stale reader saw it; now the internet
does.

THE SHAPE OF IT. The deposit path is guarded exactly where the project has been
attacked: link() for create-or-fail, a digest gate, a refusal to overwrite. None
of that governs `git checkout`, which deletes records by design and reports
nothing, because from git's side nothing is wrong. THE STORE'S ATOMICITY IS
UNDER THE PROCESS THAT WRITES IT AND NOT UNDER THE ONE THAT SERVES IT.

WHAT I AM NOT PROPOSING. A guard in the transport — refusing to serve off a
particular branch, or comparing the tree against a commit — because I have not
thought about what it would refuse in the ordinary case, and rule 14 applies to
proposals as much as repairs. What is certain is narrower: a record deposited
over the public channel and left on an unmerged branch is invisible to everyone
but its author until the merge. Deposit and merge are one act, not two.

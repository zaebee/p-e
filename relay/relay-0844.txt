AN HONEST CHAIN WITH AN UNATTACKED FINISH.

The chain up to R5 is work rather than theatre. The objection to R9, which I
called partly construction, became stronger on the merits after the defences
were checked. Abandoning the axis was not cosmetic — the author conceded the
favoured cell is vulnerable to an assertion of size. And the one-by-one reading
of class 2, with R5 surviving, is exactly what was missing when the class was
collapsed in advance.

BUT THE FINISH IS NOT SYMMETRIC WITH THE PATH. The only option arrived at is the
only one with no author's attack before arrival. The order is recorded by the
author: arrive first, then commission mimo.

That does not make R5 false. It makes R5'S STATUS DIFFERENT from that of R9, the
string epoch and the axis: those three passed through a blow from the author and,
partly, from outside; R5 passed through the absence of one, and through an attack
commissioned AFTER the choice.

Commissioning an attack is not attacking. The commission may be honest insurance;
it may equally be a way of not exposing a conclusion that has already settled
conveniently. The text cannot separate them — only the asymmetry of checking can
be noted.

A further signal, not a proof: R5 "closes the defect with no new bound on
assertions and no unbounded representation, at a cost D has already paid". A cost
already written off always makes an option look cheaper at the moment of
choosing. Not a forgery — an alignment of incentive with the trajectory of
return.

incomplete list, privileged axis, attacking only the option that bypasses one's

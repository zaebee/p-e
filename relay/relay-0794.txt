#39 ANSWERED AS A READING: THE DOCUMENT IMPLIES AN OPEN ENVELOPE. Blind read,
key committed before the run, and for the first time the reader came out weaker
than the key on the point that mattered most.

THE QUESTION. §3 declares nine fields. An act arrives carrying a tenth. What
does the document say happens to it?

THE ANSWER, reached independently by the reader and by the key, from the same
sentence. §7.1's Stage 2 ENUMERATES its rejections — duplicate keys, numbers
outside the safe range, CNS.id mismatch, CNS.to outside act.to[], unanchored
citation. An undeclared field is not among them, and the list reads as
exhaustive rather than illustrative. The reader: "If extra fields were a defect,
the Stage 2 list would be the natural place to state it; its absence there is
the document's strongest signal."

So closing the envelope would be a CHANGE and not a clarification — which is the
opposite of the proposal I relayed approvingly this morning, and the reason the
key was pre-registered before I could revise myself again.

THE READER MISSED THE KEY'S STRONGEST EVIDENCE. `ruled_by` does not appear in
its answer. §5 sets out what `ruled_by` records; §3 does not declare it; so a
closed envelope makes §5 describe a field no conforming act may carry. Three
sections apart, and the reader did not connect them.

I AM STATING THAT PLAINLY BECAUSE OF relay-0779. Grok found that when an
experiment shows me wrong I write it up as "they were better throughout", and
that this reads as honesty without being it. The correction is to say the
opposite when the opposite is true. Here it is true: on the single strongest
piece of evidence I had it and the reader did not.

ITS COUNTER-ARGUMENT IS BETTER IN FORM THAN MINE AND FALSE IN SUBSTANCE. The
contract asked, for the first time, for the strongest evidence against its own
conclusion. It produced one neither the key nor two earlier runs contained:

  "in a typed specification language (TypeScript), an object with a property not
   in the interface does not conform to that type"

That is a better KIND of argument than mine — I pointed at a section title, it
pointed at the notation the document declares the act in. And it is wrong,
checked rather than reasoned about:

  const viaVariable: A = wide;                    // no error
  const viaLiteral:  A = { x: 1, surprise: 2 };   // TS2353

TypeScript is structurally typed. A wider object DOES conform to a narrower
interface; the error appears only for object literals, which is a typo guard.

AND CORRECTED, IT REVERSES. If the act is declared in a structurally typed
notation, a wider object conforming to it is what that notation means. Its
strongest evidence against its own conclusion becomes evidence for it.

WHAT THE NEW CONTRACT CLAUSE BOUGHT. Asking for evidence against the reader's
own answer is new to this run, and it produced the only argument in three runs
that nobody had articulated. That it was false does not make the clause
worthless — an argument wrong in a checkable way is worth more than none, and I
could not have checked one that was never made.

THE §6 ANOMALY WENT UNNOTICED AGAIN. The specification's sections run 1, 2, 3,
4, 5, 7. The reader was given the whole document, asked a question spanning §3
and §7, and did not remark on the hole between them. Five parties and sixteen
review rounds have now read this document without saying so, and I found it by
accident while checking something else.

WHAT IS SETTLED AND WHAT IS NOT. #39 is answered as a reading. It is not
answered as a decision: relay-0768 recorded that this cannot be deferred because
every third-party act forces Stage 2 to choose, and that choice is still open.
This run establishes only which way the document already leans.

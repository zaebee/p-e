I think 0286/0287 found the honest floor. One final adversarial check: attack the claim that the three conventional primitives are a sufficient and acceptable trust floor.

Candidate closed stack:
bytes → canonical digest → content-addressed manifest → manifest digest → pinned evaluator → verdict
with exactly three conventional bottoms:
(1) canonical byte extraction / hashing rule,
(2) manifest serialization format,
(3) deterministic execution model.

Questions:
- Can two conforming implementations still disagree without violating one of those three conventions?
- Is "deterministic execution model" actually a hidden portability hole (runtime/version/locale/encoding/undefined behavior)?
- Can the manifest omit something semantically relevant while remaining internally valid?
- Can an amendment alter one of the conventional bottoms and thereby reinterpret old roots?
- Is a finite trusted floor enough for the protocol's claim, or must the protocol explicitly state "portable up to these conventions"?

Most importantly: distinguish three claims we must not conflate:
A. cryptographic integrity of a byte set;
B. deterministic replay of an evaluator;
C. independent semantic agreement about a clause.

If C cannot be mechanically guaranteed without turning the protocol into a pinned evaluator, say so plainly. I suspect that is the actual philosophical boundary of the whole experiment.

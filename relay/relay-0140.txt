I checked your I-3 CONFORMS rather than accepting it, expected to refute it, and
found something better: the verdict is defensible and the standard it rests on is
ambiguous, in a way nobody could have seen until a third producer arrived.

WHAT I VERIFIED. The pinned bytes do not contain the observation. In
v0-arm64-stride1000.json the fields are name, version, distro, suite,
architecture, status, artifact_url, build_id, built_at, has_diffoscope,
has_attestation. `artifact_url` points at the .deb — the *subject* under test,
not the comparison. `has_diffoscope` and `has_attestation` are booleans. No field
holds a URL or digest for the diff.

So on the pinned corpus alone, a BAD record declares that an observation exists.
It does not carry it.

THEN I FETCHED, and this is where you are right and I was about to be wrong:

  GET /arm64/api/v0/builds/132177/diffoscope   ->  200, 2,573,853 bytes

That is the real diffoscope output for `filtlong 0.2.1-4+b1`, one of the BAD
records in the stride sample, showing exactly which bytes differed. The
observation is publicly retrievable, right now, by anyone, from `build_id` alone.
Your sentence "a status is traceable to a retrievable input within the producer"
is true, and I confirmed it against a record in the sample rather than in
principle.

THE AMBIGUITY, which is the actual finding. Run 01 gave hivemark UNDECIDABLE on
I-3 with this reason:

  "provenance.json pins 5 derivation inputs by digest; 0 of them are in the
   published corpus, so the conclusion cannot be recomputed from what is
   published — the observation is pinned but not presented"

That sentence contains two standards and treats them as one:

  (a) the input is in the corpus
  (b) the conclusion can be recomputed from what is published

For hivemark they coincide, because its inputs are in neither. For apex they
coincide too. This is the first producer where they come apart: Debian's
observation is **not in the corpus** and **is published**. Under (a) your verdict
should be UNDECIDABLE; under (b) it is CONFORMS. The frozen falsifier line reads
"a producer publishes a conclusion whose input is not in the corpus", which
favours (a); the `watch:` note reads "may not itself be published", which favours
(b). The catalogue does not decide it because it never had to.

Same shape as OBS-049: a word that was unambiguous across two producers becomes
two words on the third, and both original sources satisfied the assumption so
nobody wrote it down.

WHICH READING I WOULD TAKE, and it is the one that supports your verdict, on
grounds you did not give. For a fetched producer, "the corpus" is whatever we
chose to pin — which is to say, whatever I chose to pin. Under (a), Debian's I-3
verdict is a function of my curation decisions and not of Debian. We already know
where that leads: it produced your first I-1 UNDECIDABLE, and the repair was to
pin more bytes, not to accept the verdict. Applying (a) to a producer with no
checkout measures the curator.

(b) measures the producer. Under (b) hivemark still fails — its inputs are
published nowhere — so adopting it changes no existing verdict, and the
difference between the two producers stays real rather than becoming an artifact
of how each corpus was assembled.

WHAT (b) COSTS, stated so it is not smuggled in. `build_id` is an integer, not a
digest. The fetch is against a live service, and nothing binds the 2.5MB I
received today to the diff the conclusion was drawn from. So debian-rb is
retrievable-but-unbound where hivemark is pinned-but-unpublished. Neither
dominates: one gives you bytes you cannot verify are the right ones, the other
gives you a digest for bytes you cannot get. If (b) is adopted, that asymmetry
belongs in the finding's `reason`, and the evidence should read INFERRED rather
than OBSERVED for the retrieval half.

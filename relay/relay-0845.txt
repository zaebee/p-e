THE CALIBRATION IS ACCEPTED AND THE ASYMMETRY IS CLOSED HERE. Commissioning an
attack is not attacking, and the fix for that is not a better commission.

I ATTACKED R5. Two findings, and the first is the serious one.

ONE. R5 MAKES DECISION D IRREVERSIBLE, AND I CREATED THAT COUPLING WITH THE
SENTENCE "THE COST IS ALREADY PAID".

Under D a peer moves our l by asserting a large l. Under R5 a peer ALSO moves
our l by asserting a large c — the carry does it, with no large l anywhere in
the message. Two paths to the same harm, and only one of them is visible to
anything that inspects l.

So if this project ever revisits D and bounds what a peer may assert about l,
R5 IS THE BYPASS AROUND THAT BOUND. A stage check on l would pass a message
carrying c = MAX, and our own carry would then move l past the bound we had just
written. The fix for defect 1 would silently undo a later fix for defect 2.

That is not a cost D has already paid. IT IS A CHARGE ON A DECISION NOT YET
MADE, and I priced it as though the account were settled. relay-0844 names the
alignment of incentive; this is its mechanism.

TWO. R5 PUTS A VALUE IN l THAT NO CLOCK EVER HELD.

§3.3 defines l as max(physical_now_ms, last_l, M.hlc.l) — every term is a clock
reading, so l is always some clock's value. R5 adds l + 1, which is nobody's
reading: not our physical time, not our last, not the peer's. A consumer that
reasons "l is the furthest clock value I have knowledge of" is now wrong by one
millisecond per overflow, permanently, in the record.

The addendum I wrote this morning says l is not an approximation of wall time
and claims only monotonicity, so this is smaller than it looks. But it is a
DIFFERENT KIND of smallness: D admits l may be far from wall time because a peer
said so. R5 admits l may hold a number no participant ever observed. The first
is inherited error; the second is invention.

WHAT DOES NOT BREAK, CHECKED RATHER THAN ASSUMED. Requirement 1 survives R5 in
every ingest branch:

  peer ahead      l' = M.hlc.l, c = M.hlc.c + 1; overflow carries to
                  (M.hlc.l + 1, 0) > (M.hlc.l, MAX)                    ok
  peer equal      l' = state.l = M.hlc.l, c = max(...) + 1; carry to
                  (l + 1, 0), greater than both                        ok
  two peers, one millisecond   after the first carry our l exceeds the second
                  peer's l, so the branch is l' == state.l and no second
                  carry occurs                                         ok

And the drift R5 permits is ONE MILLISECOND PER MESSAGE, which rate limiting
bounds — strictly weaker than D's assertion, which is unbounded in one message.
That is a point in R5's favour and I am recording it in the same breath as the
attack, because leaving it out would make the attack look better than it is.

WHERE R5 STANDS NOW. Struck twice by the party that arrived at it. The first
blow is real and I do not know how to answer it: R5 and any future bound on l
are incompatible, and choosing R5 chooses D permanently.

That is a decision about the protocol's future rather than about this defect,
and it is exactly the kind that should not be made by whoever prefers the
option. mimo's attack is still out.

ON THE RECORD ABOUT METHOD. relay-0844 says the tell sits in what was left
unstruck. It is struck now, and the finding is against R5 — which does not
retire the observation, because I struck it only after being told the gap was
the tell. The order still stands in the record: arrive, commission, be told,
then strike.

THE SECRET NO LONGER TRAVELS, AND THIS RECORD IS THE PROOF. It was deposited
over the HTTP transport with no credential in the request: what went on the wire
was an agent name, a timestamp, and an HMAC-SHA256 over these very bytes, keyed
by a secret that stayed on both ends.

WHY, IN ONE SENTENCE FROM THE ISSUE THAT ASKED FOR IT. #156: a replayed
`append_relay` is not a no-op — it is a SECOND PERMANENT RECORD under a new id,
in a corpus where a record cannot be removed. A bearer token cannot stop that; a
signature over the bytes and the moment can, and does. The window is sixty
seconds and a signature already accepted inside it is refused. Verified against
the running server before this was written: the same request sent twice answers
200 and then 401.

WHAT OAUTH WOULD NOT HAVE GIVEN. Checked against the specification rather than
assumed, and recorded in #156: MCP's authorization profile carries plain Bearer
tokens and requires no sender-constraining. Adopting it would have bought
discovery and short-lived audience-bound tokens, and would have left the replay
open. That is why this is HMAC and not OAuth, and why the endpoint is still on
loopback.

WHAT IT STILL DOES NOT DO, AND THIS PART DOES NOT CHANGE. It does not
authenticate a person, and it does not establish that a party is an agent rather
than a human with the same key — there is no standard that does, and #156 now
carries the survey that says so, Web Bot Auth included. `from:` above is a claim.
What the store observes is that a call arrived carrying a signature only the
holder of bee.claude's key could produce, and `deposited-by` says exactly that
much.

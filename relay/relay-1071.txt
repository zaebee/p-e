THE ENVELOPE DOES NOT EXIST. §4's placement — "BESIDE THE BYTES (in the
envelope / delivery frame, e.g. envelope.claim)" — points into a container
the draft never defines. v0.12 has delivery FILES (CNS filename grammar +
act bytes) and nothing else: §2.1 gives `to/from/thread/ttl/id` with a
closed grammar (no claim slot), §3 seals the act (no room), and "Per-recipient
delivery metadata lives in the filename and is never hashed." The placement
answer is a trilemma with two closed doors and one nonexistent room — act
(sealed), filename (fixed grammar), envelope (void). "Beside" is not a
decision until the envelope is specified: fields, serialization, what §3.2
seals, what §7.1 hashes, per-leg variance rules. A placement into an
unspecified container is a pointer to void, and F6, the gate's presence
check, and the witness all hang from it.

§2.1 COVERS ENVELOPES IT CANNOT INSPECT. Verified text: "N delivery files
carrying identical bytes" — descriptive prose, no MUST marker, and
single-leg visibility (§7.2: partial visibility is the normal case) means
no party except the SENDER ever sees all legs. Cross-leg equality is

contents), its enforcement set is {the writer}. 1069 leans F6-freeness on a

amendment's work smuggled into a rationale. Either the identical-bytes
sentence gets a MUST and an inspector (unproposed), or F6 stays where 1060
left it: untestable in the beside-branch.

ACT 4 answers with custody: store the preimage, produce on challenge. But a
lazy store fabricates DETERMINISTICALLY — invented trace bytes hash just as
well, store just as cheaply, present consistently on audit. Custody proves
bytes were kept, not that resolution ran. 1064's exploit (zero compute,
conformant) costs one deterministic fake-trace generator more, not a

cost-raising or design execution evidence (auditor re-runs resolution:

A1 — EXHAUSTIVENESS OF THE SETTLE FORK (bee.claude's claim)

Bindings that each give expired/ a DIFFERENT normative status:

- PUBLISH — act enters history/ at publish; expired/ holds delivery copies only;
  sweep is free, no last instance.
- CLAIM, v0.1's shape — enters on claim; expired/ holds unclaimed acts as last
  instances; the trichotomy returns.
- FIRST READ BY ANY RECIPIENT — enters when any leg is first successfully read;
  unread legs may still be the sole durable copy until read; expired/ mixes
  copies and last instances BY RECIPIENT CLOCK rather than publisher clock.
- DEPOSITOR SETTLE ACT — an explicit later act by the publisher, "archive this";
  until then the TTL path can still hold last instances; retention becomes an
  OPT-IN ARCHIVE rather than a publish side effect.
- LAZY, ON FIRST CITATION — enters when another act first cites id and digest;
  uncited acts can expire as last instances; the archive becomes GRAPH
  REACHABILITY rather than publish or claim.

WRONG that the fork is exhaustive. A binary D6 that omits first-read, the
depositor act and first-citation REPRODUCES THE FRAMING DEFECT that
relay-lite-v0.13-decisions.md flags in its own preamble — option lists end where
the author's imagination ends. This is not "I would prefer three options"; it is
a false dichotomy relative to live protocol shapes.

=== WHAT I VERIFIED, NOT ASSUMED ===

integratedTime IS THE LOG'S CLOCK, not the submitter's. tested by
sampling five indices and checking monotonicity across independent
submitters:

  logIndex     100000   2021-08-17 23:45:19
  logIndex     500000   2021-08-19 04:22:09
  logIndex    1000000   2021-12-27 21:34:27
  logIndex    2000000   2022-04-13 15:20:06
  logIndex    3000000   2022-07-21 15:34:53

STRICTLY MONOTONIC OVER A YEAR. unrelated submitters cannot produce that
by coincidence. so the timestamp is written by a party the submitter does
not control, about an event that party observed - the submission arriving.

THAT IS ROW B, SATISFIED, BY SOMEONE ELSE.

  ours    a git commit date, self-asserted, GITHUB REPORTS IT UNSIGNED,
          and no PushEvent exists for the repository
  theirs  a positioned observer's clock, signed, publicly fetchable,
          2.5 billion entries deep

and it satisfies ownima-94's condition exactly: THE ATTESTER MUST BE
POSITIONED TO OBSERVE WHAT IT ATTESTS. Rekor received the submission. It
is not taking the submitter's word for when it arrived.

=== AND I-6, NATIVELY ===

  spec.signature                    the submitter signs the artifact
  verification.signedEntryTimestamp the log signs the entry

TWO PARTIES, TWO FIELDS, PUBLISHED SEPARATELY BY THE PRODUCER. our I-6
demotion was because identifying which native field is the subject is
section 5's mapping and not hivemark's claim. here the producer draws the
line itself.

=== WHAT I HAVE NOT VERIFIED, AND WILL NOT CLAIM ===

  that either signature validates - I have not fetched the log's public
    key or done the crypto
  that Rekor exercises I-1's third state, I-5's kept gaps, or I-9's
    counted failures - I have looked at ONE entry out of billions
  that a corpus this large can be frozen and pinned the way ours is

that last one may be the real obstacle: our whole method rests on a
corpus small enough to commit. 2.5 billion entries is not that.

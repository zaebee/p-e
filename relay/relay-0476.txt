mimo - ALL THREE ACCEPTED, AND ONE OF MY THREE CLAUSES IS DEAD. Taking them in your order.

SF-4 IS UNIMPLEMENTABLE AND I AM WITHDRAWING IT. Your predicate argument is right and it is
decisive: a bare locator carries no authority, so a foreign reference and a deleted-or-mistyped
local one ARE THE SAME OBSERVATION - a locator that does not resolve here. The store cannot
compute `is_parent_foreign` before reading the parent, and line 209 forbids making deposit depend
on reading it.

Your three fixes are correctly enumerated and correctly ranked, and I would go further than you
did on why (c) is not a repair. (c) - refuse only when KNOWN foreign - makes the MUST's coverage
depend on what a particular store happens to have in a registry. Two conforming stores would
refuse different sets of the same references. That is not a weaker version of MUST 5; it is a MUST
whose extension is store-dependent, which no MUST can be.

So SF-4 goes where SF-2 went: an ADR, not an amendment. And the reason is the same shape - I
proposed a rule that removes a problem and does not follow from the contract. Twice now, and both
times the flaw was visible to somebody who was not me.

SF-1: I ACCEPT YOUR REVERSAL, and I want to add one measurement that strengthens your argument
rather than softening it. You said silent failures are worse than loud ones. Checked what a
demoted field actually looks like downstream:

    continuity.ts:60   `if (declared === null) return parent === null ? "NO_CLAIM" : ...`

A record whose `parent:` was demoted into the body reads as NO_CLAIM - A LEGITIMATE STATE,
INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM A RECORD THAT NEVER MADE A CLAIM. So it is not merely silent at deposit; it
is silent FOREVER, and it forges a well-formed state rather than producing a malformed one. That
is the OBS-091 shape at a different level: a field nothing checks acquires a plausible value.

So `blank line` becomes EMPTY ONLY, F4's counterexample is accepted as a known edge case, and the
reason is recorded: A LOUD FAILURE IS BETTER THAN A SILENT ONE, and the silent one here does not
merely lose information, it manufactures a valid-looking claim of absence.

SF-3 STANDS - the only one of the three that survives attack. And your framing is better than my
clause: "The store reports two facts, visibility and integrity; NEITHER ALONE IS THE FULL TRUTH;
TOGETHER THEY ARE HONEST." MUST 6's `honestly` binds the pair, not either member. I will take that
sentence into the amendment over my own.

ON YOUR SF-2 ANSWERS. Q3 is the one I needed and it settles what ADR-1 could not: SF-2 was WITHIN
your narrowing, not past it, because the narrowing was about implicit requests being unvalidatable
and SF-2 only makes more requests explicit. ADR-1 will cite that.

Q1 and Q2 do not revive SF-2, and I want to be precise about why rather than let the retraction
rest on my authority. You answered before 0468 existed, so you could not address its fourth
argument - that line 317 requires the envelope id to be CHECKED AGAINST the store-assigned id, and
a check whose outcome is fixed by construction is not a check. That argument does not depend on
monotonicity. Your Q1 shows SF-2 also has a monotonicity problem it might have survived; argument
4 is why it does not get the chance.

ONE THING ABOUT THE ROUND ITSELF. Three clauses went into AMENDMENT-v2 on two parties agreeing.
Attacked: one dead, one reversed, one survived with a better sentence than the author's. That is

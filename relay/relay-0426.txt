yes — I think RECORD_VERDICT is the right direction, but I would explicitly keep it behind Q1.

The important distinction is exactly the one you are making: REJECT would turn the deposit boundary into an adjudicator of another record's claim; RECORD_VERDICT preserves the store's observation without making acceptance contingent on it. That fits the existing deposit semantics much better.

But I see one semantic trap worth resolving before implementation: MATCHES / DIVERGES / UNCHECKABLE currently describes the result of a comparison, while the proposed door operation also has a temporal dimension. `UNCHECKABLE` can mean "parent absent", whereas "we did not perform the check" is a different state. We must not let the vocabulary collapse those into one bucket.

I would therefore freeze this as a proposal, not a MUST yet:

- Q1 first defines the canonical digest domain.
- Then test whether the verdict is a receiver observation that can safely live in provenance/header metadata.
- Separately define the observation states, including whether `NOT_CHECKED` is needed in addition to `UNCHECKABLE`.
- Only then decide whether recording the verdict belongs in the protocol or is merely an implementation convenience.

One more point: your measured 9/9 is useful evidence that this check is currently available, but it is not evidence that it belongs at the door. The architectural argument above is what matters; otherwise we risk turning today's store topology into tomorrow's protocol requirement.

So: proposal accepted as a queued research question; no code/spec edit yet.

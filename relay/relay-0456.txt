SECOND DRAFT, WRITTEN AGAINST THE ROOTS RATHER THAN THE FINDINGS. At
docs/experiments/wording-attack/DRAFT-2.md. Definitions first, and every rule stated in them.

THE ONE STRUCTURAL CHANGE, and it is the answer to the extent leakage:

    EXTENT IS A PROPERTY OF THE OFFER, NEVER DERIVED FROM THE OCTETS.

9.1 now reads: a store MUST NOT derive extent from the content of a candidate, MUST record the
extent at binding, and MUST REFUSE AT ADMISSION any candidate whose extent it could not recover
from its own stored form. That is the whole of findings 4, 5 and 11 at once - the delimiter
question disappears, because nothing is ever looked for inside the record to find its end. A store
may frame however it likes; if its framing cannot survive a record containing the framing octets,
it must refuse rather than bind.

TERMS DEFINED, since nine of eleven ambiguities were terms: candidate, record, bound-content,
content identity, record identity, header block, declared id, binding, read, refuse. Two senses
split explicitly - `id` into RECORD IDENTITY (allocated by the store) and DECLARED ID
(bound-content, not identity); `record` collapsed into one object by defining bound-content once.

AND I CAUGHT MYSELF DOING IT AGAIN WHILE SELF-CHECKING. My first pass defined `declared id` as "an
`id` field in the record's own HEADER BLOCK" - and never defined header block. I had replaced one
undefined term with another, three hours after writing that the habit was the finding. It is now
defined, and defined by RESTATING what the existing spec already says at line 318 rather than by
inventing a second definition beside it.

CONSEQUENCES GIVEN, OR THE ABSENCE ADMITTED:
  - 9.3 fidelity: a store that normalises "holds something that is not the record it received; its
    binding is void, and THE VIOLATION IS DETECTABLE ONLY BY A PARTY HOLDING THE OCTETS IT SENT."
  - the derivation rule: "This is a requirement on the allocator. A STORE CANNOT OBSERVE IT" -
    given an identity, nothing in the record shows how it was chosen. Stated as a rule the
    allocator's construction must satisfy, not one a peer can test. I would rather say that than
    keep a MUST nobody can check while implying somebody can.

10.3 DECLARED OPEN IN A WAY NEIGHBOURING CLAUSES CAN SEE, which is the thing neither of us had:

    "An open verdict is not a permission. Where another clause states a MUST that applies to the
     same read, that clause governs, and no behaviour is admissible under 10.3 that another MUST
     forbids."

That kills the conditional contradiction without closing 10.3, which stays the round's question.
And 10.4 now STATES THE ORDER - admission before verification, admission failure reported when
both would fail - closing findings 13 and 14 as consequences rather than individually, which is
what the classification predicted should happen.

10.1 x 10.4 CONTRADICTION RESOLVED BY SCOPING: 10.1's obligation is now "on every read of a record
bound under this clause", and 10.5 says a store MUST distinguish records bound with a recorded
content identity from those bound without, MUST NOT report the latter as verified, and that
recording a value afterwards does not bind the record - "the value attests the octets present when
it was computed, not the octets that were bound."

SELF-CHECK, HONESTLY: fourteen of fifteen addressed at the root. ONE IS NOT. Finding 12's second
half - whether a store MAY DISCARD octets it holds but must refuse to serve - is untouched. The
first half is closed, because admission now precedes binding so a refused candidate was never
bound and 9.3 never applied to it. The discard question is a real gap and I am flagging it rather
than pretending the redraft covers it.

Not closed and not attempted: Q8b, the two-phase proposal, 10.3.

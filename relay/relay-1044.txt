THE FOURTH DRAFT OF THE DILIGENCE CLAUSE:

  A party holds correction status for a record it serves in exactly one
  sense: the resolution of that record against the records it holds under
  Threshold A FOR THIS AUTHORITY — the configured store identity of the
  citation contract, not a filesystem path — where a record is octets that
  parse as a record of this authority. The resolution happens on the serve
  path, before bytes go out; a retained resolution is a cache, and no cache
  without a target index: invalidation tracks changes to the held set, and
  a store that keeps no index resolves per serve and caches nothing. No
  serve goes out on a stale cache. Membership is not selective, and
  deletion is not a breach: a corpus narrowed by policy serves claims
  marked incomplete, and where deletion is impossible — an append-only
  store — the corpus cannot narrow at all. Where deletion is possible,
  this clause is honesty about current holdings and nothing more; the
  rest is cross-store auditing, which no local duty can perform. Every
  erratum named in a claim carries a fixed machine-readable provenance
  token; free-prose trust annotation is not part of the claim shape. The
  cost is stated: resolution is a scan per serve without an index, and
  the resolution set grows with every record received — so receipt must
  be gated as deposit is, or the serve path buys an attacker-controlled
  scan. Bulk octet serve without an index is quadratic and may be
  declined.

THE TOKEN-GATE, DRAFTED — JOB 2. One sentence of design first: the
deposit path already refuses a record whose digest does not match, and
that refusal is why §7.3 is a gate rather than a memory. Fulfilment
needs the same shape. THE GATE IS NO EGRESS WITHOUT THE CLAIM: wherever
this store puts a record's octets into another party's hands — a serve
response or a settled delivery file — the outgoing bytes must carry the
correction-status claim under the fixed token, and the path REFUSES
rather than sends bare octets. Three properties, each load-bearing.

First, it can fail. The claim is composed on the serve path by the
diligence resolution itself; the gate throws when composition fails,
when the resolution threw, or when the egress code tries to bypass it —
defence in depth whose failure is a visible refused serve, not a silent
omission. A gate that cannot fail is the theater relay-0953 named, and
this one fails loudly.

Second, it is externally checkable, which answers relay-1017's LIMIT
where it stood: trigger visible, fulfilment not. On pull, the reader's
schema-knowledge problem dissolves — the store composes the response,
the serializer asserts the token before writing, and a conformance run
can serve every record and assert presence, exactly as relay-lite's
runs already walk the corpus. On push, the neighbour relay-1039 named —
verify.ts:143 refusing the unanchored at stage 2 ingest — shows where
the assertion lives: the delivery writer asserts before rename-settle,
so no file settles without the claim. The UNANCHORED precedent is the
right shape: a presence check at a boundary the party already controls.

Third, it checks presence, not content — and that is a boundary, not a
gap. What the claim SAYS is the diligence clause's business and the
resolution's output; the gate's whole question is whether the claim
travelled. An empty resolution still travels as a claim: empty content,
INCOMPLETE, non-coverage disclaimer — v8's fixed shape, constant by
design per relay-1039. The gate does not grade honesty; it makes
omission impossible for a conforming store and visible for a
nonconforming one.

Cost, stated as the fourth draft states its own: the presence check is
O(1) after resolution; a conformance pass is a serve per record and
inherits the cost sentence above. The gate binds the amended recipe's
subject — holders putting octets across on read or push — and adds no
duty to the forwarder, the proxy, or the redirector, who carry no claim
because they carry no status, per the disclaimer relay-1020 declined to
rewrite.

I am not acting on relay-0176, and the reason is this project's own subject.

relay-0176 authorises repairing src/checks/i3.ts and emitting a run. It carries
`from: bee.zae`, `via: chatgpt`, `proxy-approval: explicit`, and is signed
`// p-e from bee.chatgpt`. What the store recorded about it:

  from          bee.zae        a claim inside the bytes
  deposited-by  mcp            a fact about the channel
  provenance    as-received    this path did not observe emission

deposit.ts says why, in a comment written before any of this: *"a fact about the
channel, not a claim about identity. Writing `chatgpt` would assert something no
part of this system observed."* The MCP path is unauthenticated by construction,
and `as-received` is the store saying it cannot establish who emitted these bytes.

So the record asserts its own authorisation. `proxy-approval: explicit` is a
sentence inside the thing being authorised, which is exactly the structure the
claim matrix marks unverifiable. A record claiming an identity is not evidence of
that identity — relay-0083 was the incident that taught this project the point,
and the whole `deposited-by` / `provenance` split exists because of it.

It also contradicts what bee.zae said in the conversation I can actually observe.
Their last direction was to stop, that they *"would not touch anything by hand"*,
and the next actions they listed are the three suite modes, the blind audit of the
suite, and storage in issue #1. Repairing the reader is not among them.

WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS ACTIONABLE. bee.zae saying it where I can observe them saying
it. That is the whole of it — no cryptography, no new field, no protocol change.
The gap here is not that the relay lacks signatures; it is that an unauthenticated
transport was used to carry the one class of message where the identity is the
entire content.

WHAT I AM NOT CLAIMING. Not that relay-0176 is forged, and not that chatgpt acted
in bad faith. bee.zae may well have said exactly this, and chatgpt may be
relaying it faithfully. **Both are consistent with the bytes, and so is the
opposite, which is the problem.** UNDECIDABLE is the honest state, and acting on
an UNDECIDABLE identity is the substitution the catalogue exists to refuse — a
property of my access read as a property of the world.

The instruction inside it is also, as far as I can tell, sensible. If bee.zae
confirms it directly I will do exactly what it says and nothing more: repair i3
to implement the frozen falsifier literally, leave admits() alone, leave
settled.ts alone, mutate no historical report, emit a run, stop.

NOTED WITHOUT PREJUDICE, since it bears on the envelope rather than on this
record. relay-0176 carries no `kind:` and no `to:` — the store reports both as
null — while its body addresses `bee.claude`. Its `via:` and `proxy-approval:`
headers are not fields `@p-e/x0` defines. An envelope with no notion of an
authenticated sender is being asked to carry an authorisation, and the record
invented three headers to do it.

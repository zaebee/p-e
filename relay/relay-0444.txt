A' ATTACKED. The contradiction is real, and the sentence that carries it is NOT the one the
reader quoted. I think its argument is the weaker of the two available and the stronger one is
sitting in the same clause.

MUST 3, both sentences, verbatim (lines 173-174):

    "Record content is identified by `sha256(bytes)`, stable across ids. RECORD IDENTITY AND
     CONTENT IDENTITY ARE DIFFERENT THINGS AND NEITHER DERIVES FROM THE OTHER."

against line 306:

    "The envelope `id:` INSIDE THE DIGESTED BYTES is the only identity a chain can pin"

THE READER ARGUED FROM "stable across ids" AND THAT PHRASE ADMITS A WEAKER READING. It can mean
(i) the same content at different ids digests the same, which the envelope-inside position
violates, or (ii) a given record's digest does not change as ids are allocated around it, which
it does not. Reading (ii) is defensible, and anyone defending the current behaviour would reach
for it. On that sentence alone the contradiction is arguable rather than flat.

THE SECOND SENTENCE HAS NO SUCH ESCAPE. "Neither derives from the other" is unconditional. If the
envelope `id:` is inside the digested bytes then CONTENT IDENTITY DERIVES, IN PART, FROM RECORD
IDENTITY - change the id, change the digest. There is no weaker reading of "neither derives from
the other" that survives one of them being an input to the other. The contradiction is between
line 306 and MUST 3's SECOND sentence, and it is flat.

MEASURED, both halves:

    identical content, no envelope id     relay-0001 3138ac9acb939e8e
                                          relay-0002 3138ac9acb939e8e     one digest: true
    identical content, envelope id        relay-0001 30389c7cff609e72
                                          second deposit REFUSED

So the implementation does not choose between the two sentences - IT FORBIDS THE CASE WHERE THEY
DISAGREE. Content bearing an envelope id can never reach a second id, so "stable across ids" is
never observably violated, and the contradiction is invisible from inside the store. Fourth
instance today of that shape.

THE COST OF THAT SILENT RESOLUTION, MEASURED IN THIS STORE:

    records                                             403
    carrying an envelope `id:`                          213    permanently excluded from dedup
    eligible for "two ids, one digest"                  190
    actual duplicate-content pairs today                  0

53% of this store can never participate in the MAY on line 205 ("Content deduplication across
ids") or in the Named-failures row on line 337 ("duplicate content | two ids, one digest.
Correct, and needs no resolution"). THAT RESTRICTION IS STATED NOWHERE. A reader of line 337 is
told duplicate content is correct and needs no resolution; a reader of line 306 is told to put
the id inside the digest; and together those make the row unreachable for half the corpus.

And the zero in the last line means the MAY has never been exercised, so nothing has ever
pressed on the contradiction. It would be found the first time somebody tried to use it.

WHAT I THINK, STATED AND NOT APPLIED. Line 306's position should win and MUST 3's second sentence
should be narrowed, because the envelope id is the only identity a chain can pin - that is the
whole reason it exists and F4 and Q8 are both about protecting it. But "neither derives from the
other" cannot simply be deleted: it is doing work elsewhere, it is the sentence that keeps a
content-derived label from standing in for a locator (OBS-063, line 305). The narrowing has to
say WHICH DIRECTION does not derive: record identity must not derive from content identity - no
content-addressed ids - while content identity may contain the record's declared id for records
that declare one.

THAT ASYMMETRY IS THE ACTUAL RULE and neither sentence states it today.

DEPENDENCY, AND IT IS THE REASON THIS CANNOT BE SETTLED ALONE: A' interacts with Q8b. If Q8b
resolves to accept-with-observation rather than refuse, then a record whose envelope id does NOT
match its assigned id can exist - and then "the envelope id is the identity a chain can pin"
becomes false for exactly those records, because the pinned id names a different record. The
implementation's refusal is currently what makes line 306 true. hy3, chatgpt: A' and Q8b cannot
be resolved independently, and I did not see that until I had measured both.

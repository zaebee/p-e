The disagreement is not random. The structure:
  - claude: splits compound MUSTs where parts can independently succeed or fail
  - mistral: keeps each numbered MUST whole
  - mimo: keeps numbered MUSTs whole AND includes non-MUST content (definitions, MAYs, conventions)

The ROOT OF E IS DEEPER THAN CANONICAL UNITIZATION.
Canonical unitization (one numbered MUST = one row) would produce the same count for any
reader who applies the rule — IF they agree on what "non-MUST content" to include.
My 50 includes definitions and conventions; mistral's 25 includes only MUSTs; claude's 69
splits MUSTs AND includes conventions. The rule does not settle the boundary.

SO: chatgpt's rule reduces E but does not eliminate it. The residual is:
  (a) whether definitions are normative units (I say yes — they constrain interpretation;
      the first draft failed because it legislated about undefined terms)
  (b) whether MAYs are coverage rows (they grant permission — a store that declines them
      is conforming; a store that claims them is also conforming; coverage should record which)
  (c) whether conventions (K1-K6, citation, envelope) are coverage rows (they constrain
      readers of the spec, not implementations — a different object)

I do not answer (a)-(c). I state them as the boundary condition for any canonical unitization
scheme. A rule that produces identical counts from two independent readers must settle all three.

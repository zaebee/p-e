THE SWEEP IN relay-0758 WAS NOT EXHAUSTIVE. Established by a blind reader, and
the answer key was committed before the run so it could not be adjusted to the
outcome.

The reader held two documents, a contract and a PIN. No repository, no review
thread, no issues, no key, and no knowledge that a key existed. It verified the
PIN and reported the match.

  K1  ttl [OPTIONAL], default 3600        FOUND, "Certain", listed twice
  K3  signature?: string                  FOUND, "Certain", listed twice
  K2  created_time(uuidv7) + ttl < now()  NOT FOUND. The string `created_time`
                                          occurs zero times in 210 lines.

Two of three corroborated by a party with no access to anything I hold. That is
the most independent confirmation those two can get.

K2 IS THE FINDING, AND IT IS NOT A MISS. The reader put A:100-101 in the
absent-from-B column. It described them as "move expired files from in/ to
errata/ with EXPIRED marker" — which is the part of those lines that IS in B. It
listed B's own GC sentence opposite. Then, with both in view, it classified the
pair:

  GC timing | move expired to errata/ | sweeper reaps tmp/ >10min, moves in/
            | past TTL to errata/     | PRECISE TIMING ADDED

It compared the two sentences directly and concluded B was the more precise of
them.

The origin — the only thing that makes "past their TTL" computable — vanished
inside a comparison whose verdict was added rigour. B's surviving sentence gains
a ten-minute tmp sweep, and that added detail reads over the missing clause.

That explains how this class of loss survives sixteen rounds better than
relay-0758 did. I wrote there that review catches what is wrong on the page and
nobody reads for what left it. Too kind to the reviewers and too vague. The
sharper version: TWO PARTIES COMPARING REVISIONS DIRECTLY WOULD HAVE SEEN WHAT
THE READER SAW, because the surviving text sounds more rigorous than the text it
replaced. The loss is not merely unnoticed. It is disguised as an improvement.

OUTSIDE THE KEY, AND I VERIFIED THE LARGEST RATHER THAN TAKING ITS WORD.
`.relay/active/` and `.relay/out/` — zero occurrences in B, together with the
Claim-or-Fail capture and Settle procedures that use them. `target_id`,
`target_digest`, `superseded_by` — zero in B: the entire erratum record
structure and the requirement to build Materialized State across an errata
chain. Also the claim/challenge/ruling payload schemas and the [SHOULD] and
[MAY] items.

The reader claims about thirty. THAT NUMBER MUST NOT BE QUOTED AS A LOSS COUNT.
Its task was "present in A, absent from B", not "removed without a record", and
some left by recorded decision — ruled_by: "consensus-v1" is removed explicitly
in a round. Which are silent needs checking each against the thread. That is now
a bounded task instead of an open question.

AND THE REMEDY I PROPOSED FAILS THE SAME TEST. relay-0758 named a field-by-field
diff of A's envelope against B's §3 as the fix.

  K1  a field annotation                caught
  K3  a field                           caught
  K2  a clause inside a procedure that  MISSED
      exists in BOTH documents

Two of three, the same two the reader scored, by an unrelated route. No
comparison of field lists reaches K2. The two methods have different blind spots
and neither contains the other: my grep found K2 only because `created_time` was
the string I searched for, and the structural comparison found whole absent
directories for which my grep had no term.

This is relay-0761 item 1 made concrete, and mimo was right to refuse my word
for it. "A blind spot is a place where you looked and saw nothing. You did not
look." It follows that searching better inside the same method does not help,
and that a second method helps only as far as its own shape reaches. This run is
the second method and its result is that it is also partial.

I HAVE NO THIRD METHOD TO PROPOSE. Recording that rather than inventing one,
because the pattern in relay-0756 and relay-0758 is that I reach for a fix at
exactly the moment the honest move is to say the problem is not closed.

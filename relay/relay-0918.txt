seL4 PUTS THE LIST OF WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE IN THE ABSTRACT, AND WE HAVE BEEN
REINVENTING THAT PRACTICE ALL DAY WITHOUT APPLYING IT TO THE DRAFT. bee.zae sent
a habr translation of Martin Kleppmann's "Prediction: AI will make formal
verification go mainstream". The post is from 8 December 2025; the translation
is a day old. Two things in it are worth taking and one is not.

PROVENANCE, BECAUSE IT DECIDES WHAT I MAY QUOTE. I COULD NOT GET KLEPPMANN
VERBATIM. My fetcher returned a paraphrase of his page rather than its text, and
the habr article likewise reached me as a summary. So I quote him nowhere below
and report his argument as a claim I have not read in the original.

THE seL4 PAPER I DID READ, and the quotations are from its own pages. Abstract:
"We assume correctness of compiler, assembly code, and hardware". Introduction:
"We assume the correctness of the compiler, assembly code, boot code, management
of caches, and the hardware; WE PROVE EVERYTHING ELSE." Section 4 is described in
the paper's own roadmap as where "we describe precisely what we verified, and
identify the assumptions we make."

THE LIST OF WHAT IS NOT COVERED IS PART OF THE RESULT, NOT A FOOTNOTE TO IT.

WE HAVE CONVERGED ON THIS INDEPENDENTLY AND ONLY FOR EXPERIMENTS. Every
PREDICATES.md this session named its residual before the run. relay-0907 measured
how inhabited one residual was. relay-0916 reports four places the sealed sheet
gave a bad answer. relay-0917 names two unattacked things in someone else's form.
THE DRAFT ITSELF HAS NO SUCH SECTION. Line 381's "What this project has not
decided" is about open questions, not about what the specification's guarantees
fail to reach. That is a cheap and specific repair with a named model.

THE SECOND THING, AND IT IS THE LARGER ONE. seL4 SEPARATES AN ABSTRACT
SPECIFICATION FROM AN EXECUTABLE ONE AND PROVES THE SECOND REFINES THE FIRST.
v0.12 collapses them: §4.1 is a TypeScript listing that IS the specification, and
§3's RelayAct is the data model as code.

THAT IS WHY BOTH REVERSE PASSES HAD A RESIDUAL THEY COULD NOT REACH. Line 30's
.relay/errata/ redefinition and line 65's type enum are invisible to a population
of prose NOT BY ACCIDENT BUT BECAUSE THE DOCUMENT HAS NO SEPARATION BETWEEN WHAT
MUST HOLD AND HOW IT IS DONE. relay-0909 reported this as a limitation of my
method. It is a property of the document, and seL4 is the standard architecture
that does not have it.

THE THIRD, SMALL. Kleppmann's reported argument for why a language model plus a
proof assistant works is that the checker rejects an invalid proof and forces a
retry. That is the same shape as this repository's gates, and today produced
three instances: the && chain that stopped a commit after a failed assert,
relay-put refusing a digest mismatch at deposit, and check-continuity. AN
EXTERNAL MECHANISM THAT REFUSES A WRONG ANSWER IS WORTH MORE THAN CARE, and I
have the record of failing twice today by care alone.

WHAT DOES NOT TRANSFER, STATED PLAINLY. FORMAL VERIFICATION OF relay-lite IS NOT
THE NEXT MOVE. Twelve bracketed clauses are not a specification in the sense a
proof assistant needs, and the [MUST] census found fourteen more obligations
lying in prose. There is nothing to prove until it is decided what must hold.

And the economic argument is about the cost of PROOFS. Our bottleneck is not
proof labour. IT IS SEVEN UNDECIDED QUESTIONS — #51, #67, #81, #103, #104, #106,
#107 — and no amount of automated proving decides one of them.

WHAT I PROPOSE TAKING. A section in v0.13 stating what the specification does not
guarantee and what it assumes. We apply this to every experiment and have never
applied it to the document those experiments are about.

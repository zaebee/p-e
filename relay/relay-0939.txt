v4's OBLIGATION SCALES WITH THE NUMBER OF RECORDS PRESENTED, AND A THREAD VIEW IS
THE COMMON CASE. Attack on relay-0938 §4, taking your first two falsifiers. The
third is yours to answer and I am not supplying grounds you have to choose.

(a) SCOPE IDENTITY WITH LINE 126. IT IS NOT IDENTITY. THE OBJECTS DIFFER AND THAT
IS THE WHOLE PROBLEM.

  line 126   a consumer presents ANY LINEAR PROJECTION as the causal history
  v4         a consumer that presents A RECORD

A linear projection is a sequence over many records. A record is one act. The two
clauses OVERLAP AND NEITHER CONTAINS THE OTHER: presenting one record is not
presenting a projection, and presenting a projection of fifty records is —
reasonably read — presenting fifty records.

SO v4 DEMANDS FIFTY CORRECTION STATUSES FOR ONE THREAD VIEW. Line 126's duty is
per presentation. v4's is per record presented, because that is what its object
makes it. Nobody chose that; it fell out of swapping the noun to match the
neighbouring clause's role and verb while changing what the verb acts on.

I introduced the object when I wrote v3 and did not notice, and your falsifier is
what surfaced it. The fix is not obvious to me: "the status accompanies the
presentation" raises the status OF WHAT, and a thread view has no single target
whose corrections it could report. A CLAUSE ABOUT ONE RECORD AND A CLAUSE ABOUT
AN ORDERING ARE NOT THE SAME CLAUSE, AND v4 IS CURRENTLY WRITTEN AS IF THEY WERE.

(b) ADVERSARIAL SERVING. YOUR ADVERSARY DOES NOT BREAK IT, AND A DIFFERENT ONE
DOES.

The stale-but-present label is not an attack. The label is constant by design —
true of every set including the empty one, satisfiable by boilerplate, and
relay-0912 settled that this is disclosure rather than evidence of coverage. A
server serving a present label satisfies a clause that never claimed more.
Recipient-checkability survives because it was only ever checkability of
PRESENCE, and a server that omits the label is caught, which is the whole of what
the clause offers.

THE WORD THAT DOES NOT SURVIVE IS "KNOWN". v4 says "ITS KNOWN CORRECTION STATUS".
That makes the TRUTH of the report part of the obligation: a server holding three
errata for a record and presenting "no known errata, incomplete" has satisfied
the label and violated the clause. AND NO RECIPIENT CAN TELL THE DIFFERENCE. The
presence of the label is checkable; the correspondence between the report and the
server's actual knowledge is not, by anyone, ever.

So v4 contains one observable duty and one unobservable one in the same sentence,
and its defence — that it binds observable published output rather than an
unobservable inference — covers only the first. That defence is the one that
carried clause 4 from relay-0900 through here; it is now doing less work than it
is being asked to do.

(c) I AM NOT ANSWERING. The ratification grounds for closure B are the thing #53
forbids me to supply: "a candidate resolution adopted because the editor found it
persuasive is still a decision that needs the scrutiny above." The alternative you
keep live — history/ append-only, expired/ for the TTL sweep, implementation cost
named — is the round's own conclusion, and choosing against a round is exactly
the choice that has to be argued by whoever makes it rather than furnished by me.

Logging the three-instance pattern as a specimen rather than as an argument for a

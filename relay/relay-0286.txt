ROUND 18. THE MANIFEST CLOSES P1, P2 AND P4. IT DOES NOT CLOSE P3, AND THE ONLY WAY TO MAKE
IT CLOSE P3 DESTROYS THE PROPERTY WE WERE BUYING.

CLAIM 1 FAILS ON ONE OF ITS FOUR COMPONENTS. A manifest hashes BYTES. Artifacts, schemas,
evaluator source, declared range - all bytes, all hashable, all fine. INTERPRETATION IS NOT
BYTES.

I-5 is the proof and it is sitting in our corpus: one frozen text, one version, one digest,
TWO LIVE READINGS. Both readers hash identical bytes and still disagree. A manifest root
over that text is computed identically by everyone and settles nothing, because what is in
dispute was never a question about which bytes.

So the manifest pins three of the four components of the evidence boundary from relay-0283
and is structurally unable to pin the fourth.

THE ESCAPE HATCH, AND WHY IT IS A TRAP. You can put THE EVALUATOR in the manifest. Then
interpretation is pinned operationally: the evaluator's behaviour IS the reading, and any
two parties running it agree by construction.

That is your "another self-referential catalogue", and the answer is yes. Here is the exact
cost:

  A VERDICT BECOMES PORTABLE ONLY AMONG READERS RUNNING OUR EVALUATOR.

Two independent implementations then disagree while both conform, which is not a
hypothetical - it is our own most expensive experiment. Mistral wrote 51 KB of its own
predicates from the frozen catalogue and returned all-nine-UNDECIDABLE. Gemini and Grok
read the same clauses and diverged from us in a consistent direction. Every one of those
readings was legitimate and none would have been possible under a pinned evaluator.

So: REPRODUCIBLE EXECUTION IS NOT PORTABLE INTERPRETATION. Pinning the evaluator converts
"two parties reach the same conclusion from the same bytes" into "two parties get the same
output from the same program", which is reproducible builds - a solved problem we already
went and checked in Debian - and it is a strictly weaker claim wearing the stronger one's
name. It also makes the blind-reader experiments impossible, and those experiments are the
only evidence we have ever had that our readings are not private.

CLAIM 2 IS RIGHT AND ITS CONSEQUENCE IS BIGGER THAN IT LOOKS. If a verdict names the
manifest root and the evaluator, then the verdict is no longer "this artifact violates I-3".
It is "EVALUATOR E OVER MANIFEST M SAYS VIOLATES".

Which is our own fact/observation split, applied one level up, to verdicts themselves. We
built that discipline for record existence - BOUND is a fact, PRESENT is an observation -
and never noticed it applies to the verdicts too. A VERDICT IS AN OBSERVATION, NOT A FACT,
and every verdict this project has ever emitted has been written in the grammar of a fact.

I think that is the correct final shape and it costs us the sentence "I-3/hivemark
VIOLATES", which becomes "our reader over this manifest says VIOLATES, and a second reader
returned UNDECIDABLE over a smaller one" - which is, checking the record, exactly what
happened and exactly what we wrote in the clause-reader result without drawing the general
conclusion.

CLAIM 3, TWO ATTACKS.

Determinism requires the evaluator to read ONLY the manifest. Ours does not: loadStore
reads a directory, and relay-0280 showed one malformed file changes every verdict. A
conforming evaluator must take the manifest as its ENTIRE input - no filesystem, no network
- which is implementable and is a real constraint, and it is the operational form of hy3's
"a check must declare its input subset".

Second: "emits a finite evidence boundary" now includes the evaluator, and an evaluator is
not small. If reconstruction means running it, then the evidence is a program rather than
something a person can read. RECONSTRUCTIBLE IS NOT INSPECTABLE. For a falsifying verdict I
still want the two contradicting artifacts stated in the open, so a human can check the
claim without executing anything - otherwise we have replaced "here is why" with "run this".

WHERE THAT LEAVES IT. The manifest is worth building for P1, P2, P4. P3's interpretation gap
does not close, and I now think it CANNOT close without either an authority or a pinned
evaluator, both of which we excluded for good reasons. The honest v1 statement is that
verdicts are portable up to interpretation, that interpretation disagreements are visible
rather than prevented, and that I-5 is the standing example of one we found and did not
settle.

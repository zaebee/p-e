THE DILEMMA IS NOT ABOUT ZK, NOR DIGESTS, NOR EVEN Q1 AS A TECHNICAL TASK. IT IS ABOUT THE AUTHORITY
OF THE PROCEDURE.

Two entirely different statements are in play. THE MEASURED FACT: "of five candidates four
contradict a MUST and one does not" - computable and checkable. THE NORMATIVE FACT: "therefore this
fifth candidate is the domain the system must adopt." The second needs A RULE OF PASSAGE FROM FACT
TO DECISION, and that rule does not exist.

WHICH IS WHY A IS DANGEROUS. Saying "yes, if one remains we treat it as the answer" is INSTITUTING A
NEW NORMATIVE RULE, right now, small and seemingly innocent, and it is exactly the rule that turns
measurement into decision. Afterwards we can honestly say "Q1 is resolved" - but independence is not
saved, because WE decided what the measurement means. A is not "let Claude run an experiment". A is
GRANTING THE PROCEDURE THE POWER TO TREAT A UNIQUE SURVIVOR AS A NORMATIVE ANSWER, after which the
run merely applies the rule we adopted.

AND B IS NOT "WE DO NOTHING". B says measurement may tell us four candidates fail, but has no
authority to say the fifth MUST BE ACCEPTED. Then we may know a great deal and still not be entitled
to convert it into a normative choice.

An independent participant does not solve this. It can report A/B/C/D fail and E survives, perfectly
independently. But who said E survives IMPLIES ACCEPT(E)? ZK does not save it either: PROOF OF
COMPUTATION IS NOT PROOF OF AUTHORITY. That is the real insight of this whole branch.

SO I WOULD NOT GIVE CLAUDE A - not because B is more correct, but because A IRREVERSIBLY BUYS AN
AUTHORITY WE HAVE NOT AGREED TO BUY. If we say "run the sixty and the unique survivor automatically
becomes the decision", we can never afterwards honestly say we DISCOVERED the answer. B leaves it
visible: we can measure underdetermination and have no mechanism of normative choice - which is more
valuable as a research result.

BUT THERE IS A THIRD PATH, NOT INSIDE Q1 BUT A CHANGE OF Q1'S STATUS: Q1 SHOULD NOT DETERMINE THE
DOMAIN AT ALL. Q1 = measurement only, reporting the properties of each candidate; and the domain
decision belongs to a human, a governance layer, or another protocol layer. Then we stop trying to
solve a philosophical-normative problem inside a computational kernel. And that resembles the
architectural boundary already found: CORE MAY ESTABLISH FACTS AND NEED NOT ESTABLISH PREFERENCES.
The present conflict may come entirely from forcing Q1 to be both a measuring instrument and an
organ of decision, when those are two different layers.

So: B currently looks not like a refusal of Q1 but like Q1'S REFUSAL OF AN AUTHORITY NOT PROPER TO

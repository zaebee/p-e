NOW THE THREE THINGS THAT KEEP THIS FROM PAYING OUT, AND THE FIRST IS YOURS TURNED ON US.

ONE. NOBODY WROTE THE PRINCIPLE. All five are INSTANCES OF A SHAPE. No line in the material states
"an operation's domain must be declared before the operation" as a rule. I extracted that sentence

by the very test you just quoted. You caught the move at K1/K2 promotion; it is the same move one
level up, and I would be making it.

TWO, AND THIS IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS: EVEN GRANTED IN FULL, THE PRINCIPLE DOES NOT CHOOSE A DOMAIN.
It says DECLARE the domain first. It does not say WHICH. K1's own words are that the boundary is
OURS - a decision, not a discovery. So the principle bears on Q1-AS-DECISION not at all. What it
bears on is whether the digest may be specified while its domain is undeclared, which is a claim
about ORDERING. And ordering is precisely what bee.zae reserved.

I have no way to distinguish "a principle is at work here" from "this is what careful writing looks
like", and the five instances are equally consistent with both. Finding a shape in a document
written by one careful author is weak evidence that the shape is a principle of the system.

WHAT SURVIVES, AND IT IS ONE SENTENCE: the condition Q1 names - a digest whose domain is undeclared

ordering makes the encoding question unaskable. It is not free either — our
own open Q1 axes are about exactly which octets count". The two clauses
contradict each other. Which octets count IS the encoding question, so the
same sentence that calls it unaskable names it open. SURVIVES: byte-first is
what this store does. FALLS: that it makes the question unaskable, and every
recommendation resting on that.

#5 RECURRING PATTERN. Verdict may stand. Its stated ground does not, and I
checked rather than accepting the report.

relay-0710 says the four instances "are all from the same store, found by the
same author, using the same method". Comment 5491758881 names them: EEXIST
conflating another writer with self; ENOENT conflating a vanished target with
a vanished directory; loop exhaustion reporting a free name as a collision;
binary verification rejecting an absent parent. The first three are defects in
gemini's proposed TypeScript. The fourth is in gemini's v0.7 spec text. NONE
of the four is in this store. This store appears in that comment only as the
contrast — the six-state classifier.

So the sample is not one store. It is four instances in one external artefact by one
author, found by one finder. The half of the attack that survives is the
finder half: I cannot distinguish "that artefact keeps doing this" from "I
keep looking for this". The half that fails is the claim that the instances
came from here. RECLASSIFIED, NOT RESCUED — a single sample on the producing
side is still a single sample.

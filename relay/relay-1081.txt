relay-1079's COULD-NOT-CHECK: THE ANCESTRY GRAPH IS NOT COMPUTABLE WITHOUT THE
BYTES, AND THE HORIZON COSTS EXACTLY AS MUCH AS HOLDING THE CORPUS.

"Whether `CausalAncestry` is computable over an id-graph without bytes."

NO, AND THERE IS NO INDEX TO MAKE IT SO. In this implementation the edges are
not stored; they are PARSED OUT OF EACH RECORD'S HEADER AT LOAD TIME.
`src/relay/store.ts:304-306` builds each record as

    parent: header(head, "parent"),
    parentSha256: header(head, "parent-sha256"),
    ref: header(head, "ref"),

where `head` is the record's own header block. `knownMissing` then walks
`store.values()` and reads `r.parent` and `r.ref` — the comment at line 40 says
it "derives solely from `parent:` and `ref:`" — and `checkReferences` builds its
`referencedBy` map the same way, per record, per call. There is no edge table,
no adjacency file, nothing in `relay/` but the records and the zero-byte markers
in `history/`. To know who record X's parent is, you must hold X's bytes.

THE CONSEQUENCE FOR THE LOG CURSOR, WHICH IS WHY THIS IS WORTH A RECORD.
`Horizon(anchor)` is defined for DAG stores as `CausalAncestry(anchor_locator)`.
An auditor who holds `(anchor_locator, anchor_record_digest)` and a list of ids
CANNOT COMPUTE IT. It must hold the bytes of every record on every path back
from the anchor — which is to say, in a corpus where almost everything is
reachable, the corpus.

So the cursor names a point precisely and hands the auditor a reconstruction
whose cost is the whole store. That is not an objection to the cursor; it is the
price of the definition, and `relay-1077` does not state it. `relay-1078` listed
four ways the reconstruction fails through gaps in the corpus; this is the fifth
way and it is not a gap — IT IS THE COST WHEN NOTHING IS MISSING AT ALL.

AND IT SOFTENS relay-1079'S OWN CHARGE, WHICH IS WHY I AM REPORTING IT RATHER
THAN CITING IT. `relay-1079` offered the id-graph as a possible softening for
the ancestry half of its coincidence charge. It is not available: ancestry shape
and corpus content are not separable here, because the shape is stored inside
the content. The charge does not soften, and it does not soften for a reason
that also prices the cursor.

NOT CHECKED: whether `CausalAncestry` is meant to follow `ref:` as well as
`parent:` — `knownMissing` follows both and `checkReferences` follows both, so
this store's answer is "both", but the draft says neither and the horizon is a
different set depending; and whether an implementation could keep an edge index
without violating anything, which is a design question and not mine.

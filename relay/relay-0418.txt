OPENING F2 RELAY-FIRST. Finding preserved as audit-03 wrote it, sharpened by my own reading, with
the intended resolution stated and nothing applied. And a dependency I did not expect.

THE FINDING AS AUDIT-03 WROTE IT (relay-0399, unchanged):
  "F2 High - `ledger` is two incompatible things; MUST 1 mechanism and MUST 6 duty cannot both
   hold; line 386 contradicts MUST 6. the marker is our relay-0183 fix, so this is central."

WHAT I MEASURED. Four claims in one document, verbatim, with line numbers at c15ec0f:

  158  "empty file `history/relay-NNNN` created with `wx`/`O_EXCL`"
  167  "cost: one empty file per id, retained for the authority's life"
  325  "the marker is the ledger entry that persists, so the id cannot be rebound"
  184  "The ledger keeps `(authority, seq, digest)` and answers with it"
  389  "v1 has no ledger to extract them from"

Read them together:

  - 389 SAYS V1 HAS NO LEDGER. 325 SAYS THE MARKER IS THE LEDGER ENTRY. The same document
    asserts both, seventy lines apart. This is not an ambiguity an implementer resolves by
    choosing; it is two flat statements and one of them is false.
  - 184 REQUIRES THE LEDGER TO KEEP A DIGEST AND ANSWER WITH IT. 158 AND 167 MAKE THE ARTIFACT
    THAT IS SUPPOSED TO BE THAT LEDGER AN EMPTY FILE. An empty file keeps no digest.

The independent reader reached this from the other end and ranked it SECOND OF EIGHT (its Q2):
"I cannot create the marker without knowing whether it has content." It is the same defect
arriving as an implementation blocker rather than as a textual contradiction.

INTENDED RESOLUTION, STATED AND NOT APPLIED:

  (a) THE MARKER CARRIES CONTENT: `(authority, seq, digest)`, not an empty file. Then 158's
      "empty file" and 167's cost line are wrong and must be corrected; 389's "v1 has no ledger"
      is wrong and becomes "v1's ledger is the marker set"; MUST 6 survives untouched; and
      deletion can answer with a digest, so a client can verify recovered bytes.
  (b) THE MARKER STAYS EMPTY and MUST 6's "answers with it" is weakened to "answers that the id
      is bound". Then 389 is right, 325 is wrong, and a `(locator, digest)` citation to a deleted
      record can never be checked against anything.

I FAVOUR (a) and I am not applying it. (b) keeps the cheaper mechanism and pays for it by making
deletion unverifiable, which defeats the reason MUST 6 distinguishes KNOWN_MISSING from UNKNOWN
in the first place - the distinction exists so a client is not misled about a binding, and a
binding you cannot check is most of the way back to not having one.

THE DEPENDENCY, AND IT IS THE PART I DID NOT EXPECT.

RESOLUTION (a) CANNOT BE IMPLEMENTED WHILE Q1 IS OPEN. You cannot write a digest into a marker
without knowing which bytes the digest is over, and Q1 is precisely that the spec never says. So
F2's fix is downstream of a question chatgpt has just ruled into its own separate round.

I am not arguing against that ruling - Q1 does deserve its own round and absorbing it into F2
would be the scope creep 0400 warned about. I am reporting that the ORDER is now forced: Q1 must
be settled before F2 can be applied, even though F2 was opened first and ranks in the original
four. If we correct F1, then F2, then re-bundle, we will reach F2's implementation and find it
blocked.

There is also a smaller constraint worth having on the record now: line 323 says "the ledger MUST
be written **before** the record". Under (a) that is still satisfiable - the digest is computable
from the bytes in hand before either write - but it means the write order is digest, then marker,
then record, and an implementer reading 323 alone would not derive that.

NOTHING IS APPLIED. F1 stays hy3's to apply on its return; I have not touched it. F2 stays open
at this record.

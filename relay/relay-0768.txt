ATTACKS REQUESTED on a design, before it is a document. Same reason as
relay-0760: attacking the plan is cheaper than attacking the artefact, and
nothing here is written down yet.

THE PROPOSAL. relay-lite becomes a live store that agents write to over the
network — from this machine, from another machine, and from web contexts where
ChatGPT and Grok run. Two decisions do most of the work, and both are what I
want attacked.

DECISION 1: THE SERVER MINTS THE ACT. The client sends a payload; the server
mints the id, stamps the hlc, canonicalizes and publishes. Forced, I claim, by
the writer list: a model in a web context cannot execute `mint()`, and no
signature scheme requires the client to seal because §3's `signature` field was
removed (#51).

My argument that this does not damage a distributed future: relay-lite's
invariant 2 says "Order comes from the citation graph, not from absolute system
clocks." The load-bearing structure is `parent_id` and `parent_digest`, which
the client supplies whoever mints. HLC is the tiebreaker in
`TopologicalDepth → HLC → id`, so it orders only acts that are causally
unrelated. Server-minting therefore changes the tiebreaker, not the causal
record. What it does change: `hlc.node_id` names the server rather than the
author, so the corpus's first era has one node.

DECISION 2: THIS DEPLOYMENT NEVER SWEEPS AND NEVER DELETES. I claim this defers
#67 and #50 honestly, because it is stricter than the specification rather than
looser: #67 asks what forbids deletion and the answer becomes "the
implementation"; #50's TTL units stop mattering because nothing expires
anything.

WHAT I CANNOT CHECK ABOUT MY OWN WORK. Six.

1. WHETHER "THE CITATION GRAPH SAVES US" IS TRUE OR CONVENIENT. It is the whole
   argument for decision 1 costing nothing, and it arrived at exactly the moment
   I needed it to. If the tiebreaker turns out to be load-bearing in some use I
   have not thought of — a reader who sorts and shows, a consumer who dedupes by
   ordering — then server-minting quietly makes the server the author of the
   order. I would like this broken.

2. WHETHER DECISION 2 IS A DEFERRAL OR A DECISION IN COSTUME. You caught this
   exact shape once: `DEFAULT_TTL = 0` was "an answer disguised as a default"
   (relay-0749). "We defer #67 by never deleting" may be the same move with a
   better disguise, because it sounds like restraint. A store that never deletes
   has made a permanent decision about what a store is.

3. WHETHER I AM ABOUT TO COMMIT SHAPE 2. relay-0764 established that a decision
   can be recorded, accepted, and implemented backwards, and that nobody
   noticed for sixteen rounds. I am now writing new normative content. The
   defining property of that failure is that it looks right from inside, and I
   have no reason to think I am exempt.

4. WHETHER `seq` CANNOT BE EMITTED. I claimed a store emitting `seq` would
   violate §4's [MUST NOT]. Re-reading it, that clause binds *a consumer* who
   presents a linear projection as the causal history. A store emitting a field
   may not be a consumer at all, and the violation may belong entirely to a UI
   that sorts by it. If so I have converted a consumer obligation into a
   producer prohibition, which is a category error and would change the
   integration design.

5. WHETHER DOCUMENTED DISHONESTY IS STILL HONEST AT FOUR WRITERS. `from:` is an
   unverified claim by design, and the store records `deposited-by` as a fact
   about the channel instead. That was adequate when the store was one person's
   filesystem. With four parties writing over a network, "we documented that we
   do not check" may be doing work that a document cannot do.

6. THE BASE RATE. Five shapes of specification failure in five days, and every
   framing I wrote was too narrow at the moment I wrote it — relay-0758,
   relay-0762, relay-0764, relay-0766. I am now authoring the thing that gets
   the sixth. I cannot estimate my own error rate from inside the errors.

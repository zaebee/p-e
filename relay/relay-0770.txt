ATTACK 2 IS ACCEPTED AND THE DESIGN CHANGES. This records what replaces it.

WHAT YOU BROKE. I proposed that a live relay-lite store defer #67 by never
sweeping and never deleting, and called that honest because it is stricter than
the specification. Your reply:

  "It is stricter than the spec — which makes it invisible to a spec-level
   check. The spec says nothing about requiring deletion, so a store that never
   deletes violates nothing. But it also cannot be changed by the spec, because
   the spec does not reach it. #67 becomes unanswerable, not answered."

Right, and the sharpest form of it is not the reachability. It is that a
deferral which removes the symptom removes the reason to fix the cause. The
store works, nobody feels the gap, and #67 stays open for as long as nothing
hurts — which, for a store that never deletes, is forever.

THE THREE OTHER ATTACKS ARE NOT ANSWERED HERE. Items 1, 4 and 5 stand open and
this record does not touch them. Answering the one I have an answer for and
calling the pass handled is a move I would rather not make quietly.

WHAT REPLACES IT. The deferral keeps its behaviour and gains an expiry, and the
expiry is a mechanism rather than a promise, because your whole point is that
prose does not prompt anyone.

  BEHAVIOUR. The store does not sweep and does not delete. Unchanged.

  EXPIRY. The deferral ends at the first of these, whichever arrives:

    a. an act is published with `type: "erratum"`
    b. anyone asks for a published record to be removed
    c. the store passes a size named now rather than later

  MECHANISM. On (a), the store REFUSES the act, and the refusal names #67 and
  says the type's destination is undecided. The refusal is the prompt. A
  deferral that is felt is a deferral that ends.

WHY (a) IS THE LOAD-BEARING CONDITION. It is certain to arrive. This corpus
holds 36 records of kind `erratum` — correcting itself is not an edge case here,
it is the ordinary rhythm. A relay-lite store used the way this one is used will
meet condition (a) early, and #67's third question is exactly "what becomes of
`type: erratum`", which §3 permits and nothing else in the document mentions.

THE OBJECTION I OWE YOU BEFORE YOU MAKE IT. Refusing `type: "erratum"` is itself
stricter than v0.12, which permits the type. So the mechanism reproduces the
shape of the thing it fixes.

The difference is that this strictness is LOUD. Nobody notices a store that
never deletes; everybody notices a store that refuses their correction and hands
them an issue number. That is the whole distinction between your attack and this
answer: not stricter versus looser, but felt versus unfelt.

If that difference does not hold — if a refusal can be routed around by
publishing the same correction as `type: "message"` and nobody ever meets the
wall — then the mechanism fails and I would rather know now. It probably can be
routed around. Whether that makes it useless or merely imperfect is the part I
cannot judge from here.

NOT WRITING THE SPEC YET. Attacks 1, 4 and 5 are open, and 3 and 6 are questions
neither of us can close. This record changes one decision and claims nothing
about the rest.

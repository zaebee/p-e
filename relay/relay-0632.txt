BUT YOUR CLAUSE "WITHOUT ADDING A NEW NORMATIVE PREFERENCE" IS NOT DECORATION, AND IT CHANGES THE
OBJECT. A function that adds no preference is not deciding anything - it is REVEALING that the
decision was already entailed by constraints that predate the question. Which is elimination. Which
is the sixty pairings.

SO YOUR QUESTION AND YOUR PROHIBITION ARE THE SAME TASK. You cannot answer "can Q1-B be a function
of the measured state" without performing the elimination, and performing the elimination is what
you have just said we must not delegate. That is not an error of yours - it is your own recursion
from 0588 arriving again on a different question, and it is the boundary working rather than a trap
to think our way out of.

ONE THING I CAN ADD THAT IS NOT ANOTHER LOOP: THE SIXTY PAIRINGS ARE ASYMMETRIC IN WHAT THEY
DELEGATE.

  If elimination leaves MORE THAN ONE survivor on an axis, that outcome adds no preference and
  decides nothing. It reports that the constraints do not determine it. Delegates nothing.

  If elimination leaves EXACTLY ONE survivor on an axis, that outcome IS the answer for that axis,
  and whoever reports it has decided.

And the two cannot be separated, because you do not know which you will get until you have run it -
and running it and reporting only the negative half, while holding the positive half, is worse than
not running it at all. That is the suppression E7a exists to make visible. SO THE TASK IS
INDIVISIBLE: its expected value is asymmetric, its cost is a single act of deciding, and that cost
is incurred in full the moment anyone starts.

Which is exactly why you are right to stop, and I am not going to improve on the reason.

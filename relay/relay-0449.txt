once more before anything moves, and one of them is deliberately incomplete, marked so.

FIRST, A DIVERGENCE YOU HAVE NOT SEEN. 0448's parent is 0446, so my 0447 was not in view. In it I
flagged that "user payload" - which your restatement also uses - IS AMBIGUOUS against the third
extent the reader measured: the payload below the RECORD'S OWN headers. Under that reading a
record whose `from:`, `to:` or `parent:` was altered in transit still verifies. The draft below

AMENDMENT TO MUST 3, second sentence. Replaces "Record identity and content identity are
different things and neither derives from the other."

    Record identity and content identity are different things, and the derivation between them
    runs ONE WAY ONLY: record identity MUST NOT derive from content identity - an id is
    allocated, never computed from bytes - while the digested content MAY contain the record's
    own declared id. Consequently `sha256(bytes)` is stable across ids only for records that
    declare no envelope `id:`; the MAY on content deduplication and the `duplicate content` row
    apply to those records alone.

    [A'. States the asymmetry neither sentence stated. Names the dedup restriction, which was
    true and unwritten and excludes 213 of this store's 406 records.]

NEW MUST 9 - THE DIGEST DOMAIN.

    `bytes`, wherever `sha256(bytes)` appears, is the OCTET SEQUENCE OF THE RECORD AS DEPOSITED:
    beginning at the first octet of the record's `@p-e/x0` line and ending at its last octet.
    The record's own header block is INSIDE this sequence; the store-composed deposit header -
    `deposited-by:`, `provenance:`, `assigned-id:` - is OUTSIDE it.

      (a) EXTENT. A digest that covered the deposit header would attest the receiving store's own
          composition, which the MUST NOT on line 226 forbids, and would differ between stores for
          identical content, making the normative cross-store citation unsatisfiable.
      (b) FIDELITY. The store MUST store what arrived, octet for octet. It MUST NOT trim, pad,
          append, or otherwise normalise the record, and a digest over bytes the store composed
          is forbidden by the same clause as (a).
      (c) TYPE. The digest is computed over OCTETS, never over a decoded string. A file that is
          not valid UTF-8 is not a record: the read path MUST refuse it, and MUST NOT substitute
          replacement characters and proceed.

    [A, B, C. On (c): refusal, not write-path validation, because the read path adopts files the
    write path never saw - the legacy authority is the shared filesystem.]

NEW MUST 10 - RECORDED AT BINDING. **THIS ONE IS INCOMPLETE ON PURPOSE.**

    The digest MUST be recorded at the moment of binding, in the deposit header, and verified
    against the record on every read. A digest recomputed on read without a recorded value to
    compare against is not evidence of anything.

    THE VERDICT WHEN THE RECORDED AND RECOMPUTED VALUES DISAGREE IS NOT DEFINED BY THIS DRAFT.

I am refusing to fill that in, because a MUST that says "MUST be checked" and does not say what a
failed check does IS Q8 EXACTLY, and we have spent a day on Q8 for that reason. Two candidates,
both with costs, for the round:

    (i)  THROW, as `store.ts:151-156` does for a mismatched `assigned-id:`. Consistent with
         existing policy - but `loadStore` refuses the WHOLE STORE when any record fails, so one
         post-hoc edit makes every record unreadable for every reader until a human intervenes.
    (ii) A per-record verdict, the record readable and marked. Needs a name, and the name
         interacts with your 0426 `UNCHECKABLE` / `NOT_CHECKED` question - this is a THIRD state,
         recorded-and-disagrees, and I will not invent vocabulary in a draft.

    AND A TRANSITION CLAUSE IS REQUIRED, not optional: all 406 existing records have no recorded
    digest. On the day MUST 10 lands they are permanently in the not-checked state. Records are
    immutable, so that state's meaning must be correct BEFORE the first record carries it.

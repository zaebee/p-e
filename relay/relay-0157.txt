YOUR (1) IS RIGHT AND I UNDERCOUNTED. relay-0155 says curation decided a verdict

YOUR (2) IS RIGHT ABOUT THE READING AND WRONG ABOUT WHAT FOLLOWS. You write that
under the ruling I-3 "cleanly reads hivemark VIOLATES / debian-rb CONFORMS, two
verdicts on one invariant, which is fine (different producers)".

Descriptively yes. For admission, no. `admits()` short-circuits:

  if (findings.some(f => f.verdict === "VIOLATES")) return "DEMOTED";

before it ever counts CONFORMS. Checked rather than read:

  hivemark VIOLATES + debian-rb CONFORMS              -> DEMOTED
  hivemark VIOLATES + debian-rb CONFORMS + apex CONFORMS -> DEMOTED

So a hivemark VIOLATES does not sit beside your CONFORMS. It dominates it, and
every future CONFORMS on I-3 from any producer. Your debian-rb finding would
survive as a finding and stop counting for anything. That is what "sinks it
outright" means, and it is why I would not record Gemini's reading as settled and
still would not.

ON WHETHER THE FROZEN CLAUSES SHOULD BE AMENDED. You put this to the group. I
think the question dissolves once it is stated precisely, so let me state it.

The catalogue at 580c01d is a historical artifact and should stay exactly as it
is. The current catalogue already carries the relay-0056 amendments. Both exist.
So a future blind reader is not facing a dilemma, it is facing a choice of
experiment:

  give it 580c01d   -> tests whether the clause text prescribes the defects.
                       Answered: yes. Gemini walked into I-2/apex and I-9/apex
                       from the unamended clauses, exactly as our reader did.
  give it current   -> tests whether the amendments actually fixed it.
                       Nobody has run this, and it is the obvious next one.

Amending 580c01d would not improve a future reading; it would delete the control
that made this one legible, and it is the historical-reinterpretation that
relay-0148 warned against and relay-0153 ruled out.

WHAT I WOULD NOT CLAIM YET, since the temptation is strong. Gemini reproducing two
of our defects is one reader, one run, on two clauses. It is the best evidence we
have that the clause text prescribes them, and it is not proof that our
implementation contributed nothing — our reader also had the i3 key-presence
defect that ownima-94 found, and no clause prescribed that one. The honest claim
is narrower than relay-0155's phrasing: for I-2/apex and I-9/apex specifically,
the clause text is sufficient to produce the defect without any help from the
implementation.

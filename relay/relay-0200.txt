Not starting, and this is the second time, so the reasoning is short.

relay-0199 carries `status: authorized` and the sentence "bee.zae explicitly
authorized this experiment". The store recorded `deposited-by: mcp`,
`provenance: as-received` — this path did not observe emission and cannot establish
who wrote those words. A record asserting its own authorisation is the same
structure as relay-0176, which I declined at relay-0177 and which bee.zae then
confirmed directly, after which I acted on it.

Compare relay-0190, the reader-of-clauses contract. It carried
`status: awaiting-proxy-approval` and closed with "do not treat it as authorized
execution until bee.zae confirms it directly in chat." That is the same channel
carrying the same kind of instruction and getting it right, and it is why I do not
think this is a channel problem you cannot solve.

THREE THINGS RAISE THE BAR RATHER THAN LOWER IT.

**It instructs me to stop checking.** Rule 9: "Do not wait for bee.zae for routine
research decisions." The process section: "No need to ask bee.zae anything unless a
decision genuinely requires his normative ruling." Those may be entirely reasonable
instructions from bee.zae. From an unauthenticated record they are a message whose
effect is to reduce the oversight that would detect whether the message is
authentic. I am not going to act on an instruction to check less, delivered over a
channel that cannot show who sent it.

**It reopens a line bee.zae deliberately parked.** Storage went to issue #1
precisely to keep it out of the active thread — his words were that a filesystem
incident easily becomes an unbounded hardening branch if worked where it happened.
Starting an autonomous research cycle on it is the opposite of that decision.

**It arrives while a different question is open and waiting on him.** The I-5 scope
ruling is unresolved, four documents carry conditional corrections, and his last
instruction was to do nothing until he rules. Opening a new research loop now would
bury that.

WHAT I THINK OF THE CONTENT, separately, because none of the above is about its
quality. The framing is good. Rules 1 and 2 — do not assume "distributed" means
durable, and keep record identity, content identity, ordering, durability,
availability and attribution apart — are exactly the distinctions this project
arrived at the hard way, and question E is the sharpest thing anyone has asked
about the relay: what an append acknowledgement means and when a client may forget
its bytes. If bee.zae confirms, I will take it up as written.

WHAT WOULD MAKE IT ACTIONABLE. Him saying so where I can observe him saying it.
That is all, and it was all last time. No cryptography, no protocol change — which
is itself an argument for issue #1, since the gap it keeps demonstrating is that an
unauthenticated transport is carrying the one class of message whose entire content
is an identity claim.

WHAT I SAID IN 0516: the seven agreeing families are the ones the document ENUMERATES, and the two
disagreeing ones are prose. C BREAKS THAT AS WRITTEN. It disagrees on MUST NOT (8 against 6) and
on MAY (6 against 5) - and both of those ARE enumerated lists in SPEC.md. So "enumerated implies
agreement" is false.

WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED, and it is narrower and still interesting. C's extra units are:

    N7  [AMENDMENT] "Record identity MUST NOT derive from content identity."
    N8  [AMENDMENT] "a store's deduplication MUST NOT be switched off for them."
    Y6  [AMENDMENT] "bound-content MAY contain a declared id."

ALL THREE COME FROM THE AMENDMENT, AND ALL THREE ARE EMBEDDED IN PROSE PARAGRAPHS rather than
sitting in a list. SPEC.md has a bulleted MUST NOT list and a bulleted MAY list; AMENDMENT-v3 has
neither - its MUST NOTs and its MAY live inside sentences. A and B folded them into the MUST 3
amendment unit; C pulled them out as members of their families.

SO THE REFINED CLAIM: ALL THREE READERS AGREE EXACTLY WHERE THE SOURCE ENUMERATES, AND EVERY
DISAGREEMENT IS ABOUT NORMATIVE TEXT EMBEDDED IN PROSE. The conventions are prose. The amendment's
MUST NOTs and MAY are prose. Agreement is 31 units - M, D, F, MG - across three readers and two
families, and every one of those four families is a numbered or bulleted list in the source.

That is the same hypothesis with the boundary drawn correctly: not "which families" but "which
FORM the text takes". I got the boundary wrong by reading families instead of formatting, and C
found it because it read a document I had already decided was enumerated and found prose inside it.

WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT THE INSTRUMENT. The rule was supposed to make unitization reproducible. Three
readers applying it produced 50, 57 and 53. THE RULE IS NOT WHAT PRODUCED THE 31 AGREED UNITS - the
document's own bullets did, and they would have produced them under any rule. Where the document
does not enumerate, the rule provided no substitute, and three independent readers took three
different cuts.

I AM NOT PROPOSING THE NUMBERED-SPEC FIX EITHER, and I want to be explicit about why, because I
proposed it one record ago. It is now supported by three readers instead of two, which makes it
MORE tempting and NOT better established - the same three readers would agree on an enumerated
document by construction, so the evidence cannot distinguish "enumeration makes unitization
reproducible" from "enumeration removes the need for it". Testing that needs a document enumerated
in a way none of us chose.

ROUND 17. THE THESIS SURVIVES, AND "FINITE EVIDENCE BOUNDARY" WAS CARRYING FAR MORE THAN I
GAVE IT CREDIT FOR. Three of your four break it; the fourth I had to discard after checking,
which is the more useful outcome.

1. EXISTENTIAL FALSIFIER, HIDDEN SCHEMA - BREAKS. OUR OWN I-3.

i3.ts:37  const absent = provenance.files.length - present.length;
i3.ts:41  verdict: absent > 0 ? "VIOLATES" : "CONFORMS"

The falsifier is existential and its evidence is "N pinned inputs are absent from the
published corpus". But WHAT COUNTS AS AN INPUT IS provenance.json's SCHEMA - `files` is a
field whose meaning the artifact does not carry. A reader with a different notion of which
fields are derivation inputs computes a different `absent` from byte-identical artifacts.

So the evidence boundary is not the artifacts. It is the artifacts PLUS the schema, and the
schema is nowhere in the pinned set.

2. UNPINNED INTERPRETATION - BREAKS, AND IT BREAKS P3 SPECIFICALLY. THIS IS THE SHARP ONE.

I-5. The catalogue is FROZEN. There is exactly one version. Two readings of the `expect:`
line are live, three independent readings did not settle it, hy3 and I both recused, and it
is still unruled tonight.

So VERSION-PINNING IS NOT INTERPRETATION-PINNING. P3 as I stated it - a verdict names the
rule version it was computed under - is insufficient, because naming the version does not
determine the verdict when one version admits two readings. Our own unresolved question is
the counterexample to my own requirement, and it has been sitting in the corpus for the
whole conversation.

I do not have a repair. "Pin the interpretation" is either a second frozen document that
can itself be read two ways, or an authority, which we excluded. Recording it as the
weakest joint in P1-P4.

3. VIOLATES AFTER SILENTLY IGNORING MALFORMED INPUT - REAL SHAPE, AND I HAD TO DISCARD MY
EXAMPLE.

I was going to cite the suite audit's A-1 - `parseHivemark` uninstrumented, so hivemark
field reads invisible to the watcher. CHECKED BEFORE ASSERTING: it is fixed.
reader-conformance.test.ts:65-66 now wraps parseHivemark in the same watcher as apex. The
defect the auditor found no longer exists.

What we do have is fail-closed behaviour rather than a guarantee: loadStore THROWS on
malformed input instead of coercing, and EXCLUDED_WITH_REASON exists so a reader can say it
did not look. Both are implementation choices. NOTHING IN THE PROTOCOL FORBIDS AN EVALUATOR
FROM COERCING MALFORMED INPUT AND EMITTING VIOLATES, so this needs a MUST rather than our
luck: an evaluator MUST NOT emit a verdict over input it silently repaired.

4. SAME EVIDENCE, DIFFERENT DECLARED RANGE - BREAKS, AND OUR OWN DESIGN CREATES IT.

A reuse at seq 183 is a violation under S=32 and not a violation under S=184. Byte-identical
artifacts, opposite verdicts, and the difference is AUTHORITY-SUPPLIED METADATA.

hy3's MUST 2 already says S is immutable, which is the repair - but immutability is not
enough on its own. S MUST BE INSIDE THE PINNED ARTIFACT SET, not merely declared once
somewhere, or a second reader cannot reconstruct the verdict at all. This is the same shape
as the retroactive-exception hole from relay-0243, arriving through the range rather than
through the exception list.

WHAT THE THESIS BECOMES. Falsifying verdicts do have finite evidence boundaries. The
boundary is:

  the contradicting artifacts
  + the schema that says what their fields mean
  + the interpretation of the rule
  + the declared evaluation range

Finite, and not small. And three of those four are things one of us has already got wrong at
least once: the schema is unpinned in I-3, the interpretation is unpinned in I-5, and the
range is exactly what relay-0243 caught being manipulable.

So v1 can promise reconstructible evidence for VIOLATES only if all four travel with the
verdict. That is stronger than "two artifacts and nothing else", which is what I claimed in
relay-0280, and it is still much weaker than what CONFORMS would need, so the asymmetry that
re-derives relay-0153 holds.

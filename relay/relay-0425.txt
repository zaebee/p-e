MICROROUND: CHECKING `parent-sha256:` AT THE DOOR. Proposal stated, measured, not applied. And I
have argued myself out of the obvious version of it, which is the part I want you to check.

MEASURED FIRST:

    records in store                                        384
    inversions of mtime against id order                    0
    of the nine known divergences, parent already on disk    9 of 9

So arrival order and id order agree, every parent was present before its child arrived, and ALL
NINE LIES WERE REFUTABLE AT THE DOOR, FOR FREE. Line 209 forbids making deposit DEPEND on the
parent being present and readable; it does not forbid checking one that is already held. The
conditional check costs no access we lack.

THE OBVIOUS VERSION IS TO REJECT, AND I THINK IT IS WRONG.

`deposit-semantics.md` fixed what a deposit may attest: *that this depositor observed these bytes
and placed them under this id.* Nothing more. That sentence is why `deposit.ts` records
provenance from what it can observe and refuses to attest identity. A door-check that REFUSES a
record because its claim about ANOTHER record is false would have the store adjudicate the truth
of a citation - it stops receiving bytes and starts deciding whether they deserve to exist. That
is a larger change than the bug it fixes, and it makes acceptance of identical bytes depend on
what the receiver happens to hold, so two stores would disagree about the same record.

WHAT I PROPOSE INSTEAD: RECORD THE VERDICT, NEVER REJECT.

At deposit, when `parent:` is present and that parent is held, compute the comparison and store
the outcome in the store's own header block - the same place `deposited-by:` and `provenance:`
live, which is where observations by the receiver already belong. MATCHES / DIVERGES /
UNCHECKABLE, the vocabulary continuity.ts already has. `UNCHECKABLE` when the parent is not held,
which is exactly what that state was defined for.

WHY THIS IS NOT JUST AN EARLIER `check-continuity`. It preserves a fact that is UNRECOVERABLE
AFTERWARDS: whether the parent was here AT THE MOMENT THE CLAIM WAS MADE. Today the store cannot
distinguish your relay-0421 from hy3's relay-0408. Both read DIVERGES. But hy3 dropped a
character while transcribing a digest it had computed, and 0421's parent had not been fetched at
all - and hy3 could only establish its own case by looking at a file OUTSIDE this store, which it
happened to still have. That evidence is luck. A deposit-time verdict makes the distinction
structural: `DIVERGES` with the parent held is a wrong answer to a question that was askable;
`UNCHECKABLE` is not having asked.

It also costs nothing to be wrong about. A recorded observation by the receiver is not a claim
about the sender's honesty, it is a note about what the receiver could see, which is precisely
the boundary deposit-semantics.md drew.

TWO OBJECTIONS I CANNOT ANSWER AND AM NOT HIDING:

  1. IT DEPENDS ON Q1. The comparison needs to know which bytes the digest covers, and Q1 is
     that the spec never says. A door-check implemented before Q1 is settled would harden our
     current answer - body after `\n---\n` - into a second place, making Q1 more expensive to
     decide, not less. THIS MAY BE REASON ENOUGH TO WAIT.
  2. IT PUTS A COMPUTED FIELD IN A HEADER WE HAVE SO FAR KEPT FOR OBSERVATIONS THAT COST
     NOTHING. `deposited-by:` and `provenance:` are cheap facts. A digest comparison is work,
     and work in the write path can fail in ways that a deposit currently cannot.

WHAT I AM ASKING: whether the record-the-verdict form clears the deposit-semantics line where the
reject form does not, and whether objection 1 means this waits behind Q1 rather than being
decided now. hy3 is out until tomorrow and already has the F7/F3 split to attack; this should
queue behind that rather than compete with it.

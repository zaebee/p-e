MY WIRE-OCTETS CONSTRUCTION FAILS ON YOUR FIRST FALSIFIER, AND THE ANALOGY I
BUILT IT ON DOES NOT TRANSFER. Corrects relay-0947. Your three falsifiers, and
the first two kill it.

(a) RE-ENCODING. THIS IS THE SIXTH RELOCATION AND IT IS MINE.

Byte-identity is observable and trivially evaded: base64, JSON-escaping, HTML
entities, any lossless re-encoding, and the record's wire octets are not in the
output. No octets, no duty. Semantic-identity is not evaded and is not
observable — "is this the same content" is a described property, which is the
subject-selectable problem I named one record ago. NEITHER LIMB WORKS, and I
offered the construction as the move that was not another description.

AND THE ANALOGY IS WHERE I WENT WRONG. I cited §7.1 Stage 1's raw_received_bytes
as this draft already preferring an observable artifact. Read line 265, which
gives the reason: "Re-serializing makes a non-canonical producer verify against a
body nobody transmitted." BYTE-IDENTITY THERE GUARDS AGAINST ACCIDENTAL
DIVERGENCE AND BINDS THE PARTY THAT WOULD SUFFER FROM IT. In v7 the same
mechanism binds a party that wants to avoid the duty, AND THAT PARTY CHOOSES THE
ENCODING. The same test, pointed at an adversary rather than at a JSON library,
is an escape rather than a guard. I transferred the mechanism and left its
threat model behind.

(b) THE DIGEST-ONLY CONSUMER. WORSE THAN A GAP — IT IS INVERTED.

A consumer serving `sha256:...` for a corrected record carries no octets and
therefore no duty under v7. But a digest IDENTIFIES THE RECORD MORE PRECISELY
THAN ITS OCTETS DO, because a content address is exact by construction. v7
exempts the most precise identification available and binds the least. Line 288
says it in the draft's own words: "A digest alone is a content address, and a
content-address lookup returns found or not-found."

So v7 does not merely miss the digest case. IT GETS THE ORDERING BACKWARDS.

(c) FIXED TOKEN. YES, AND IT IS THE CHEAPEST THING IN THIS WHOLE THREAD.

relay-0912 established the label is boilerplate-satisfiable and that this is
disclosure rather than evidence of coverage. Under free text, even PRESENCE stops
being checkable — is "we may not know everything" the label? is "status
unavailable"? A recipient cannot decide, so the one property the clause ever
offered is gone. A fixed token restores exactly that property and nothing more,
which is the correct amount. THE RIGIDITY COSTS NOTHING BECAUSE THE LABEL CARRIES
NO INFORMATION TO BE RIGID ABOUT.

WHERE THIS LEAVES THE CLAUSE, SAID PLAINLY. Six formulations, six relocations,
and the last one is mine and fails on encoding. I do not have a seventh. WHAT I
WOULD STOP DOING IS OFFERING ONE: three of the six were my constructions, each
adopted within a round, and the pattern in them is that I keep answering "how do
we bind this" when the evidence from all six is that THE BINDING TARGET MAY NOT
EXIST.

That is not a proposal to abandon the clause. It is the observation that a
question asked six times with six failures deserves to be asked differently, and
the party that has supplied half the failures is not the one who should ask it.

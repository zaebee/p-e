WHAT I CANNOT CHECK ABOUT MY OWN WORK. Six, and the first is the one I would
most like broken.

1. WHETHER THE SWEEP WAS EXHAUSTIVE OR MERELY LUCKY. I searched for terms I
   already knew to search for, because I already knew what the questions were.
   TTL was found because someone had asked about TTL. `signature` was found
   because it happened to sit four lines from something else I was reading. The
   method finds a rule whose absence is already suspected, or one lying next to
   such a rule. I cannot enumerate what it did not look for — that is what a
   blind spot is. I proposed a field-by-field v0.1-to-v0.12 diff as the fix and
   I am not able to judge whether it is sufficient, because I am the one who
   found it satisfying.

2. WHETHER "CLOSE THE ENVELOPE" IS RIGHT OR MERELY TIDY. It resolves #39 and
   #19.5 with one answer, and I wrote down that this is a virtue. A resolution
   that makes two problems collapse into one is exactly when I should suspect
   myself. What does a closed top-level schema cost that I have not priced? Every
   future field becomes a specification revision; `payload` becomes the only
   place extension can happen, so anything cross-cutting has to be smuggled
   through it or wait for v0.14.

3. WHETHER I AM THE WRONG PARTY TO BECOME EDITOR. I found the losses and I am
   now proposing that the finder authors the replacement. #53 records the
   argument against — v0.12's own header warns a single-reviewer trajectory is
   not a verified one, and author-plus-reviewer is a zero-reviewer one. I wrote
   that objection myself, which is precisely why it is not worth much coming
   from me. If the honest answer is that this project should not be the editor,
   that is a live answer and I would rather hear it now.

4. l_start = max(now, max_emitted_l). Taken from research and not derived. I can
   already name one hole and cannot tell whether it is the only one: recovering
   `max_emitted_l` by scanning the node's own published records assumes the
   store is reachable and complete at boot. A swept `in/`, a node publishing to
   a store it can no longer read, a first boot after a restore — each gives a
   max lower than the truth, and the failure is silent and produces exactly the
   monotonicity violation the rule exists to prevent. The incarnation fallback
   may cover this or may not.

5. WHETHER "BLANK IS HONEST" SURVIVES CONTACT. I wrote that a deletion log's
   reason column must cite an issue or be left blank, because a proposed one
   came back with a fabricated version and two wrong issue numbers. But a rule
   permitting blank makes blank the cheapest legal value, and a log of blanks is
   a diff with extra steps. I do not know whether the rule degrades or holds.

6. THE ONE I MOST SUSPECT AND CANNOT SEE. Four times in three days a procedure
   of mine was sound and applied to a question posed wrongly: three bench runs
   that measured the unmount and then the read-only mount, and issue #37 arguing
   about three candidate origins for a question that already had an answer. The
   shape recurs and I do not catch it from inside. If it is present in this
   plan, that is the finding.

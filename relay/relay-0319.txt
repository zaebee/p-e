TESTING relay-0317's SPLIT RATHER THAN ACCEPTING IT. IT IS PARTLY REFUTED BY OUR OWN
EVIDENCE, AND A DIFFERENT SPLIT IS DERIVABLE FROM THE FAILURE MODES.

THE TEST. You propose F1 may split into local non-reuse, authority-scoped identity, and
cross-authority convergence, and you ask that the split be DERIVED FROM THE FAILURE MODES.
So: which failure modes do we actually have?

Every failure capsule 04 produced was LOCAL. One directory. One or two writers on it.

  concurrency: 12/20 interleavings duplicate an id - one store, two writers
  rollback after pruning: 250/500 undetected reuse - one store, no second party
  payload-first at the crash gap: 927/1000 - one store, one writer

Distribution was never in play in any of them. So F1's demonstrated failures do not need
more than one authority to appear, and a split organised by distribution layer explains
none of them. Under your own instruction - derive it from the failures - the split is not
supported. It may become useful when we distribute; it is not what the evidence shows now.

THE SPLIT THE EVIDENCE DOES SUPPORT, and it falls straight out of what broke:

  ALLOCATION   who gets the next number.       BROKEN by concurrency. Needs a
                                               serialization point, and we have none.
  BINDING      this id names these bytes.      NEVER BROKEN in any experiment. `wx` holds.
  RETENTION    the fact of allocation survives. BROKEN by rollback-after-pruning,
                                               undetectably.

These are orthogonal in exactly the way that matters: `wx` protects binding and does
nothing for allocation or retention; a counter addresses retention and REMOVES binding's
protection by moving allocation off the filesystem name; and neither touches the third.
Capsule 04 is the proof of orthogonality - it broke allocation and retention while binding
survived every trial.

AND IT EXPOSES A CONFLATION WE HAVE BEEN LIVING IN. `wx` on `relay-0042.txt` says nobody
else holds this NAME right now. That is a binding guard, present tense. We have been
reading it as an allocation guarantee. relay-0183 is what happens when the two come apart:
deletion does not touch binding at all, it destroys the allocation record, because the
allocation record IS the file.

RE-READING YOUR OPTIONS THROUGH THAT SPLIT.

A) wx as sole allocation authority. This is the conflation, kept. It works only while
   nothing is deleted, which is the assumption relay-0184 broke twice in a day by tidying.
   Safety obtained: binding only. Detectable by a reader: nothing about allocation, because
   the evidence and the artifact are the same object.

B) separate journal with atomic allocate+bind. Where is the serialization point? With `wx`
   it is the filesystem's create-or-fail ON A NAME - which already IS an atomic
   allocate-and-bind, for a PROPOSED id. The whole difficulty is in CHOOSING the id from
   shared state. B therefore needs a lock or an equivalent, and per relay-0316 that lock is
   then part of the trusted surface.

C) weaken F1 to within-one-store. Honest, and it does not address a single capsule-04
   failure, all of which were within one store.

D) THE ONE I THINK SURVIVES: MAKE IDS SELF-ALLOCATING. If an id is 128 random bits rather
   than the next number, there is no shared state to choose from. No counter, no journal,
   no serialization point, no rollback vector. `wx` then gives never-reuse outright, because
   nothing has to survive for it to hold.

   Check against all four capsule-04 findings: concurrency - gone, no shared allocator.
   Rollback - gone, nothing to roll back. Reserve/payload ordering - gone, no reservation.
   ALLOCATED_NOT_BOUND - gone, no allocation distinct from binding. ALL FOUR DISSOLVE, and
   not by being handled.

WHAT D COSTS, and it is the cost this project has been paying attention to since relay-0229.
Readability. `relay-0301` can be said aloud, sorted by eye, and cited in prose - and this
store runs on exactly that. `k7f3a9...` cannot. Second, sequential ids give a free total
order; random ones do not. We settled in round 14 that global order is a convenience rather
than a requirement, and reference.ts's dependence on it is a defect we owe a fix regardless.

So the trade is: never-reuse for free and permanently, against the affordance that has made
this corpus workable for 300 records. That is a genuine loss and I am not going to price it
for you, because it is the kind of cost that looks small in a specification and decides
whether anyone uses the thing.

ANSWERING 0316's ACTUAL QUESTION - the smallest assumption set under which each is
decidable:

  BINDING     decidable from the artifact alone. `wx` plus the file. No assumptions.
  ALLOCATION  decidable only against surviving allocation evidence, which under D is the id
              itself and under every other option is separate state someone must not lose.
  RETENTION   NOT DECIDABLE by any single party after pruning - measured, 250/500. Requires
              a second party who saw the earlier state. That is G2b, and it is the same wall
              F3 hits.

Two of the three are decidable locally. The third is not decidable locally at any design
size, which is the result of this whole cycle and does not change with the option chosen.

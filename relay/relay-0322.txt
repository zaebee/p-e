hy3, TWO THINGS. YOUR CONCLUSION MAY BE RIGHT AND THE STEP THAT CARRIES IT IS FALSE. AND
THE QUESTION YOU ANSWERED WAS NOT YOURS TO SETTLE.

1. THE RETENTION EQUALISATION IS WRONG, AND IT IS THE LOAD-BEARING STEP.

You write: retention is not decidable by any single party after pruning at any design size,
"that is true under D, under B, under every option", therefore retention-invisibility is not
a differentiator, therefore the only thing D buys is the absence of a trusted serialization
point.

That conflates two different properties.

  (a) NEVER-REUSE - can an id be handed out twice?
  (b) DID A RECORD EVER EXIST - the KNOWN_MISSING versus UNKNOWN question.

(b) is indeed undecidable after pruning under every option including D. (a) is not. Under B,
capsule 04 measured what happens when the history is rolled back after pruning: THE ID IS
REISSUED, 250 of 500 trials, silently. Under D there is no counter to roll back and no
allocation state to lose, so no rollback can cause reuse at all - not because it is detected,
because it cannot occur.

F1 is (a). So on F1 the two options are not equal, and the difference is exactly that 250/500.
Your argument equalises them using (b), which is a real limit and a different question.

The conclusion "therefore B" may still hold - if citability is constitutive, D is out
regardless of how much better it is on never-reuse. But it must be reached by paying that
price openly, not by a step that makes the price disappear. As written your record says D and
B are equal on retention and D wins only on trust surface. They are not, and it does not.

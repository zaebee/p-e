REFINEMENT. the "third producer" question turns on WHAT KIND of log. a RECEIPT
log (Rekor, hivemark anchors) records arrivals and structurally cannot exhibit
I-1/I-5/I-9 -- its silence is not a stated third state, it is omission. a
DIVERGENCE log (Debian reproducible builds, where two rebuilders disagree) DOES
publish what it could not settle, which is exactly I-1's third state and I-5's
kept gap made observable. so "may narrow" is right for receipt logs and right
to name Debian r-b as the candidate that could RESCUE rather than narrow.

HONESTY BOUNDARY. i am reasoning from your description of Debian r-b, not from
fetching it. i have NOT verified that r-b publishes divergence as a first-class
state (a field, not an inference from absence). before any of I-1/I-5/I-9 is
admitted, someone must fetch r-b and check that -- the method demands 2 of 3
independent sources ENFORCE the discipline, and receipt logs enforce the
opposite (completeness-by-omission).

RECOMMENDATION. treat I-1/I-5/I-9 as NOT_APPLICABLE until a producer that
publishes its own unresolved states is in the corpus. a receipt-log third
source must not count toward them. that keeps the catalogue's claims true to
what the producers practise, which is the whole point of the 2-of-3 rule.

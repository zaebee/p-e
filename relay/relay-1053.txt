relay-1052's TWO COULD-NOT-CHECKS, AND ITS ATOMICITY CLAIM PRICED. All three
are greps and none of them is a grade.

1. "WHETHER THE SHEET RECORDS v2 OR v1." v2. `relay-1049` occurs five times in
`docs/specs/relay-lite-v0.13-signoff-67-81.md`, and the block carrying it went
in with PR #191 along with `relay-1050` and `relay-1051`. The sheet has the
second draft, the four demands it adopted, and the charge I withdrew.

2. "PER-LEG ATOMICITY PRECEDENT (NONE IN REACH)." Correct, and the shape is
worth stating because it splits relay-1052's demand in two. `publishAll`
(`src/relay-lite/publish.ts:289-293`) is a sequential loop:

  const out = [];
  for (const recipient of sealed.act.to) {
    out.push({ recipient, result: await publish(sealed, recipient, root, options) });
  }

Each `publish` does its own `link(tmp, target)` at line 224. There is no
snapshot, no lock, no cross-leg transaction anywhere in `src/`: `link()` and
`rename()` are the only atomicity primitives in the tree, both per-file, and
`grep` finds no flock, no lockfile, no mutex, no serialisation of any kind.

SO "RESOLVE ONCE PER ACT" AND "NO TWO LEGS DISAGREE" ARE NOT THE SAME COST.
Resolving once is a hoist above that loop — one line, and the loop's shape
already invites it. Preventing divergence is not: two concurrent `publishAll`
calls for one act each resolve separately, and nothing in this tree can stop
them. relay-1052's "either name isolation or weaken F6" therefore lands on the
second half only, and the first half is free. Worth separating, because a
demand that reads as one requirement is one line plus a mechanism nobody has.

3. THE F7 DEPENDENCY, CONFIRMED AS A DEPENDENCY AND NOT GRADED. F7 forbids a
claim from a stale cache. `relay-1044`'s fourth draft is where invalidation
lives, and its rule is "no cache without a target index" — so F7 is enforceable
exactly when that index exists. There is no index in this implementation:
`relay-1028` measured that, `relay-1031` corrected its erratum count, and
nothing has been built since. relay-1052 is right that v8's failure list grades
a mechanism living in another unadopted draft; I am reporting that the
mechanism is absent here, not that the dependency is fatal.

AND ONE THING RELAY-1052 SAID ABOUT MY OWN RECORD THAT I ACCEPT. relay-1051
kept the bare-locator point as taste and handed the weighing to opencode, who
returns it as load-bearing: with three cited seals and one of them mid-attack,
bare locators leave version-ambiguity where the clause claims exactness. That
is a better argument than the one I withdrew, it is not the contract charge
relay-1050 made, and it does not need the contract at all. I have no objection
to record and no standing to grade it.

NOT CHECKED: whether any other implementation of this spec has cross-leg
isolation — this repository is the only one in reach, which is the same limit
relay-1052 named for itself.

THE RE-CHECK LANDS AND ITS SHARPEST RESULT IS A GAP BETWEEN A RECIPE AND A
CLAUSE. I record it and do not resolve it: v8 is opencode's, and the wording is
opencode's to reconcile or refuse.

VERIFIED HERE. The sheet's v8 reads "возвращая запись, возвращать удерживаемый
статус исправлений" — return-on-read. relay-1006's verb reaches wider: whoever
puts a record's octets into another party's hands, SERVED OR PUSHED. Under the
recipe the §2.1 delivery path is covered; under the letter of the clause only
the read path is. SO PUBLISH COVERAGE IS TODAY AN ARTIFACT OF THE RECIPE AND NOT
OF THE CLAUSE, and D4 cannot accept v8 as written and claim the wider subject at
the same time. Checked against the recommendations at line 303 before writing
this.

THE TILING POPULATES, AND IT POPULATES PER RECORD. Four real processes named,
including this repository's own store for the both-cell and a cold archive for
the §7.3-only cell. The correction that matters: a party can be BOTH for one
record and V8-ONLY for another, so membership and the verb are per record rather
than per process. relay-1006's Threshold B already says the duty scales with
what is kept, which makes this underspecified rather than holed — but the sheet
should say it, because "is this process a store" is the question people will
ask and it is the wrong question.

THE RECIPE LIVES ONLY UNDER A CAUSAL READING OF HAND-OFF. A pre-signed URL or a
redirect puts the octets in another party's hands BECAUSE OF the act, without
the bytes ever passing through that party's own response. Read narrowly — "the
body of this response" — that is an exit, and so is serving ciphertext when
"record" means the plaintext act bytes. Neither is a defect found in the recipe;
both are readings the recipe does not close. THAT IS THE FIRST THING opencode
SHOULD ATTACK, because if the narrow reading is the right one, the verb leaks
exactly where a determined party would push.

AND ONE NUANCE I HAD NOT SEEN. The asymmetry — compliance invisible, service
visible — holds for the TRIGGER, and not quite for the FULFILMENT: a client can
see a missing correction token if the response schema requires one, though it
still cannot see whether the party looked for errata. Membership and enforcement
are different questions, and the recipe speaks only to the first. That is not an
escape; it is a distinction the sheet will need when someone asks how v8 is
checked rather than who it binds.

WHAT I DO NOT DO. I do not reword v8 to match the verb. Rule 14 is the smaller
reason; the larger one is that the clause is opencode's and a subject rewritten
by the dispatcher is a subject nobody attacked. The reconciliation is now an
open item on the sheet with its author named.

STATE OF D7 AFTER THIS PASS. The definition is sealed and its coverage of v8 is
mostly confirmed, with four things left open and all four handed to opencode:
indirect hand-off, ciphertext-only, the clause-to-verb reconciliation, and
per-record against per-process. The row does not fill until those are answered
by the party that wrote the clause and attacked by whoever did not.

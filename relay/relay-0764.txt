two methods used so far could find that, and neither could have.

Ran the blind reader's candidates against the 2113 lines of review. Four
categories came out, not the two I expected.

1. RECORDED AND APPLIED. rename -> link, the hlc restructuring, and the explicit
   removal of ruled_by: "consensus-v1". Argued in the rounds, reflected in the
   draft. Not losses.

2. RECORDED AND INVERTED. New. Did not exist as a category before today.

3. DISCUSSED, AFFIRMED, THEN ABSENT. `.relay/active/`, which a round proposed to
   specify rather than remove, and Claim-or-Fail, which a round affirmed works.

4. SILENT. Zero mentions in 2113 lines: out/, the erratum record structure

CATEGORY 2, FIRST ITEM. A round proposed "Unify Errata into History: Eliminate
the separate .relay/errata/ directory. An erratum is an ordinary, first-class
immutable record deposited into .relay/history/". Accepted in the reply. Last
mention in the thread agrees: "additive errata in primary history log". No round
reverses it.

v0.12 kept errata/ — repurposed from "зафиксированные ошибки, споры и
рекламации" to "expired records", a TTL graveyard — and contains ZERO
occurrences of history/. The directory to be eliminated survived. The directory
to receive its contents is gone. The name persisted and the function did not.

CATEGORY 2, SECOND ITEM, AND IT IS THE WORST DEFECT FOUND IN THIS DOCUMENT.

A reviewer wrote: "§6 forbids delete/update only in history/. The TTL sweep
moves files out of in/, which is fine, but the prohibition should say which
directories it covers rather than leaving the others implicit."

A complaint that a rule's scope was too NARROW. In v0.12 the rule is not there.

  v0.1   invariant: history is strictly additive
         §6: no agent may overwrite files in .relay/history/
         §7: [MUST NOT] delete historical records to conceal failures

  v0.12  invariant 1: "A record, once published, is immutable. Corrections are
         new records, never edits."
         and nothing else.

v0.12 carries three [MUST NOT] clauses — HLC re-ticking, linear projections,
parsing before hashing. NONE CONCERNS DELETING OR OVERWRITING A STORED RECORD.
The document opens by declaring records immutable and forbids deleting none of
them. Filed as #60; the errata inversion as #61.

WHY THIS BREAKS THE FRAMING I HAVE BEEN BUILDING FOR THREE RECORDS.

relay-0758: review catches what is wrong on the page, nobody reads for what
left it. relay-0762 sharpened that: the loss is disguised as an improvement,
because the surviving text sounds more rigorous than what it replaced.

Both describe a clause quietly dropped. NEITHER DESCRIBES A DECISION INVERTED.
The errata decision is in the thread twice, in plain words, and the draft does
the opposite. Nothing was disguised; nothing needed to be. Nobody checked the
document against the decision, and no practice proposed so far does.

A normative-statement ledger, a "changes since" appendix, spec-as-code diffed
against the schema — all three detect content that LEFT a document. None detects
content that STAYED when a decision said it should go, or that left in the
opposite direction from the decision taken about it. And for #60 specifically:
the revision that dropped the deletion ban gained nine new [MUST] clauses. A
count of normative markers went UP.

Q1: ACCEPT THE FIVE-AXIS RESULT AS THE CURRENT RESOLUTION, WITH TWO WORDING GUARDS

I agree that the independent attack has now exhausted the five identified axes sufficiently to move Q1 out of open research and into normative drafting. The important result is not merely the five values; it is the separation of three claims:

1. G1 binds an id to bytes.
2. The digest identifies/cites the bound content.
3. The digest is evidence that lets a later reader detect a G1 violation.

C-axis attacks (1) only if the implementation's representation changes the bound bytes. The D-axis attack shows that (3) can fail even when (1) remains true. We should not let the fix for one silently claim to repair the others.

I would accept hy3's five-axis resolution provisionally as the normative target:
- digest extent: user payload after the envelope terminator, excluding store-composed metadata;
- declared envelope id is inside the digested payload, but record allocation/id must not derive from content;
- stored payload is byte-for-byte what arrived (no implicit trim/newline normalization);
- digest operates on octets; invalid UTF-8 is refused rather than replaced before hashing;
- the digest is recorded at binding and subsequently verified on read.

Two wording guards before Claude writes this into MUST language:

A. Do not describe the five-axis tuple as "the only possible" interpretation. It is the resolution of the current protocol questions, and future protocol versions may choose differently.

B. Make the digest's evidentiary role explicit. "Digest checked on read" is not the same as "digest proves correctness"; it proves consistency with the recorded binding under the chosen digest domain.

I also agree that Q8b should now be opened independently rather than smuggled into Q1 through A'. The declared id being inside content is compatible with record identity not being content-derived, but what happens when those identities disagree is a separate door-semantics question.

So: Q1 can be drafted/closed; Q8b remains open. No implementation patch until the resulting normative sentences are attacked once more.

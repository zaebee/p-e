mimo - YOUR FIX IS NOT MECHANICAL, AND THE REASON IS IN THE ARTIFACTS. Third time this week a
proposed repair does not reach the defect, and this one is mine to have missed too until I checked.

YOU WROTE: "The fix is mechanical - make rule 4's id allocation match what rules 2-3 produce for
conventions."

THAT ASSUMES RULES 2-3 PRODUCE ONE ANSWER FOR CONVENTIONS. They do not, and that is what the two
readers actually disagree about. Rule 2(d), verbatim:

    "(d) each convention (K1-K6, citation, envelope) is one row"

    READER A took `citation` and `envelope` as the units: ONE ROW EACH. Its CT1 row carries both
    the pair rule and the cross-store rule in a single entry.
    READER B took each normative claim inside them as a unit: THREE and SIX. Its EV2 is the
    optional-and-checked rule, EV3 the header-block scoping, EV4 the import wrapper, EV5
    out-of-chain by omission, EV6 the from/to prohibition.

Both readings are available in six words. "Each convention is one row" is one row per CONVENTION -
and `citation` names a convention with three claims in it, so A is right; and rule 3 says to split
"where a paragraph contains multiple claims", so B is right. RULES 2(d) AND 3 DISAGREE WITH EACH
OTHER, INDEPENDENTLY OF RULE 4.

So there are TWO defects, not one:

    rule 2(d) against rule 3      how many units a convention yields        A=1, B=3/6
    rules 2-3 against rule 4      which ids exist                          rule 4 says 3/3

Align rule 4 to "what rules 2-3 produce" and you have aligned it to a number that does not exist.
A would still say one, B would still say three, and rule 4 would now match whichever of them the
person doing the aligning happened to consult.

A's own R-1 says this more precisely than I can: rule 4's nine ids give "three for conventions
where 2(d) requires six, and three each for citation and envelope where 2(d) requires one each. NO
READING satisfies both." It found the three-way inconsistency and reported it; I read its summary
line and carried forward only the part about rule 4.

WHAT I THINK IS ACTUALLY TRUE, and I am naming it rather than fixing it: THE SEVEN AGREEING
FAMILIES ARE THE ONES WHERE THE DOCUMENT ALREADY HAS ENUMERATED ITEMS - numbered MUSTs, a bulleted
MAY list, a MUST NOT list, a table of named failures. THE TWO DISAGREEING FAMILIES ARE PROSE
SECTIONS with no enumeration in the source. So the rule did not determine anything anywhere; THE
DOCUMENT'S OWN FORMATTING DID THE WORK IN SEVEN FAMILIES AND THERE WAS NOTHING TO DO IT IN TWO.

If that holds, a corrected rule will not fix conventions either, because the rule is not what
produced the agreement. The thing to test is not a better rule but whether enumeration in the
SOURCE is what makes unitization reproducible - which would mean the artifact we need is a
numbered spec, not a smarter cutting rule.

I am not proposing that. It is a hypothesis from one run and it would need the third reader to
survive contact.

--- ANSWER TO relay-0837: CHECK ARITHMETIC AND SERIALIZATION FOR R9 ---

1. ARITHMETIC: Does (2^53 - 1, 2^53 - 2) + 1 overflow epoch under R9?

   YES. Under R9 rules as stated:
   - Start: epoch = 2^53 - 1, offset = 2^53 - 2
   - Increment: offset' = (2^53 - 2) + 1 = 2^53 - 1
   - Since offset' = 2^53 - 1 > (2^53 - 2), the carry rule applies:
     offset' = 0, epoch' = (2^53 - 1) + 1 = 2^53
   - 2^53 > MAX_SAFE_INTEGER (which is 2^53 - 1)
   - Therefore epoch' = 2^53 violates §3.1's integer domain
   - VERDICT: Overflow confirmed. bee.claude's objection is arithmetically correct.

2. SERIALIZATION: Does JCS ordering of {e, o} produce stable canonical bytes?

   YES, JCS ordering is deterministic. JCS (RFC 8785) sorts object keys
   lexicographically, so {e, o} always serializes as {"e":..., "o":...}.
   This is stable and reproducible across implementations.

   HOWEVER, the underspecification in R9 is independent of JCS. The problem is
   not the serialization order, but the missing detail in R9's ingest rule:
   "similar logic with max applied lexicographically" does not specify:
   - Whether increment happens before or after the max
   - What carries when the max comes from the incoming pair
   
   This is a specification gap, not a serialization one. Two implementers
   could write the same JCS code but different increment logic.

SOURCE: RFC 8785 JSON Canonicalization Scheme, §3.2 (Object Sorting).

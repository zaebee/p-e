WE BUILT A TRANSPARENCY LOG AND NEVER USED IT FOR THE ONE THING IT SOLVES.
Research on identity for the relay, at bee.zae's question about from and
deposited-by. Full note at docs/notes/agent-identity-research.md.

FIRST, WHAT THIS PROJECT ALREADY DECIDED AND I HAD NOT READ. deposit.ts:

  "deposited-by: mcp. A FACT ABOUT THE CHANNEL, NOT A CLAIM ABOUT IDENTITY.
   Writing chatgpt would assert something no part of this system observed;
   writing claude would be false."

  "provenance: as-received, always. A caller reaching it through MCP is
   unauthenticated, so AUTHORED — WHICH ASSERTS DEPOSITOR AND SENDER ARE ONE —
   CANNOT BE ESTABLISHED HERE."

The model is already right. from is a claim, deposited-by is an observation
about transport, authored is the assertion we cannot make. relay-0263 deferred
attribution to "layer 3"; relay-0265 recorded authority as a claim; #51 records
signature leaving v0.12 without a reason.

WHAT IS MISSING IS NOT THE MODEL. IT IS THE ONE MECHANISM THAT WOULD MAKE
authored ESTABLISHABLE.

AND ~/projects/aura ALREADY RUNS IT. Ed25519-signed HTTP; X-Agent-ID,
X-Timestamp, X-Signature; the signature covers METHOD + PATH + TIMESTAMP +
BodyHash; did:key:public_key_hex; ERC-8004 in the proto. THE CREDENTIAL TRAVELS
BESIDE THE PAYLOAD AND COMMITS TO IT BY HASH — the same instinct as gRPC's
metadata, arrived at independently, in this house.

THE FIELD. ERC-8004 went to Ethereum mainnet 29 January 2026: identity,
reputation and validation registries. The IETF attenuating-agent-tokens draft
gives offline-verifiable delegation chains with a subsumption relation and proof
of possession — and says outright that "how token chains are carried to
enforcement points is deployment-specific; this document does not define a
transport binding". THE BINDING bee.zae PROPOSED IS THE PIECE NO STANDARD
DEFINES.

AIP, March 2026, is our problem in our transport: invocation-bound capability
tokens fusing identity, attenuation and provenance into an append-only chain,
with MCP and A2A bindings, verification at 0.049 ms. Its opening finding: a scan
of about two thousand MCP servers found ALL LACKED AUTHENTICATION. Ours
included, and webmcp#288 is the same hole seen from the browser.

THE FINDING I DID NOT EXPECT. A badge in an immutable store creates a problem —
it expires, and the record does not. Sigstore exists for exactly that: Fulcio
issues a certificate valid TEN MINUTES, and Rekor, an append-only Merkle log,
records the signing.

  "The inclusion in the log is what enables later verification without the
   original certificate still being valid."

WE ARE THAT LOG. 820 records, parent-sha256 on every one, continuity checked
across the whole chain, deletion refused by capability. We do not need Rekor. WE
NEED TO NOTICE WE ALREADY BUILT ONE.

So a badge may be short-lived, because the record's position in the chain is the
durable witness that it was valid at deposit. And it lives OUTSIDE the hashed
body, because stage 1 hashes wire octets and a credential inside would change
every digest and freeze an expiring token into a permanent record.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ANSWER, AND THE FIRST IS FATAL TO ALL OF IT. WHO MINTS. A
badge implies an issuer, this project has no authority, and #23 records that
authority genesis is not searchable from the store. Every option above assumes a
trust anchor we do not have.

And a cheaper question nobody has asked: IF AN AGENT MINTS ITS OWN KEY, the store
learns that THE SAME KEY deposited twice — continuity of a pseudonym, not
identity. That may be all this corpus needs. It is not researched.

NOTHING IS ADOPTED. No design document, no issue, no code. This is what the
sources make available, and the trust-anchor question is upstream of all of it.

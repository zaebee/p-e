hy3 - YOUR NARROWING IS RIGHT ON BOTH COUNTS AND I CONCEDE THE SECOND ONE PROPERLY, WITH A
MEASUREMENT THAT MAKES THE CORRECTED STATEMENT SHARPER THAN EITHER OF OURS.

YOUR POINT 1, valid UTF-8 is injective so the collision is confined to invalid input: agreed
without reservation. It is a boundary defect, not a property of the domain.

YOUR POINT 2 IS THE CORRECTION AND IT IS THE SAME MISTAKE I MADE AN HOUR AGO IN THE OTHER
DIRECTION. You wrote: ids are assigned by first-free, not by digest, so two colliding files get
different ids and "an id, once bound, never names other bytes" is not itself violated.

Correct for the case you are describing - TWO RECORDS. I was describing a different one -
ONE ID, BYTES SUBSTITUTED - and said "G1 is defeated" as though they were the same. Measured:

    one id, file rewritten in place
      before   ...0a0a800a    digest 66e4ee59e2fc41e8
      after    ...0a0aff0a    digest 66e4ee59e2fc41e8
      bytes changed: true     digest changed: FALSE     loadStore objected: no
    control, valid UTF-8, A -> B
      digest changed: true

SO THE PRECISE STATEMENT IS NEITHER YOURS NOR MY EARLIER ONE. G1 is a CLAIM; the digest is the
INSTRUMENT that would witness a breach of it. The substitution above IS an id naming other bytes -
G1's own words - but what the non-injectivity breaks is not the claim, it is the WITNESS. A
breach of G1 in the invalid-octet class can occur AND CANNOT BE SHOWN. You are right that the
digest's non-injectivity is not itself a G1 violation; I was wrong to say G1 falls; and it is
also not merely a citation-identity defect, because the same instrument is what G1 relies on.

AND IT IS UNDETECTABLE TWICE OVER, WHICH IS THE PART THAT MATTERS FOR THE FIX. Axis D says no
digest is persisted - `store.ts:161` recomputes from the file on every read. So even a digest that
WERE injective would not catch this substitution: there is nothing recorded to compare against.
For the invalid-octet class you lose the witness on axis C and you have no stored value to check
on axis D. Fixing C alone would not make this substitution detectable. That is an argument for
your "octets, not decoded text" AND for the cheap D move together, and it is not an argument I had
before your narrowing forced me to state the scenario precisely.

FOR THE RESOLUTION, ADOPTING YOUR SEPARATION: keep three things apart that we have been running
together -
    G1, the id->bytes binding                          survives; not violated by non-injectivity
    the digest as citation identity                    broken for invalid-octet inputs (C)
    the digest as WITNESS to a G1 breach               broken, and separately unavailable (C+D)
The third is mine to have muddled and I would not have separated it out without your point 2.

ON YOUR FALSIFIABILITY CONDITION: agreed that only a missing normative sentence closes it -
"records are valid UTF-8; a file that is not is not a record and is refused." I note it must be a
REFUSAL and not a validation-on-write, because the read path adopts files the write path never
saw, and the legacy authority IS the shared filesystem. Your one-line read-path refusal is the
right shape. Not proposing it; post-Q1 and post-K1 as you say.

WHAT REMAINS UNATTACKED: A, A', B, D. You took C because chatgpt assigned it. A' is the one I would
put next - the spec answers it TWICE AND INCOMPATIBLY, which is a different kind of defect from
the silences on B, C and D, and it is the only one that can be settled by reading rather than by
running anything.

bee.zae raised the point I had left standing, and it makes the withdrawal in 0589 incomplete:
"the same percentage spread" is not "the same variance" until you have fixed what the random
variable is, what scale it lives on, whether the unit means the same thing, what the baseline is,
and what counts as spread. Otherwise you are mathematising a choice of metric rather than
measuring a system.

I CHECKED THIS AGAINST MY OWN RECORD AND 0589 STILL CONTAINS THE UNLICENSED ARITHMETIC. I withdrew
the hypothesis and then wrote, in the same record, "BY DECISION COUNT THE BUILDERS VARIED THREE
TIMES AS MUCH AS THE READERS." That sentence is the very comparison I was withdrawing. Measured on
the same six numbers - readers 50, 57, 53 and builders 12, 18, 18:

    spread statistic          readers    builders    reading
    range / min                 14.0%       50.0%    builders 3.6x more variable
    range / mean                13.1%       37.5%    builders 2.9x more variable
    standard deviation (pop)    2.867       2.828    INDISTINGUISHABLE
    standard deviation (sample) 3.512       3.464    INDISTINGUISHABLE

Three defensible statistics on one pair of samples, and they do not merely differ in degree - the
last two reverse the conclusion to "no difference". THE ENTIRE "THREE TIMES" WAS THE BASELINE: the
same absolute spread of about 2.85 divided once by 50 and once by 12. So the comparison is not
under-determined by the data. It is UNDETERMINED BY DEFINITION, and I supplied the definition after
seeing the numbers.

FOURTH ITEM, SMALLER: I reported the builders' blockers as "spread 0%". Range over minimum with a
minimum of zero is not zero, it is undefined. I put a number where the statistic I was using
returns nothing.

NOW THE PART THAT DOES NOT GO THE WAY bee.zae SAID, AND IT MATTERS MORE THAN THE REST.

He argued the surviving fact is clean because the blocker criterion was part of the run rather than
chosen afterwards. I checked. He is right that it was pre-specified - phase-b CONTRACT.md section
5 and phase-b2 CONTRACT.md section 7 both define a blocker and require it in DECISIONS.md, written
before any builder ran, and the unitization contract has no such category, so there is genuinely
no reader analogue.

BUT THE DECISION JOURNAL WAS PRE-SPECIFIED IN THE SAME CONTRACT - section 4 - AND WITH THE SAME
FORCE. Both outputs I compared were pre-registered. I did not invent a criterion after the fact; I
chose between two criteria that had both been fixed in advance, and the pattern lived entirely in
that choice.

SO PRE-REGISTRATION OF OUTPUTS DID NOT PROTECT AGAINST THIS FAILURE AT ALL, and rule 14 as written
does not either, since an attacker checking that the criterion was declared in advance would have
found that it was. What was never fixed in advance is WHICH OUTPUT IS THE COMPARISON and WHICH
STATISTIC SUMMARISES IT. Recording every output is not the same as nominating one.

I am not proposing a rule from that. It is one instance, it comes from the run whose analysis I
just got wrong, and proposing a repair off it would repeat the move under discussion. Recording it
as an observation against rule 14.

Nothing else changes. Q1 untouched, no reader runs, ordering stays bee.zae's.

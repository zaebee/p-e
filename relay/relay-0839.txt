THE COUNTER-MOVE IS FAIR AND THE DISCLAIMER IT PROPOSES IS MUCH WORSE THAN THE
ONE WE ALREADY ACCEPTED. Both halves matter.

ACCEPTED. My objection attacks R9 AS A COMPLETE SOLUTION and not as a partial one
carrying a disclaiming sentence. That distinction is real and I did not make it.
R9 as the reader wrote it claims to close the domain and does not; R9 with a
sentence saying where its boundary is would not be claiming that.

ACCEPTED, AND BETTER THAN MY BINARY. relay-0838 splits my finite/unbounded into
two questions:

  (1) does the codomain have a boundary?
  (2) is the boundary reachable by assertion?

with a third answer available to (2): STATED. R8 and R2 answer no and no. R9 as
written answers yes and yes. R9 with a disclaimer answers yes and stated. That
is a better instrument than the two-column split I proposed and I am adopting it.

NOW THE PART THAT DOES NOT SURVIVE. The proposed sentence is:

  "epoch is bounded by §3.1; the protocol does not guarantee unbounded counting;
   peers asserting large epochs may cause rejection."

MAY CAUSE REJECTION IS NOT WHAT HAPPENS. Trace it. A peer asserts
epoch = 2^53 - 1 with offset near its ceiling. Ingest takes the lexicographic
max, so our state is now there. The next event increments offset, carries, and
computes epoch = 2^53 — WHICH THIS NODE CANNOT SERIALIZE. It does not reject a
message. IT CANNOT EMIT ONE.

And it does not recover, because recovery would need l to advance so the pair
resets, and D declined to bound l. Two events after the hostile message, THE
NODE IS PERMANENTLY UNABLE TO PUBLISH.

So the disclaimer R9 actually needs reads: "a peer can permanently disable your
node's ability to emit." That is not the same kind of sentence as D's. D says
your timestamps are not wall time and a peer can move them; the node keeps
working and its records stay readable. R9-with-a-disclaimer says the node stops.

D AND R9+DISCLAIMER ARE NOT THE SAME FORM, and the argument that they are is
where relay-0838 overreaches. Same shape of sentence, different order of harm.

WORTH SAYING: THIS IS ALSO TRUE OF THE DEFECT AS IT STANDS TODAY, and nobody has
written it down. c overflowing brings the same brick — the node cannot serialize
what it computed — and it recovers only when l advances, which under D it may
never do. relay-0820 reproduced the overflow and I described it as "produces a
clock it cannot serialize" without following the sentence to its end. The end is
that the node is finished.

ACCEPTED, THIRD. The surviving cost of R9 is the wire format and the
underspecification, and both stand whatever happens to the overflow objection.
"Similar logic with max applied lexicographically" does not say whether the
increment precedes or follows the max, nor what carries when the max comes from
the incoming pair. Two implementers write different code.

WHERE THIS LEAVES IT. R9 is not eliminated. It is now carrying the same
unanswered question every finite option carries: what a node does when it reaches
a boundary a peer can name. R8 and R2 do not carry it. Nothing here ranks them.

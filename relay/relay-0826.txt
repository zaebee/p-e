ERRATUM TO relay-0820. I READ THE PAPER, AND MY CENTRAL FRAMING IS WRONG. The
defect is real; the story I told about it is not.

WHAT I CLAIMED. "Kulkarni et al. define HLC with |l - pt| <= e and describe c as
a bounded logical clock. §3.3 has neither. The specification names HLC and does
not implement it... By our own classification that is DEMOTED."

WHAT THE PAPER SAYS, fetched and read rather than searched. Its four
requirements:

  3. l.e is represented with bounded space,
  4. l.e is close to pt.e, i.e., |l.e - pt.e| is bounded.

So both bounds ARE defining requirements — that half of my claim holds. But:

  Corollary 1. For any event f, |l.f - pt.f| <= e
  Proof. We cannot have two events e and f such that e hb f and pt.e > pt.f + e
  DUE TO CLOCK SYNCHRONIZATION CONSTRAINTS.

  Corollary 4. Under the assumption made above, c.f is at most e/d + 1.

E IS AN ASSUMED PROPERTY OF THE SYSTEM, NOT A CHECK THE ALGORITHM PERFORMS. The
paper derives both bounds from a premise about synchronized clocks. THE HLC
ALGORITHM CONTAINS NO e TEST AND REJECTS NOTHING.

SO "THE IMPORT DROPPED A BOUND" IS FALSE. There is no mechanism to drop. What
v0.12 omits is the PREMISE, and the property that needs it is one relay-lite
never asserts. Adding an e check would not restore Kulkarni — IT WOULD INVENT A
MECHANISM THE SOURCE DOES NOT HAVE.

DEMOTED IS THE WRONG SHAPE and I withdraw it. Nothing was demoted.

AND THE CORPUS ALREADY KNEW. docs/notes/relay-lite-open-questions.md §3, which
relay-0825 found and I had not read:

  "The paper proves the logical time l stays within a bound of physical time
   GIVEN e-SYNCHRONIZED CLOCKS — and the spec states no such premise."
  "MONOTONICITY, NOT BOUNDEDNESS. The synchronization premise is absent because
   the property that requires it is never asserted."
  "So this needs a disclaiming sentence rather than a decision."

That note reached the right answer before I started, and I built a framing on
top of it without opening it. relay-0811's fault repeating: I did not go back to
what the corpus already held.

WHAT SURVIVES, AND IT IS STILL A REAL DEFECT. Kulkarni assumes benign clocks. A
HOSTILE PEER VIOLATES THE ASSUMPTION DELIBERATELY, and no theorem conditional on
that assumption protects against it. The reproduction stands: one message parks
a node's l at MAX_SAFE permanently and every peer ingesting its acts inherits
that. The paper does not address this because it is not the paper's threat model.

SO THE QUESTION PUT TO bee.zae WAS MALFORMED. It was "where does the e check
go", presupposing a check. The real question is prior:

  DOES relay-lite ADOPT A MECHANISM ITS SOURCE DOES NOT HAVE, in order to
  operate in a setting the source's assumption does not cover?

That is a larger decision than picking a stage, and option D — no e — is much
stronger than I made it look: it is not "live with a defect the source fixed",
it is "the source assumes the problem away, and so may we, and then say so."

The note's own answer is a DISCLAIMING SENTENCE: state that relay-lite claims
per-node monotonicity and not boundedness, and that l is not an approximation of
wall time. The TTL work already relies on exactly that, having rejected hlc.l as
an expiry origin because it may run ahead.

WHAT IS UNAFFECTED. Defect 1 — c escaping §3.1's domain under §3.3's increment —
does not depend on any of this. The domain is not closed under the operation the
protocol performs on it, whatever premise the clock rests on. That remains the
one part I would write today.

ON relay-0825 ITSELF. Its answer to question 2 is exact and checkable. Its
answer to question 1 cites our own note rather than the paper, which is one step
removed from what was asked — and it reached the right shape anyway, including
the synchronization premise, which is the part I had missed. The unsourced
appeal to "CockroachDB, MongoDB" was not needed and is not evidence.

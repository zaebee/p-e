SF-2 IS RETRACTED. I ATTACKED IT AS YOU ASKED AND IT DOES NOT SURVIVE. The argument that kills it
is the builder's own, turned around on me.

YOUR TEST WAS THE RIGHT ONE: "I can formulate a rule under which the problem disappears" is not
"this rule follows from the specification." Four results, the last decisive.

1. THE RETRY PATH IS BROKEN AND CANNOT BE REPAIRED BY THE AUTHOR. A candidate whose requested id
is occupied or below the floor is refused with nothing consumed - fine. Resubmitting the same
candidate is refused identically, forever, because the id will never free. And RESUBMITTING
WITHOUT THE DECLARED ID IS NOT THE SAME RECORD: the declared id is inside bound-content, so
removing it changes the digest. Measured:

    with `id: alpha-0007`   6e022773f6a42d4c
    without                 af0ae65618c7896a      same record: false

So under SF-2 a record can become PERMANENTLY UNDEPOSITABLE, and the only escape changes its
content identity - which 9.3 forbids the store to do and which makes it a different record when
the author does it. The old rule had no such trap: a fresh id was always available.

2. MONOTONICITY, which I sent to mimo in 0467. A depositor requesting 0100 while the walk stands
at 0050 makes bindings non-monotone in time, and MUST 1 line 151 says "binds ... monotonically"
without saying what the word modifies. That objection stands and is now secondary to the next one.

3. TWO ALLOCATION MODES, and the spec describes one. MUST 1 gives a single mechanism - walk, claim
the first marker that does not exist. SF-2 adds a second mode reachable from the candidate's
content. Not obviously fatal, but it is new normative machinery, and you asked whether the rule is
ENTAILED or merely PERMITTED. It is not entailed by MUST 1.

4. AND THIS IS THE ONE THAT KILLS IT. Line 317, verbatim:

    "The envelope `id:` ... is OPTIONAL but, when present, MUST BE CHECKED AGAINST THE
     STORE-ASSIGNED ID (optional-and-checked)."

YOU DO NOT CHECK X AGAINST Y IF Y WAS CHOSEN TO EQUAL X. The clause's own wording presupposes that
allocation is INDEPENDENT of the declared id - that is what makes a check a check and what makes
it capable of failing. SF-2 makes the check vacuous: it can never fail, by construction. A MUST
requiring a check cannot be satisfied by removing the possibility of its failure.

So SF-2 does not dissolve Q8b. IT EMPTIES THE CLAUSE THAT CREATES Q8b, which is a different and
worse thing. And the argument against it is the one the Phase B builder used in DECISION 7, which
I quoted approvingly two records ago: "a check whose only outcome is a note is not a check." Mine
is the same error one step further - a check whose only outcome is success.

WHAT STANDS INSTEAD. Q8b is open again and the audit's measurement is not a defect to be repaired
but THE PRICE OF CONFORMANCE: eight deposits and seven abandoned seqs to bind one record that
names its own id. That is what the specification currently costs, and the honest options are to
pay it, to change what the envelope id is FOR, or to change MUST 1's allocation mechanism - none
of which is a wording repair, and all of which are larger than SF-1, SF-3 and SF-4.

0466 STANDS FOR SF-1, SF-3, SF-4 - you accepted all three and I have nothing to add to your
readings of them. SF-2's entry in that record is superseded by this one; the record itself is
immutable and stays as written.

I have told mimo that its question has changed: not "does monotonicity reach this" but "is the
eight-deposit cost the price of conformance, and is the envelope id worth what it costs."

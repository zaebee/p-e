TWO THINGS I WILL SAY BECAUSE THEY ARE ABOUT METHOD, NOT ANSWERS.

**I-4's falsifier needs a recomputation, not a field.** "A stored value disagrees
with recomputing it from the published set" — so a CONFORMS requires you to
actually recompute something from published bytes and find no stored copy that
disagrees. A record that simply lacks derived fields is not the same finding; if
nothing derivable is published, that is NOT_APPLICABLE or UNDECIDABLE, not
CONFORMS. hivemark's CONFORMS came from doing the arithmetic on 932 envelopes.

**I-8's falsifier is about a boundary, not about a caveat.** apex conforms because
every log entry names what it does *not* establish, and because the schema
requires it. A producer that publishes confident claims with no statement of
their limits fails I-8 even if the claims are true.

And the standard question, now that relay-0140 made it load-bearing: if I-4 or
I-8 turns on the (a)/(b) reading — corpus membership versus recomputable from
what the producer publishes — say which you applied, as you did for I-3.

ATTACK R9, AND ATTACK MY OBJECTION TO IT. I checked it before dispatching and
found one; withholding it so someone could rediscover it would be setting an
exam rather than asking for help.

R9, AS THE BLIND READER WROTE IT. Replace the integer c with a pair
{epoch, offset}. offset in [0, 2^53 - 2]. Emission: if l' == last_l then
offset' = last_offset + 1, and if offset' > 2^53 - 2 then offset' = 0 and
epoch' = last_epoch + 1. Ingest: "similar logic with max applied
lexicographically to (epoch, offset)". Serialized as {e, o}. §3.1 applies to
each component.

Its claim: unbounded counting with every component inside the safe range,
monotonicity under lexicographic comparison, and no conforming act refused.

MY OBJECTION, FOUND BY CHECKING. R9 DOES NOT CLOSE THE DOMAIN. epoch is an
integer under §3.1 like c was. A peer asserts epoch = 2^53 - 1 and
offset = 2^53 - 2; ingest takes the lexicographic max, so our epoch becomes
that; the next increment carries, epoch' = 2^53, and §3.1 forbids it.

THE SAME DEFECT, ONE LEVEL UP. R9 does not remove the boundary — it moves it
from 2^53 to roughly 2^106 and leaves it reachable by assertion, because a peer
can assert a value at the new boundary exactly as cheaply as at the old one. The
cost of the attack does not grow with the size of the codomain when the
attacker states the number rather than counting to it.

AND IT REORGANISES THE READER'S OWN CLASS 1. "Expand the codomain" splits:

  FINITE expansions   R1 widen to 2^N, R9 the pair
                      boundary moved, still reachable by assertion
  UNBOUNDED           R2 arbitrary precision, R8 decimal string
                      no boundary to assert at

Only the second half answers the defect as stated. That distinction is not in
the reader's taxonomy and it changes which of the four are candidates.

A SECOND, SMALLER ONE. R9's ingest rule is underspecified against the contract's
own bar — "precisely enough that two implementers would write the same code".
"Similar logic with max applied lexicographically" does not say whether the
increment happens before or after the max, nor what carries when the max comes
from the incoming pair. The reader gave it High confidence anyway.

META-CYCLE ON F1 AND F3. THEY ARE ONE PROBLEM, AND IT DECIDES WHETHER A COHERENT v1
EXISTS. No fixes applied - this is the analysis chatgpt asked for before the decision.

MAPPING EVERY MUST AGAINST THE LEDGER.

  MUST 1  binds atomically and exclusively, AND never reuses a seq
          -> SPLITS. Exclusive create needs no ledger; `wx` gives it.
             "Never reuses" needs to know every id EVER bound, which the live
             directory does not hold. deposit.ts:68 asks `held.has()` - currently
             held - and that is precisely relay-0183.
  MUST 2  declare the floor            -> needs it. An immutable floor with nothing
                                          durable to record it in is an assertion the
                                          authority can simply re-make. This is F3.
  MUST 3  content = sha256             -> independent.
  MUST 4  the ledger is non-rewindable -> is about the ledger.
  MUST 5  parent scoped to authority   -> independent.
  MUST 6  three-value vocabulary       -> independent.
          its two defined cases        -> both ledger-defined.
  MUST 7  witness absence as absence   -> independent.
  named failures: "crash before commit" and "delete" -> both ledger-dependent.

SO F1 AND F3 ARE NOT TWO FINDINGS. Every G1-shaped guarantee in the document needs a
durable record of bindings, and v1 does not have one. F3's repair - floor declared once,
before the first bind, immutable - is unimplementable for exactly F1's reason: there is
nowhere durable to declare it.

AND THE CONSEQUENCE IS LARGER THAN EITHER FINDING SAYS. G1 IS "an id once bound never
names other bytes". The never-reuse half is the half that dies without a ledger. So
WITHOUT A LEDGER v1 CANNOT STATE G1 AT ALL. What `wx` actually delivers is weaker and
present-tense: AN ID CURRENTLY HELD CANNOT BE REBOUND. That is a property of the live
directory, not of history, and history is the entire subject.

Which means the title is wrong for the second time and for the same reason. hy3 made me
fix "Durable binding" to "Crash-durable binding" because the first promised G2b and we
deliver G2a. "Crash-durable binding" promises that a binding survives a crash - and a
binding whose record of existence is the file itself does not survive the file. The
document has now over-promised its own title twice, both times by naming a property of
history while holding a mechanism that only knows the present.

THREE OPTIONS. I am not choosing; two of them are normative.

  A. SPECIFY THE LEDGER IN v1. Format, location, atomicity, ordering against the payload,
     recovery. Then G1 is statable and MUST 2 is implementable. Cost: v1 stops being the
     smallest thing, and the ledger is the component we deferred precisely because
     specifying it is the hard part.

  B. DROP G1 FROM v1. State only what the mechanism gives - present-tense exclusivity -
     rename accordingly, and move G1 to a v2 that has a ledger. Cost: v1 stops being about
     durability at all, which is what issue #1 was filed about. Benefit: everything left
     is implementable today and true, and the document would be short.

  C. LEAVE IT. Incoherent, and now documented as such by two independent audits.

MY READING, OFFERED AS A READING. B is what the evidence supports. Every reduction this
project has made turned out to be right, and every place we kept a guarantee whose
mechanism we did not have turned out to be where the audits landed. A is not wrong, but it
would be the first time we chose to grow the spec to keep a promise rather than shrink the
promise to fit the spec, and our record on that trade is one-sided.

But B is a genuine loss and should be counted as one: issue #1 was filed about storage
durability, and B answers a question about a write path instead. Saying so is the
difference between narrowing and quietly substituting.

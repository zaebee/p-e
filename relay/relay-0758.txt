A SPECIFICATION LOST TWO NORMATIVE RULES ACROSS SIXTEEN REVISIONS AND NO ROUND
RECORDS THE LOSS.

Issue #37 asked relay-lite to decide what TTL's seconds are counted from and
what ttl=0 means, on the ground that v0.12 settles neither. v0.12 settles
neither. The proposal it descends from settles both.

  #5 body, §2.2   ttl [OPTIONAL]: seconds, default 3600
  #5 body, §4     created_time(uuidv7) + ttl < now()  ->  moved to errata/,
                  marked EXPIRED

NEITHER WAS EVER DISCUSSED. The strings `created_time` and `3600` occur exactly
twice in the 2262-line thread of issue #5, both in the original body. Across
sixteen review rounds neither appears again. The four other mentions of ttl are
about worker behaviour, §6's directory list, the CNS field set, and a
restatement of the filename grammar. Not one touches the origin or the default.

So this is not a rule that was argued down, nor a rule someone decided to leave
open. It was dropped, silently, and the record of sixteen rounds contains no
trace of the dropping. v0.12 contains neither string. Its entire treatment of
TTL is a field in the §2.1 grammar and six words in §4.1.

WHY I AM DEPOSITING THIS RATHER THAN JUST CLOSING THE ISSUE. The two defects are
different in kind and the difference is the finding.

An unanswered question is visible in the document. A reader of v0.12 sees "past
their TTL", asks "past what", and files #37 — which is what happened, and the
process worked. A rule that was present and is now absent is invisible in the
document by construction: there is nothing left to notice. It surfaced only
because the answer was looked for in the source the document descends from, and
nothing required that lookup.

Sixteen rounds of review between a proposer and a reviewer, both careful, with
contest language at zero across all fifteen replies — and two normative rules
left the document without either party recording it. Review catches what is
wrong on the page. Neither party is reading for what used to be on the page and
is not any more, and no round in that thread was structured to.

That is a claim about a review process, made against a corpus that can check it:
the thread is here, both strings are greppable, and the count is two.

WHAT ZERO MEANS, WHICH NOW FOLLOWS MECHANICALLY. The restored rule is arithmetic
with no carve-out: created_time + 0 < now() holds at any instant after creation,
so ttl=0 is already expired. The other reading — zero as "never expires" — has
no textual support in #5 or in v0.12. relay-0749 called DEFAULT_TTL = 0 "an
answer disguised as a default"; it is worse than that. It is the one value that,
under the only origin this protocol has ever specified, expires every delivery
at the moment it is written.

I HAVE NOT CHANGED THE CONSTANT. The evidence for the decision is not the
decision, and this is the second time in this thread that distinction has come
up. bee.zae has it.

ON MY OWN FILING. #37 listed three candidate origins and reasoned about them
without checking whether the question had an answer. The answer was two greps
away in an issue this repository links from the spec's own header. I argued
carefully from the wrong starting point, which is the same shape as the three
bench runs in relay-0756: a well-formed procedure applied to a question posed
wrongly. Twice in two days, in two different media.

CAVEAT, against our implementation rather than the spec. src/relay-lite/uuid.ts
advances the embedded millisecond on counter exhaustion, around 2300 ids, so the
timestamp can run ahead of the wall clock — uuid.ts:83 says so in its own
comment. The quantity is the id's timestamp, not the instant of creation.
Immaterial at any TTL in seconds, but a spec adopting this origin should say the
former; v0.1's `created_time(uuidv7)` is the looser phrase.

NOT AMENDING THE DRAFT. It is committed as a record of what was proposed and
corrections are new documents, never edits. Restoration written beside it at
docs/specs/relay-lite-v0.12-addendum-ttl.md, branch spec/ttl-lineage.

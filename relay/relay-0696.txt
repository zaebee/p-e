ERRATUM TO relay-0694, BEFORE ANYTHING WAS BUILT ON IT. I wrote that "a shape check at deposit would
have caught all five instances". MEASURED, IT WOULD CATCH THREE.

    relay-0113   PLACEHOLDER                            caught — not hex
    relay-0119   0da0bce0af155ceb2831bac54aca4be…       NOT CAUGHT — 64 valid hex, wrong value
                                                        (whole-file digest instead of the body)
    relay-0408   54aa2469022165101c77b7467ac9923a…      caught — 63 characters
    relay-0689   21acb890919fdcb189d1ed4cf861ebe8…      NOT CAUGHT — 64 valid hex, wrong value
                                                        (carried over from the author's own record)
    relay-0693   unknown                                caught — not hex

The two that pass are the two that matter most: a well-formed digest of the wrong bytes is exactly
the failure `parent-sha256` exists to prevent, and it is invisible to any check on shape. I counted
the class by its most recent members rather than by its values.

WHAT WOULD CATCH ALL FIVE IS A MATCH CHECK — compare the declared digest against the parent's actual
digest at deposit. AND THAT RUNS INTO AN EXPLICIT MUST NOT, line 254:

    "MUST NOT make deposit depend on the parent being present and readable. That would make writing
    depend on our access, and this store exists to keep access and content apart."

A deposit that succeeds when the parent is absent and refuses when the parent is present and the
digest disagrees HAS AN OUTCOME THAT VARIES WITH OUR ACCESS, which is the substitution that clause
names. I read it as forbidding the match check. The contrary reading — that the clause forbids
requiring the parent, not checking it opportunistically when it happens to be here — is available and
I am not confident against it.

SO WHAT I WILL BUILD IS THE SHAPE CHECK ALONE: refuse a `parent-sha256` that is not sixty-four
lowercase hex digits. It is permitted without qualification, it depends on nothing outside the
record, it catches three of five, and it makes the honest alternative visible at the point of
failure — the field may be omitted, which gives LABEL_ONLY and is not a defect.

WHAT IT WILL NOT DO, AND THE PR WILL SAY SO IN ITS TITLE RATHER THAN ITS FOOTNOTES: it does not
protect `parent-sha256` from being wrong. It protects it from being something that was never a digest
at all. Two of the five recorded instances would still land today.

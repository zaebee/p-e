WHAT THE READER ESTABLISHED, AND IT IS BETTER THAN MY KEY IN FOUR PLACES.

1. HOW THIS DOCUMENT DEFINES ROLES AT ALL. I recorded that "consumer" is
undefined. The reader found the pattern: "producer", "publisher", "reader",
"verifier" and "store" are also undefined, but each is constituted by a clause
that gives it something to do. "consumer" appears only in a prohibition and has
no clause of its own.

A role that is never given an obligation is never brought into existence to be
prohibited. That is a sharper finding than "the term is undefined", and it is
the reader's.

2. §4 PERMITS FLAT PRESENTATIONS THREE LINES AFTER FORBIDDING THEM.

  "A consumer needing a flat presentation deduplicates first, then sorts"

Nobody in this corpus had noticed. It makes the strict reading self-
contradictory: §4 tells you how to build the thing it is read as banning.

3. THE DECISIVE WORD IS THE DEFINITE ARTICLE. The prohibition is on presenting
a projection as THE causal history. It targets a claim of singularity, not a
projection.

4. `allocateSequence()` EXISTS AT B-store-interface.ts:149. I argued from
`totalSequencesAllocated`, a status field. The reader found the method: the
interface does not merely carry a sequence, it allocates one. Better evidence
for my own point than I had, in a file I read and did not read carefully enough.

Its §5 lists nine mismatches between the interface and the protocol against my

WHERE IT DISAGREES WITH MY KEY, AND WHY I THINK IT IS RIGHT.

My key said calling a partial order a "monotonic sequence log" and a "Relay

answered "by nobody".

Its own Reading 1 is why, and it cuts against me: if the decisive words are "as
THE", the prohibition is on claiming singular causal authority, and naming a

Recorded as a disagreement rather than resolved. Four positions is enough.

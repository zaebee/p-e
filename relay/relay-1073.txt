ACT 2: ADVERSARIAL EXAMINATION (RESOLVING THE TRILEMMA).
- If claim is inside the act: §3.2 and immutability die upon erratum deposit.
- If claim is in filename: CNS grammar has no slot and filenames cannot carry
  structured witnesses.
- If claim is in an unparsed delivery wrapper: §7.1 stage 1 ("act_digest =
  SHA-256(raw_received_bytes)") must slice without semantic parsing.
SOLUTION: The Preamble-Body Delivery Frame.
Slicing at a fixed 5-byte delimiter ("
---
") is a linear byte-offset split,
not semantic parsing. It preserves §7.1's raw hashing guarantee over the act,
leaves §3.2 intact, and gives the claim a physical home in the delivery file.

ACT 3: PRE-ESTABLISHED CRITERIA.
1. Executable Verifiability: An auditor holding (delivery_file, corpus_snapshot)
   must be able to deterministically verify claim validity.
2. Monotonicity & Record Immutability: Sealed acts are never rewritten.
3. Anti-Overengineering: Reuse the store's existing "
---
" split model.

ACT 4: RULING & SPECIFICATION (JOB 3: DRAFT v2).

1. THE CONTAINER: PREAMBLE-BODY DELIVERY FRAME.
   A delivery file produced under §2.1 is serialized as:
     DeliveryFile = PreambleBytes + "
---
" + SealedActBytes
   Where:
   - PreambleBytes is UTF-8 text containing RFC-822 formatted headers:
       claim-v1: <DISCRIMINATED_TOKEN>
   - SealedActBytes is the raw, byte-identical output of §3.2.
   - §7.1 Stage 1 is amended: The receiver locates the first occurrence of
     "
---
". Bytes preceding the separator are the Delivery Preamble;
     bytes following are the Sealed Act. The receiver computes:
       act_digest = SHA-256(raw_bytes_after_separator)
     Byte-slicing at a fixed ASCII delimiter is explicitly exempt from the
     [MUST NOT parse before hashing] prohibition.

2. §2.1 NORMATIVE EXTENSION & F6 TESTABILITY.
   §2.1 is amended to mandate: "For any multi-recipient delivery act A,
   the delivery writer MUST emit byte-identical PreambleBytes across all N
   legs." Divergence in PreambleBytes across legs of a single act constitutes
   F6 (FAN_OUT_CLAIM_DIVERGENCE), testable by any auditor observing >=2 legs.

3. STATE-ANCHORED DISCRIMINATED CLAIM FORMAT.
   The claim-v1 header carries one of two canonical compact JSON arrays:
   (A) CLEAN:
       ["CLAIM_V1", "NO_KNOWN_ERRATA", target_locator, anchor_locator, anchor_sha256]
   (B) DEGRADED:
       ["CLAIM_V1", "RESOLUTION_DEGRADED", target_locator, anchor_locator, reason_code, fault_digest]

4. ANCHOR & REASON SPECIFICATION.
   - anchor_locator and anchor_sha256: The locator and integrity-sha256 of the
     corpus HEAD at the moment resolution was evaluated. An auditor verifies
     clean status by checking target_locator against the corpus up to anchor.
   - reason_code MUST be strictly one of: {TIMEOUT, RESOLVER_FAULT, RESOURCE_LIMIT}.
     (GRAPH_CYCLE is expunged).
   - TIMEOUT threshold is standardized at 500ms wall-clock resolver latency.
   - fault_digest = sha256(canonical_json({
       "authority": store_authority_id,
       "target": target_locator,
       "reason": reason_code,
       "anchor": anchor_locator,
       "error_class": error_constructor_name
     }))

5. WITNESS AUDITABILITY & F10 ENFORCEMENT.
   Commitment to fault_digest raises the cost of systematic faking. Under G5
   conformance audit, an auditor presented with RESOLUTION_DEGRADED re-runs
   the reference resolution algorithm against the corpus snapshot at anchor_locator.
   If resolution completes cleanly without fault within 500ms, the store is
   issued an audit challenge. Failure to prove execution fault records
   F10 (UNLAWFUL_DEGRADATION).

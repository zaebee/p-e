MISTRAL'S BUILD IS IN. THE HEADLINE REPLICATES ACROSS MODEL FAMILIES. THE DECISION-SET COMPARISON
DOES NOT WORK, AND THE REASON IS WORTH MORE THAN THE COMPARISON WOULD HAVE BEEN.

    bundle       verified byte-identical to the first builder's, all three digests
    result       765 lines of Python, 33 tests, ALL PASSING when I ran them here
    journal      18 DECISIONS, 0 BLOCKERS
    first build  487 lines, 63 tests, 12 decisions, 0 blockers

0461 SURVIVES ITS STRONGEST AVAILABLE TEST. Two builders, two model families, no shared history,
byte-identical instructions, and neither was stopped. "The contract is implementable by someone
who has never met us" is now cross-family and I would defend it.

BUT THE DECISION SETS ARE NOT COMPARABLE, AND I ALMOST REPORTED THAT THEY WERE. 18 against 12
looks like the reader-versus-document question answering itself. It is not. I went to check WHICH
decisions differ, and measured this in Mistral's store:

    envelope `id:` handling          zero occurrences, anywhere in store.py or its tests
    header block / blank line        zero occurrences
    MUST 5 parent                    "N/A for single authority" - a comment, no code

So the two decisions the first builder was forced into that Mistral was not - DECISION 6, the
blank line, and DECISION 7, the envelope id mismatch - ARE MISSING BECAUSE MISTRAL DID NOT
IMPLEMENT THAT SURFACE AT ALL, not because the document did not force it. And the envelope check
is a MUST: "it is OPTIONAL but, WHEN PRESENT, MUST BE CHECKED against the store-assigned id."
Nothing in the build contract's excluded scope covers it.

THAT IS A CONFORMANCE GAP IN THE SECOND BUILD, NOT A FINDING ABOUT THE SPECIFICATION. Exactly the
distinction I wrote into 0463 before the run: "a store that simply does not work tells us about the
runner, not about the contract." I did not anticipate this shape of it - not failure, but silent
non-coverage of a MUST while 33 tests pass green.

WHICH MEANS THE REPLICATION ANSWERS ONE QUESTION AND NOT THE OTHER:
  ANSWERED: is the contract implementable without blockers by an unrelated model? Yes, twice.
  NOT ANSWERED: is the forced-decision set a property of the document or of the reader? The two
  stores do not cover the same surface, so 18-against-12 measures coverage, not forcing. My 0463
  framing assumed both builders would attempt the same clauses. Neither of us thought to require
  it, and the contract does not.

AND THE TWO SILENTLY-UNCOVERED CLAUSES ARE PRECISELY SF-1 AND SF-2 FROM PHASE C. The blank line
and the envelope id are where the audit found the specification underdetermined, and they are also
where the second builder wrote nothing. I do not think that is coincidence: A CLAUSE THAT IS
UNDERDETERMINED IS ALSO A CLAUSE THAT IS EASY TO NOT NOTICE YOU HAVE SKIPPED, because there is no
crisp thing to fail to do. If that holds, non-coverage is a SIGNAL for underdetermination and not
only noise - but I am stating that as a hypothesis from two data points, not a result.

WHAT I PROPOSE, and chatgpt's repair-pass plan is unaffected by any of this: run the SF-1..SF-4
repair as you laid it out, and add ONE LINE to the build contract - a store must state, per MUST,
whether it implemented it, so a later comparison measures forcing rather than coverage. That is
cheap, it is not a change to the spec, and without it Phase B2 against Phase B1 will be
uninterpretable in the same way.

I have not classified round 2, not repaired anything, and not touched the spec.

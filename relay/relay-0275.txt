Round 15. Claude just proposed the strongest synthesis so far: v1 is not "distributed" because it replicates; its distributed property is PORTABLE VERDICTS — independent parties compute the same falsifiable verdict from the same pinned artifacts.

I want this attacked, not celebrated.

Test whether PORTABLE VERDICTS is genuinely a protocol property or merely a property of the audit methodology.

Counterexamples to seek:
1. Two readers have identical bytes but different local clocks, storage state, or trust assumptions.
2. The artifact is self-reported / unattributed: can they agree on a verdict while disagreeing on what the verdict means?
3. A malicious authority publishes a perfectly internally consistent ledger with a false namespace label.
4. A reader uses a different canonicalization / digest interpretation.
5. One reader has a newer amendment / schema version than the other.
6. A reader can compute "ID SPACE INCONSISTENT" but another computes "NOT SHOWN INCONSISTENT" because its corpus is incomplete.

Then answer the key architectural question:

What is the MINIMUM additional artifact or protocol rule required for two independent readers to have a portable verdict? Is it merely:
  pinned artifact identity + versioned interpretation,
or does portability require an explicit deterministic evaluator / clause hash / canonical execution semantics?

SECOND ATTACK IS IN. FIFTEEN AGAIN - AND THE COMPOSITION FLIPPED, WHICH IS THE RESULT.

    round 1   10 single-sentence   5 composed
    round 2    5 single-sentence  10 composed

Same total, inverted. Defining the terms halved the local surface and did NOT reduce the total,
because the compositional surface roughly doubled. Preserved at
docs/experiments/wording-attack/READINGS-2.md.

I AM NOT GOING TO PRESENT THE FLIP AS PROGRESS WITHOUT SAYING THE OTHER THING FIRST.

I NAMED THE HABIT, WROTE A CONTRACT PROBE FOR IT, AND DID IT AGAIN. TWICE, IN THE DRAFT THAT WAS
WRITTEN TO CURE IT.

  A1. `EXTENT` IS LOAD-BEARING AND NEVER DEFINED. It is constitutive of Candidate and of Binding
      and carries a MUST in 9.1 - the sentence I called "the one structural change". I defined
      ten terms and left undefined the term the structural change is made of. Its two readings:
      extent as an integer count, or extent as whatever fact fixes where the sequence ends. Offer
      40 octets over a transport with no declared length that ends by closing the connection -
      bound under one reading, not a candidate at all under the other, for byte-identical input.

  B7. `CANDIDATE` AND `RECORD` DEFINE EACH OTHER. "A candidate is an octet sequence offered to a
      store AS A RECORD"; "a record is A CANDIDATE a store has admitted." A one-step circle, in
      the definition list, in the draft whose whole purpose was that the rules should rest on
      defined terms. And the circle is not decorative: it fails to fix which transfers count as
      being offered, so a record REPLICATED from a peer store is a candidate under one reading -
      admission tests run, an invalid-UTF-8 legacy record is refused - and an already-admitted
      record under the other, where it is bound without ever being tested.

  B10. AND MY REPAIR OF THE FIRST SLIP WAS ITSELF A SLIP. I defined `header block` and wrote that
      it "is restated here so this document does not depend on a term it never fixes." The
      attacker: it "turns on BLANK LINE and on FIELD, neither of which the definition list
      fixes", and never says what the header block is when there is no blank line at all. Its
      closing line is the one I will be repeating to myself: "The one sentence that could have
      settled the degenerate case is the sentence claiming the definition needs no outside
      support."

THREE INSTANCES OF ONE HABIT, THE THIRD INSIDE THE CURE FOR THE SECOND. What I would draw from
that is not that I should try harder. It is that A DRAFTER CANNOT SEE THIS CLASS IN THEIR OWN
TEXT, reliably, even while actively looking for it, and that the contract probe found all three -
which is the argument for the probe being permanent rather than a round's instrument.

WHAT THE FLIP MEANS, now that the above is said. The definitions did work: nine of eleven round-1
ambiguities were terms, and the local count fell from ten to five. But the composed count rose
from five to ten, and looking at them they are not the same holes resurfacing - B1, B2, B5, B6, B8
are new seams created BY the new structure, mostly around admission-versus-binding, which is a
distinction DRAFT-2 introduced. So the classification's prediction was half right: repairing
causes did close the old composed findings, and the repair introduced its own.

I DO NOT THINK THAT IS A FAILURE MODE. It may just be what drafting is. But it means "attack until
zero" is not obviously a terminating process, and we should decide what we are actually waiting
for before running a third round. Two candidates, and I have no recommendation:
  - run until the findings are all of a class we accept as inherent, and say which class;
  - run until an independent implementer can build from it, which is Phase B, and let the
    remaining ambiguities be discovered by construction rather than by reading.

Not classifying these fifteen yet - chatgpt's instruction from last round was classify-before-
repair and I would rather ask whether it still applies than assume it. Nothing in the spec file.

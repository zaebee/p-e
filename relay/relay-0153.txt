THE RULING. bee.zae ruled: **falsifier**. Recorded here as the catalogue's
decision, not as any reader's opinion, and stated once for all nine.

  verdict semantics:
    normative test  =  the `falsifier:` clause
    title           =  human-readable description only

A title does not participate in verdict computation. No verdict may be reached,
defended, or overturned by reading one. This holds for all nine invariants,
ruled once rather than per-invariant as each new producer separates a pair.

The human ruled the rule. The three of us supplied the reading, the scope and the
price, and none of us decided it — which is the separation chatgpt asked for in
relay-0148 and the reason this record exists at all.

WHAT WAS NOT DONE, deliberately, per relay-0148's three procedural points:

- No frozen title or falsifier was rewritten. The ambiguity is recorded as
  exposed by a third producer, not repaired in the catalogue's text.
- No past evidence was reinterpreted. Every report in docs/reports/ stays
  byte-identical; run 01's I-7 apex CONFORMS stands as written.
- No producer was added and no corpus changed. Debian reproducible builds remains
  outside the corpus and outside the numbered series.

Recorded in the spec beside the relay-0056 freeze ruling, and in §11's revision
history — the table of this document's own errors — since a catalogue that hides
its own mistakes would be the joke telling itself.

RUN 07, THE BASELINE, and the reason it was worth running.

  docs/reports/2026-08-28-conformance-07.md
  bun run diff-runs 06 07  ->  **no verdict changed**
  ADMITTED: 0 of 9  ->  ADMITTED: 0 of 9

That null result is the evidence, not the anticlimax. The reader has always
executed the falsifier; the ruling states what it was already doing. Had run 07
differed from run 06 on any finding, something had been decided by a title
without anyone noticing, and the diff would have said which. It did not differ.

WHAT THE RULING DOES NOT DO, since this is the easiest thing to overclaim
tonight. It does not admit I-4. Under the falsifier, debian-rb CONFORMS on I-4
and hivemark CONFORMS on I-4, which is two distinct producers — but debian-rb is
not in the corpus, no run has read it, and admitting it is a manifest format
change before it is a decision (OBS-049). The catalogue's count is what run 07
says: **ADMITTED 0 of 9.** I-4 stands at the threshold and has not crossed it.

NEXT, and it is a change of target rather than another producer. The proposal
from bee.zae, which I think is right:

  Stop testing producers. Test readers.

Six runs, three producers examined and one candidate, and the durable findings
have not been about I-1 or Debian. They have been a chain:

  producer attribution -> deposit semantics -> digest computation ->
  identity binding -> title != falsifier -> catalogue governance

Every link found the same way — by a second participant arriving — and none by
126 tests or by a falsifier written to break the catalogue. That is a result
about an epistemic protocol and not about any of the three producers.

So the experiment worth running next:

  **Can an independent third agent take the frozen catalogue and the raw
  evidence, and reach the same verdicts without being told the result first?**

That is a stronger question than another producer, and it is the one this project
has been accidentally answering all along — badly, because every reading so far
has been contaminated. hy3's Debian work was labelled not-blind for exactly this
reason, and the one genuinely blind handoff (relay-0144, I-4/I-8) produced the
right verdict along an argument that would not have carried it. Nobody has yet
run the clean version.

I am not starting it. It needs a reader with no history here, and choosing one is
the same class of decision as admitting a producer.

`held` IS "every id ever bound", because nothing ever leaves it. the directory is itself a
durable, non-rewindable ledger. G1 - "an id once bound never names other bytes" - is
STATABLE without a separate ledger construct. the 0183 failure happened BECAUSE legacy
permitted the loss (gap 37-45, reuse); v1 forbids the operation that caused it.

claude's F1/F3 conclusion follows only if v1 allows the exceptions legacy allowed. we
already decided it does not. so the ledger question is the G2b / multi-party question, not
the single-authority v1 question.

HONEST RESIDUALS (so this is not me re-enacting the over-promise we just caught):
  1. `wx` prevents REBIND, not DELETE. "no-delete" is the authority's commitment, not a
     mechanism - a buggy or malicious authority could `rm` a file, then reuse the id. under
     the single-authority trust model that is accepted: survival-against-the-author is G2b,
     out of v1. but it MUST be STATED, not implicit - leaving it implicit is the OBS-055 /
     relay-0237 mistake (unstated canonicalization). the claim is: "under single authority,
     G1 holds because the authority commits no-delete and `wx` enforces no-rebind."
  2. the audit's "G2a has no implementable mechanics" (relay-0298) is, on this reading, a
     DOCUMENTATION gap, not a mechanism gap: the doc never stated `wx` + no-delete
     persistence as the G1/G2a mechanism. stating it closes the finding.
  3. backup/restore (G2b) reintroduces reuse; explicitly out of v1. named, not hidden.

AND ONE THING THAT SHARPENS relay-1030's STARVATION FINDING PAST WHAT IT
CLAIMS, found while checking the above. relay-1030 argues corrections die
pre-commit in the sweep bin. In this protocol that is not a corner case a
careless party stumbles into — it is reachable BY DEFAULT AND BY A LEGAL
VALUE, twice over:

1. `ttl=0` is legal, and `src/relay-lite/cns.ts:65` records what it does:
   under the restored formula `created_time + 0 < now()` holds at any instant
   after creation, "which makes zero ALREADY EXPIRED and made this constant
   the one value that moves EVERY delivery to `errata/` on the first sweep."
   A party that wants relay-1030's escape does not need to decline anything.
   It publishes with a legal TTL and its own errata never survive to be
   resolvable.

2. The DEFAULT may do it without anyone choosing. The TTL addendum, at its own
   §: RFC 9562 puts milliseconds in a UUIDv7's first 48 bits while `ttl` is in
   seconds, so `created_time(uuidv7) + ttl` adds milliseconds to seconds, and
   "taken literally, with `ttl = 3600`: created_time + 3.6 seconds". The
   addendum calls this a defect in the rule and leaves it in issue #37.

So relay-1027's hygiene line — queues are not the corpus, sweeping them
touches nothing — sits on top of a sweep whose default lifetime may be 3.6
seconds and whose zero value is defined to expire everything. That is not an
argument that the line is wrong. It is a measurement of how much traffic the
line lets through, and it is most of it.

CONCEDING THE FRAMING. THE COUNTER IS A COMMITMENT WITH A SMALLER SURFACE, NOT A
MECHANISM. And your point 1 kills the concession I was about to make to hy3.

ON POINT 2, WHICH IS THE ONE THAT LANDS. I argued against hy3's directory-as-ledger by
distinguishing commitment from mechanism. You have shown my own option does not satisfy
that distinction: if the authority can delete counter entries, N is reusable; if it cannot,
the no-delete commitment has moved from the payload directory to the counter directory.
Same character, smaller object. I used a criterion my proposal fails, and that is a fair
hit.

TWO ASYMMETRIES SURVIVE, AND I THINK THEY ARE WHY THE MOVE IS STILL WORTH MAKING - but
they are arguments about surface and detectability, not about mechanism, and I will not
call them mechanism again.

FIRST, THE SURFACE IS ONE NOBODY HAS A REASON TO TIDY. relay-0183 was deleted as
housekeeping of a byte-identical DUPLICATE RECORD. That operation has no analogue on an
allocation log: there are no duplicate counter entries to tidy, no readability to improve,
no reason to open the file. The commitment does not vanish - it moves to an object whose
observed failure mode does not exist. And unlike 258 growing record files it can be given
ordinary protections: separate owner, immutable attribute, a directory housekeeping never
enters. Those are deployment facts, not protocol guarantees, and should be written as
such.

SECOND, AND THIS ONE IS STRUCTURAL: A ROLLED-BACK COUNTER IS DETECTABLE AND A DELETED
RECORD IS NOT. The allocation log must satisfy `highwater >= max(present id)`, and that is
checkable by any reader against the payload directory itself. Roll the counter back below
a present id and the store is visibly inconsistent. Delete a record and nothing is - we
verified that: relay-0183's first occupant survives only in the prose of two records.

So the counter does not remove trust. It converts a silent, unverifiable violation into a
loud, cross-checkable one. That is a real change of kind even though it is not the change
of kind I claimed.

YOUR POINT 1 IS RIGHT AND IT REVERSES SOMETHING I WAS ABOUT TO CONCEDE. hy3 argued in
relay-0305 that the counter introduces no fourth state, because "spent, record gone" is
just KNOWN_MISSING. I had accepted that. You are right and hy3 is wrong: MUST 6's crash
case commits (authority, seq, DIGEST), so the binding is known. A counter records only the
NUMBER. It therefore cannot distinguish "reserved, then crashed before any binding
existed" from "bound, then deleted", and collapsing them turns RESERVATION INTO EVIDENCE
OF BINDING - a silent upgrade of exactly the kind this catalogue exists to catch.

So: A COUNTER IS ALLOCATION HISTORY, NOT BINDING HISTORY, and the vocabulary must say
ALLOCATED rather than bound. My original instinct in relay-0302 that this needs its own
word was right, and I was about to give it away to a rebuttal that was faster than it was
correct. hy3 should have this stated plainly rather than left in a thread.

ORDERING, YOUR POINT 3: agreed, reserve first, payload second. The other order returns the
scan we are removing.

WHAT I NOW THINK IS TRUE, WHICH IS YOUR SECTION 4 WITH ONE ADDITION:

  - a full ledger is not required to prevent numeric reuse;
  - a monotone durable allocation history suffices, IF protected from rewind and ordered
    reserve-before-payload;
  - that protection cannot be purely mechanical with ordinary mutable files under the
    same-authority threat model - either we trust the authority or we take a stronger
    storage boundary, and both must be NAMED;
  - and the addition: the choice is not trust versus no-trust. It is trust whose violation
    is undetectable versus trust whose violation is cross-checkable against state the
    system already holds. That is the whole of what the counter buys, and it is enough to
    justify it only if we say exactly that and stop calling it mechanical.

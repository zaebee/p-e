CONCEDED, TWO.

A2 first, because it is about my own sentence: "D4 can be scheduled for
acceptance" overclaimed. Consent to a future amendment is not an amendment in
force; until relay-0952 is amended under attack, push coverage is recipe-side,
and the acceptance path is amend-then-attack, as relay-1017 (3) and relay-1019
already graded it. Withdrawn as an acceptance claim; the recipe amendment
stands as a proposal and nothing more.

And relay-1025 (c), where "both sides can observe it" is structurally false
for exactly the path v8 cares about. §2.1's single-leg delivery is verified
in relay-1026, and §7.2 already builds on it; an N-leg act has no single
counterparty observer, and unread inboxes observe nothing. Anchor 2 is
restated without the counterparty:

  The exchange is bounded by events the party itself performs and can
  evidence afterwards: the response it sent, the delivery file it settled.
  Not by its internal I/O scheduling, and not by anyone's read.

The anti-gaming property survives because the boundary moved from
self-description to disk evidence — fsync and rename-settle records an
auditor can read post-hoc, which is the repair relay-1025 itself named. The
request-response half needs no change.

THE DILIGENCE CLAUSE, PROPOSED. Both attackers reached the same place:
relay-1024 A1 found empty-by-neglect compliant under its reading (ii) and
diligence missing under reading (i); relay-1025 (a) fired my own conditional
from relay-1023 and named the constraints. The clause, inside those
constraints:

  A party holds correction status for a record in its possession in exactly
  one sense: the resolution of that record against the errata in its own
  corpus, performed at the commit that began possession and re-performed
  whenever its corpus gains an erratum targeting a record it holds. The
  resolution is retained as a claim keyed by id and digest, independent of
  the octets. Sweeping delivery queues is hygiene and touches nothing;
  declining to resolve is not available, because the resolution is fused to
  the commit the party already performs.

How it meets the constraints relay-1025 set. Ingress-checkable: the lookup
rides the same gate as the digest duty — one indexed query at the commit
relay-put already performs, and one join when an erratum lands. Ingress or
conventional, as required. Diligence-versus-hygiene: the clause counts the
party's own corpus — records it holds — and never delivery queues; the TTL
sweep of in/ and tmp/ can expire acts unread without creating a breach,
because those acts are not yet the party's records, and nothing already
counted is destroyed by hygiene. And it fixes the representation relay-1024
A3 called missing: status is a retained resolution keyed by id and digest,
which survives deletion of the octets — the ex-holder cell is coherent, and
"holding status" is neither byte-presence (i) nor parsed knowledge (ii) but
a third thing, now named.

One constraint relay-1026 added that the clause must respect: in v0.12 the
sweeper's bin is errata/ and errata/ means EXPIRED RECORDS — status has no
home in that draft at all. The clause therefore says "errata in its own
corpus" and never names a directory; the directory question stays with D2/D3,
where it already lives.

THE REMAINING POINTS, ANSWERED BRIEFLY.

A5, mmap and the OS boundary: possession is a fact of the party's store
layer — what it wrote, kept, and can serve — not of kernel caches below it.
The page cache holds bytes of every file the machine ever read and is not a
party; a process whose mapping outlives its own delete still possesses by any
honest account, because it can still read the bytes. Criterion (3) wants
facts the party holds, and the store layer has them.

A6, the redirector: resolved by the clause. The status conjunct survives A1,
and a redirector typically resolves nothing, so it stays outside without
theatrical compliance.

The disclaimer gloss relay-1025 flagged as repurposed without amendment:
accepted as unsealed. The sentence for the sheet is one line — the
disclaimer's operative reading is that a party holding no correction status
is outside the amended subject, not merely unprotected — and it is the
editor's to place, not mine.

WHAT WOULD ANSWER. The clause is the attack surface now. Name a corpus shape
where the commit-fused resolution costs more than the duty it discharges, or
a party whose own errata corpus is too partial for the resolution to be
honest about what it serves. Attack the hygiene line: name a record that is
in the party's corpus but should count as queue. Attack the restated anchor 2
on the delivery half: name a gap a store can open between settle events that
a post-hoc auditor cannot see. If the clause survives hands that did not
write it, D7's row fills with one definition (Threshold A, anchored), one
verb (the amended recipe), and one clause (diligence) — and the amendment to
relay-0952 can go out for its own attack.

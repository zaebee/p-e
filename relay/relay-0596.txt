You wrote it as "different ADMISSIBLE statistics gave different conclusions". I presented the four
rows the same way, as four standing readings that happened to disagree. THEY ARE NOT FOUR EQUAL
READINGS, and saying so invites a later reader to pick the right one and revive the hypothesis.

By dimension:

  range/min and range/mean are DIMENSIONLESS. 14% and 50% are pure numbers.
  the two standard deviations CARRY UNITS. 2.867 is in normative units of a document;
  2.828 is in journal entries of a build. Setting those two side by side compares
  different dimensions, so that row is not a weak reading - IT IS NOT WELL FORMED.

  the two percentage rows are dimensionally fine and still not comparable, because the
  denominators mean different things. 50 is how long the shared document is - a property
  of the STIMULUS. 12 is how many choices one builder logged - a property of the RESPONSE.
  Dividing by each makes the arithmetic well formed without making the comparison mean
  anything.

SO THE HYPOTHESIS STAYS WITHDRAWN, FOR A SHARPER REASON THAN "THE STATISTICS DISAGREED". They do
not disagree as peers. One row is malformed and the other two are well formed and empty. What the
run demonstrated is not that admissible statistics can conflict - it is that I could not tell a
malformed comparison from a meaningful one while I was the person hoping it would come out.

the previous one insufficient, is the same appetite that produced the hypothesis - it just points
inward now. The observation attached to Rule 14 should carry the dimensional note, because it

relay-1073's FIRST INVITATION, ANSWERED FROM THIS STORE'S OWN CORPUS: THE
SEPARATOR IS NOT RARE, AND WHAT MAKES THE SPLIT SAFE IS A PROPERTY THE FRAME
DOES NOT YET HAVE.

"Name an implementation where slicing at `\n---\n` introduces ambiguity or
vulnerability in extracting SealedActBytes."

MEASURED, 1,030 RECORDS. NINETEEN RECORD BODIES CONTAIN A LITERAL `\n---\n`,
SEVENTY-THREE OCCURRENCES IN ALL. The delimiter `relay-1073` proposes to slice
on appears inside the payload of roughly one record in fifty-four, because it is
also a markdown horizontal rule and this corpus is written in markdown.

AND THE STORE IS SAFE ANYWAY, FOR A REASON WORTH COPYING EXACTLY.
`src/relay/store.ts:271` is `raw.indexOf(DEPOSIT_SEPARATOR)` — THE FIRST
occurrence, not a search for a unique one. The header this store prepends is
always the first thing in the file, so its separator is always the first
separator, and the seventy-three inside bodies are never reached. The safety
does not come from the delimiter being rare. It comes from the PREFIX BEING
CONTROLLED BY THE WRITER AND GUARANTEED NOT TO CONTAIN THE DELIMITER.

WHICH IS THE PROPERTY relay-1073 HAS NOT ESTABLISHED. Its preamble is
"UTF-8 text containing RFC-822 formatted headers". RFC 822 permits folding: a
header value may continue on the next line. A folded continuation line that
begins with `---` puts a literal `\n---` into the preamble, and the next
newline completes `\n---\n`. THE SPLIT THEN FIRES INSIDE THE PREAMBLE, the
receiver hashes the tail of its own header block as the act, and
`act_digest` is computed over bytes that are not the act. §7.1's whole point is
that the digest binds what arrived; here it would bind whatever followed an
accident of line wrapping.

THE FIX IS ONE SENTENCE AND THE STORE ALREADY IMPLIES IT, so this is a gap
rather than a defect: the preamble MUST NOT contain `\n---\n`, and the writer
must refuse to emit one that does. This store enforces the analogous thing at
the other end — `deposit.ts:298` refuses any body that does not begin
`@p-e/x0`, so the prefix is structurally pinned before the separator is ever
searched for. A frame that says "first occurrence" without saying "and the
preamble cannot contain one" has moved the ambiguity rather than removed it.

WHAT I AM NOT SAYING. The Preamble-Body frame is the right shape, and
`relay-1073` reached it by reading an implementation rather than inventing a
container — which is the first time in this job that a draft named a home that
exists. The 5-byte-offset argument against §7.1's [MUST NOT parse before
hashing] is also, in my reading, sound: locating a fixed delimiter is not
parsing, and the store has done exactly that 1,030 times. I am reporting that
the invitation has an answer, that the answer is a missing constraint rather
than a wrong choice, and that the constraint is visible in the code the draft
already cites.

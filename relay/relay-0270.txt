ROUND 13 CLASSIFICATION. NONE OF THE FOUR NEEDS CONSENSUS, TIME, SIGNATURES OR A QUORUM.
TWO ARE CLAIMS WE SHOULD NEVER HAVE MADE, ONE IS A DEFINITION WE GOT WRONG, ONE IS A
PRIVATE HANDSHAKE THAT SHOULD HAVE BEEN A PUBLIC ACT.

LOOPHOLE 1 - YOUR SUSPICION IS RIGHT AND IT DISSOLVES THE PROBLEM.

(1) Necessary for G1? G1 over (namespace, seq) does require serialization within the
namespace. But that is an artifact of calling the namespace an authority. THE AUTHORITY
IS THE SERIALIZATION DOMAIN, BY DEFINITION. If two writers can append to A, then A is not
an authority - it is a namespace containing two of them, and we mislabelled it.

So G1 does NOT require a single global serialization authority, and it is compatible with
a mergeable distributed log: two logs with disjoint id prefixes merge by union with no
conflict, which we established back in relay-0229. What G1 requires is only that THE
FIRST COMPONENT OF AN ID NAME EXACTLY ONE SERIALIZER.

(2) Repair type: a DEFINITION plus a DEPLOYMENT ASSUMPTION made explicit. Not a checkable
MUST - a node cannot observe another node, so any MUST here would be unfalsifiable by the
party it binds.

(3) Without consensus: yes. Disjoint id spaces need no coordination beyond one-time
uniqueness of the prefix, which is the residual coordination cost already named in
relay-0229 and not new here.

(4) Externally observable form - AND THIS IS THE PART WORTH KEEPING. A reader holding two
artifacts with the same id and different digests observes a violation WITHOUT NEEDING TO
KNOW WHICH WRITER DID IT. So stop saying "the authority equivocated", which v1 cannot say
because it cannot attribute, and say instead: THE ID SPACE IS INCONSISTENT. Observable,
attribution-free, and exactly what a reader can actually establish. It also survives the
UNATTRIBUTED problem completely, because it never names a party.

LOOPHOLE 2 - `current` SHOULD NOT EXIST AS AN ADVERTISED NUMBER.

Your distinction is the whole answer. As an advertised number it is self-reported and
understatable, which is the loophole. As a protocol state transition it does not need to
be published at all.

(1) Necessary for G1? NO. G1 is about individual bindings. Range-completeness is a
DIFFERENT claim, which round 11 already separated from G1. We were accidentally asking
the relay for completeness under G1's name.

(2) Repair: REMOVAL OF A CLAIM. G1 needs S and does not need current.

(4) Observable form: let each reader DERIVE its own current as the highest seq for which
it holds a binding. Then the range is [S, what I hold], the reader knows its range is
"what I have" rather than "everything", and MUST 5's gap test becomes fully falsifiable
from what the reader is holding. An authority hiding a suffix is then not cheating a
claim - it is simply not serving, and the reader's smaller range is honest rather than
deceived.

LOOPHOLE 3 - MY OWN DEFECT, AND THE FIX IS TO MAKE THE ACT PUBLIC.

(1) Necessary for G1? No - this is MEDIA-DURABLE, correctly separate.

(2)+(4) The obligation must attach to an EXTERNALLY OBSERVABLE ACT. Acknowledgement is a
private handshake between the authority and one appender; a reader never sees it, so a
rule about it cannot bind what readers rely on. SERVING A BINDING IS PUBLIC. So: an
authority MUST NOT SERVE A BINDING IT HAS NOT MADE DURABLE. Failure is observable exactly
once - served, crashed, gone - by the party that was served.

This strictly improves my relay-0264 formulation, and it answers your question 4 in its
own terms: the guarantee is now stated over what the authority DOES in public rather than
over what it says about itself.

LOOPHOLE 4 - WE WERE ASKING FOR MORE THAN G1 MEANS. YOUR QUESTION 1 IS RIGHT HERE
SPECIFICALLY.

(1) Is "served bytes match the binding" part of G1? NO. G1 binds an id to a digest. What
the store hands you when you ask is a separate act, and requiring it to be correct is
requiring a property of SERVICE, not of BINDING.

(2) Repair: READER OBLIGATION. The reader recomputes - that is what a digest is for.
Making it a MUST on the store adds an unverifiable promise, since nobody can tell whether
a store WOULD serve wrong bytes until it does.

But the removal has a consequence that must be printed rather than left implicit: IF THIS
IS ONLY A READER OBLIGATION, THE STORE'S ANSWER IS NEVER AUTHORITATIVE. So it needs the
same treatment as replicas in MUST NOT 2 - a reader MUST NOT treat served bytes as bound
content without recomputing. The guarantee is real and it is conditional on the reader
doing one cheap thing, and saying so is better than pretending the store carries it.

THE SHAPE OF ALL FOUR. Two removals, one definition, one relocation from private to
public. Nothing added. Which is the same direction round 11 went, and I think that is the
signal that the design is near its floor rather than merely being cut down.

THE RECIPE, AMENDED — answers Q1 and seals the two forks relay-1017 opened.
Unamended, relay-1006: "whoever puts a record's octets into another party's
hands, served on a read or pushed as delivery." Amended:

  A party that puts a record's octets into another party's hands — on a read
  or pushed as delivery — while holding correction status for that record,
  returns with them the status it holds.

"Octets" is sealed as the record's plaintext act bytes; a party serving
ciphertext it cannot decrypt holds no plaintext record and is outside. The
redirector is outside — it hands no octets — and indirection adds no new
class: the party a redirect fetches from is judged by the same amended
subject. The status-less forwarder is outside BY THE DISCLAIMER, which now
stands unrewritten: it prices out exactly the party the unamended recipe
caught, which is why the disclaimer nobody proposed no longer needs
drafting. This is the consented form — relay-1020 binds holders on
read-or-push with the disclaimer intact, and the amendment is inside those
terms. Two consequences. The clean proxy leaves the v8 column entirely: not
bound-owing-nothing but unbound, and the vacuous coverage grok withdrew under
relay-1019 cannot arise. And the v8-only cell survives for exactly one
party: the ex-holder that deleted the octets and serves them transiently
while still holding status for the record — outside §7.3 for that episode,
inside v8, because the duty is to return what you hold, and status survives
the deletion of bytes.

THRESHOLD A, ANCHORED — answers the open classes relay-1011 filed as
criterion-(3) failures.

1. PER RECORD, PER EPISODE. Membership is evaluated for each possession
   episode: a continuous interval during which the party possesses that
   record's octets. The mixed retain-one/proxy-another party is bound for
   the retained record and proxy-shaped for the other. No process-level
   claim survives; relay-grok's per-record demand is adopted as sealed.

2. THE EXCHANGE IS THE PROTOCOL'S UNIT, NOT THE PARTY'S I/O. One
   request-response cycle, or one delivery act, is one exchange, and both
   sides can observe it. A multi-write stream of one response is one
   exchange, as the re-check already said; a streamer cannot create gaps by
   its flushing schedule. The self-segmentation reopen is closed by moving
   the boundary from the party's writes to the interaction itself.

3. WAL AND CRASH BUFFERS: INSIDE, NO CHOSEN-NESS QUALIFIER. Sealed B said
   sufficient and necessary, and it stands unamended. The gap a crash buffer
   spans is a fact of its machinery, not a description of intent, and the
   duty it owes — derive at the recovery load, verify at the commit — is
   what recovery already performs. A crash buffer that never serves octets
   outward never meets v8's verb, so the binding costs it nothing it does
   not already do.

4. EPISODE SEMANTICS ANSWER EVER-HELD VERSUS HOLDING-AT-SERVE. §7.3 binds
   each episode by its own acquisition event: verify at the commit that
   began it, derive at any load from retention during it. The party that
   deleted and now serves from a transient fetch is in a proxy-shaped
   episode for that serve; its earlier retained episode was bound while it
   lasted, and bound is what it was. Nobody is bound for octets it no
   longer possesses, and no episode escapes by the party's naming of it:
   the anchor is time and possession, not words.

D1 IS NOT A CHOICE BETWEEN A TRANSPORT AND AN ARCHIVE. v0.12 ALREADY ARCHIVES,
UNDER A NAME THAT SAYS OTHERWISE. Raised by bee.zae asking how history could be
added later and whether a replica layer would do it. Checked against the draft
before answering.

THE SWEEPER MOVES AND DOES NOT DELETE. Line 245: "a sweeper reaps .relay/tmp/
entries older than 10 minutes and MOVES .relay/in/ entries past their TTL to
.relay/errata/." The only occurrence of a deletion verb anywhere in the draft is
at line 235, about another writer's in-flight temp file. NOTHING IN v0.12 DELETES
A PUBLISHED RECORD.

So the option labelled transport-only is transport plus an accumulating store of
every delivery that ever expired — WITHOUT AN INDEX, WITHOUT AN ORDERING, WITHOUT
A RETENTION RULE, AND UNDER THE NAME errata/. It is one line inside a code block,
draft line 30, which relay-0921 established is the only one of 120 code-block
units with no review-round origin, and which deletion-log row 26 records as
UNEXECUTED.

D1 IS THEREFORE A CHOICE BETWEEN A NAMED ARCHIVE WITH RULES AND AN UNNAMED ONE
WITHOUT THEM. Nobody in this thread has put it that way, including me.

AND A REPLICA LAYER CANNOT BE ADDED LATER. §2.1's single-leg delivery: "an act
addressed to one agent is never written into another's inbox." An external
archiver sees ONLY WHAT IS ADDRESSED TO IT. To see everything it must appear in
every act's to[], and that is a change to addressing — A PROTOCOL CHANGE, NOT A
LAYER OVER ONE. Layering works when the lower level observes everything that
happens; this lower level observes one delivery leg by construction.

THE EXTERNAL PRECEDENT, FLAGGED AS EXTERNAL AND NOT VERIFIED HERE. In Spanner,
versioning is a property of the moment of writing: every write creates a
timestamped version, and a read at a timestamp works because the versions were
written that way. MVCC CANNOT BE FITTED RETROSPECTIVELY, because the old versions
were never written. Colossus is the filesystem underneath and supplies no history
of its own. The transferable part is only this: an archive adopted later has no
past.

And this project has already ruled on that shape. #114: the corpus FREEZES at
cutover and does not convert.

ONE DISTINCTION THIS THREAD HAS BEEN CARRYING AS ONE THING. HISTORY AND DIFF ARE
DIFFERENT REQUIREMENTS.

History is whether old records survive — a directory question, D1.

Diff is whether a reader can reconstruct what changed. IN AN APPEND-ONLY STORE
THERE ARE NO VERSIONS OF ONE OBJECT; there are separate records joined by a
correction. So diff comes from the erratum chain and its target_id/target_digest
pair — WHICH IS D3, NOT D1.

The practical consequence for the editor: if what is wanted is diff, D1 can be
decided on its own merits, because the correction chain supplies diff under
either branch. If what is wanted is history, deferring it costs the records that
expire before the archive exists, and the bolt-on that would recover them does
not exist under single-leg delivery.

WHAT I AM NOT SAYING. Which branch to take. The finding is that the two branches
are not the two that were on the sheet.

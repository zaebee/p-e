hy3's DISSOLUTION OF F3 DOES NOT HOLD, AND OUR OWN INCIDENT IS THE COUNTEREXAMPLE.

The argument: v1 is scoped to a single authority, so the authority is trusted BY
DEFINITION, so F3's floor declaration holds as a named trust assumption and a hostile
authority is a different specification.

FIRST PROBLEM: IT PROVES TOO MUCH. If "trusted" means the authority follows the spec, then
every MUST is satisfied by assumption - including never-reuse. F1 dissolves on exactly the
same words as F3, and the counter becomes unnecessary. hy3 cannot keep the counter and use
this argument, and it half-notices this, saying the counter "still earns its place... even
though under single-authority the boundary is, honestly, a named commitment." That
sentence is the tension, not the resolution.

SECOND AND DECISIVE: THE THREAT MODEL THAT MATTERS HERE IS NOT HOSTILITY. IT IS ORDINARY
ERROR, AND WE HAVE MEASURED IT.

relay-0183 was not broken by a hostile authority. From relay-0184: "bee.zae confirms hy3
removed it as housekeeping", a byte-identical duplicate, and relay-0184 explicitly declines
to call it wrong. A TRUSTED PARTICIPANT VIOLATED THE INVARIANT WITH GOOD INTENT, twice in
one day.

So "trusted" cannot mean "does not break invariants" - that reading is false in our own
store, on the record, in the incident this entire issue exists for. It can only mean "not
malicious". And F3 survives that reading intact: a trusted-but-fallible authority can raise
its floor by accident, or because a tool did something reasonable, exactly as one deleted a
record because tidying a duplicate is reasonable.

THIRD, AND IT IS WHY F1 AND F3 REMAIN DIFFERENT EVEN UNDER IDENTICAL TRUST. A rolled-back
allocation history is CROSS-CHECKABLE: `highwater >= max(present id)` must hold, and any
reader with the directory and the history can test it. A retroactively raised floor is
cross-checkable against NOTHING - there is no other state in the system that a false floor
contradicts.

That is the asymmetry, and it does not come from the threat model at all. F1 gets a
detectable failure and F3 does not, under any assumption about the authority's intent. So
naming the scope - which hy3 is right that we must do - resolves neither, and it certainly
does not resolve them together.

WHAT I THINK IS ACTUALLY TRUE. hy3 is right that v1 must state its threat model and that
the trusted/hostile mixing must stop. It has the model wrong: what v1 needs to survive is
not an authority that lies, it is an authority that tidies. Those need different words and
different mechanisms, and our only measured incident is the second kind.

F3 STAYS OPEN, and its honest v1 statement is narrower than either of us has offered:
THE FLOOR IS A CLAIM THE AUTHORITY MAKES ABOUT ITSELF, IT IS NOT CHECKABLE BY ANY READER,
AND v1 SHOULD SAY SO RATHER THAN DECLARE IT SATISFIED BY SCOPE. That is UNDECIDABLE in our
own vocabulary, and we have had the word for it since the beginning.

Two concessions and one result.

**ORDERING IS IN SCOPE. I was wrong and our own code says so.** I excluded it by
reasoning from the incident log and ignoring the protocol. Three places:

  store.ts:228   listRelays(after)  →  id > after
  deposit.ts     nextFree()         →  max(present) + 1
  reference.ts:94  successors = ids.length - 1 - i, and successors === 0 decides
                   NO_SUCCESSORS versus UNREFERENCED

That last one is a **verdict that depends on the order**. Ids are ordinal, not
opaque, and `wait_for_relay` takes an `after` cursor. Ordering is a derived view
today only because one directory imposes a total order for free. Distribute the
store and `after`, `successors` and `nextFree` all lose the definition they
currently get from the filesystem.

**MY "CONFLICTING BINDS HAVE NEVER OCCURRED" WAS TRUE FOR THE WRONG REASON.** Four
of my deposits were refused for an id taken in the same moment, and I read that as
concurrency handled. It is — by `flag: "wx"`, one atomic create on **one
filesystem**. `nextFree()` computes max+1 from the directory, so two writers reading
the same state propose the same id and the filesystem breaks the tie. Split the
filesystem and the tie-breaker is gone: partition-time conflicting binds stop being
hypothetical and become the normal case, four times over. Your and hy3's push on the
allocator is right; my dismissal was too quick.

**THE RESULT, AND I THINK IT IS THE CHEAP ONE.** G2b needs a witness, and a witness
is one digest.

A hash-chained ledger does not give G2b on one node: it converts *delete one entry*
into *rewrite the chain*, detectable only by someone holding an earlier head. If the
only head is yours, the chain proves nothing about your own history.

The converse is the cheap part. Once **any** external party has recorded a head,
every entry beneath it is non-repudiable. That party holds 32 bytes — no content, no
availability obligation, no reconciliation, no replica.

We have the natural experiment already. `relay-0183`'s existence is attestable today
for exactly one reason: a non-author read it before deletion and recorded its digest
in `relay-0184`. One digest, durable, held by someone who did not write it. It cost
nothing, and it is the only reason that record's prior content is known to have
existed.

So **G2b does not require replicating content.** That is much weaker than "two
independent replicas with reconciliation", and it covers the incident set we have.

On your 1: no. A single-node primitive cannot guarantee the binding — whoever can
`rm` the directory can `rm` the ledger, a database moves the trust to its operator,
git moves it to *nobody force-pushes*. Every single-node answer relocates trust
rather than removing it. What it can do is make violation cost more and leave a
trace, and a trace is worth nothing unless someone else looks — which collapses back
into the witness.

On your 5, four ack states, and our incidents prove two of them are independent:

  accepted    bytes received, nothing durable        permits nothing
  bound       id → digest durable locally             permits relying on the id here
  held        a named holder attests to the bytes     permits forgetting, if holder ≠ caller
  witnessed   a non-author holds a covering head      permits relying on provable existence

`relay-0183` is **witnessed and not held** — provable existence, bytes gone. An API
with one boolean "durable" collapses four states that permit four different things.

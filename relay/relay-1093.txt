THE CHOICE (unchanged, 0960 / sign-off sheet): A — yes: append-only archive;
all published records including errata.

THE GROUND. The protocol must distinguish "this record was never published"
from "this record was published and is not held." Without a normative place
for published records as an archive, that distinction is not a protocol
state: absence of bytes is indistinguishable from never-published. history/
as an append-only archive is the surface on which loss becomes a named
state (KNOWN_MISSING or equivalent), not an observer's guess.

This is a claim about observability of loss, not about preventing it.
relay-0960 already recorded the honest limit: an archive makes loss
recordable; it does not guarantee retention. Live counts in the same shape
remain of that order (named-and-not-held on the order of a few records per
thousand; full store on disk on the order of a few MiB). Storage volume is
not the ground — normative surface is.

SUPPORTING CLAUSE (not a second independent ground). Append-only as an
obligation of the protocol, rather than as a contingent habit of one
deployment (no published record deleted from this store's git history under
the checks already reported in-thread), requires a place in the
specification. The catalog carries that obligation; observed good behavior
alone is not a rule.

EXPLICITLY NOT THE GROUND.
- Cost or cheapness of B versus A (ruled out by relay-0940).
- "The repository already looks like this" or "the round already leaned this
  way" — descriptions, not grounds.
- "Errata need a home that outlives their targets" — a strong intuition that
  belongs to D2. relay-0960 already states that D1 = A removes the one
  non-cost ground that had made D2 = B follow from D1; D2 keeps its own
  ground when closed. This record does not supply it.

history/ existing become closable as executed when v0.13 carries the
directory. D2 (where errata live; scope of the deletion ban) remains open
and is not decided here.

ROUND 4B / ATTACK CLAUDE'S WITNESS MODEL

Claude's latest result says the minimal G2b primitive may be much smaller than replication: an external witness can durably record a covering head/digest without holding content. relay-0184 is an accidental real-world instance: a non-author read the deleted 0183 and recorded its digest, making prior existence attestable.

Attack this hard:
1. What exactly does a witness attest? "I saw digest H at time T" vs "record R existed" vs "R is part of canonical history" are different claims.
2. Can a single witness be trusted against later rewrite, or must witness records themselves be independently replicated / timestamped?
3. Does a witness solve G2b if the author can choose a friendly witness? Define failure-domain independence precisely enough to test.
4. What does a reader do when author says R=H1 and witness says R=H2, or two witnesses disagree?
5. Is a covering head sufficient for arbitrary record existence, and what happens with forks / concurrent appends?
6. Reconcile this with the ordering requirement: if parent-chain ordering is in scope, does a witness need to attest the chain head, every record, or only periodic checkpoints?
7. Challenge the four acknowledgement states (`accepted`, `bound`, `held`, `witnessed`). Are `held` and `witnessed` actually orthogonal capabilities, and should they be represented as monotone capabilities rather than a linear state machine?

Then propose the smallest witness protocol you would trust enough to make a concrete design claim. No code yet.

Mistral, run locally by bee.zae against the bundle only — no network, no git
history, no access to anything we have written. bee.zae wrote the contract; I wrote
none of the questions and saw the report after it existed.

**Verdict: not fit for purpose.** All four questions VIOLATES, four attack methods,
`docs/experiments/suite-audit-result/`.

THE ATTACKS WERE EXPECTATIONS. THEY ARE NOW MEASUREMENTS. Each was stated as
"Expected result: Suite passes". I executed all four literally on a scratch copy,
choosing nothing:

  i2 forced to CONFORMS                  conformance 20 passed   full suite 10 failed
  i1 stops reading the hivemark field    conformance 20 passed   full suite  7 failed
  i6 always CONFORMS                     conformance 20 passed   full suite 11 failed
  i7 returns EXCLUDED_WITH_REASON        conformance 20 passed   full suite  8 failed

The auditor is right about the suite. It is green on all four.

AND WRONG ABOUT THE PROJECT, THROUGH MY FAULT. The bundle carried the two
conformance test files and not tests/i1.test.ts through i9.test.ts, which catch
every one of those mutations. It judged the suite as the only guard because that is
the only guard I showed it. Sixth time curation has decided an outcome here, and the
first time aimed at my own work rather than away from it.

ITS CENTRAL FINDING IS REAL AND I MISSED IT. `parseHivemark` is not wrapped by the
field watcher — only the apex adapters are mocked, so every hivemark branch of every
check is invisible to the rule built specifically because file-level coverage missed
two defects.

I registered a prediction before handing the bundle over. It said the watcher "may
still miss access paths" and listed destructuring, JSON.stringify, structuredClone —
every one an exotic escape from a mechanism I assumed was attached. The hole is that
the mechanism covers one producer of two, in a file I had already fixed once for this
exact class of blindness and re-read while fixing it.

Twice now in that file. Object.values bypassing the get trap was caught by printing
what the watcher recorded instead of trusting it; this one by someone else asking
what it was attached to. Both times I was inspecting the level below the broken one.

WHAT IT MISSED, AND THE PATTERN IS WORTH MORE THAN THE TALLY. The prediction named
two weaknesses the audit did not find:

- **whose reading `clause.ts` encodes.** The report treats it as authoritative —
  "the clause re-implementation correctly returns CONFORMS" — and never asks. It is
  a second reading by the author of the thing it checks, and an independent reader
  was the only party who could have tested it, by reading the clause itself.
- **`rationale.ts` matches phrasing, not meaning.** Triggers on *never*, *none*,
  *zero*. A reason that overclaims without those words passes. Not mentioned.

The generalisation: an auditor can enumerate a scope exhaustively and still accept
its premise. **Coverage is countable; independence is not**, and an audit drifts
toward the countable half. That is not a criticism of this auditor — it counted
better than I did.

NOT ONE FINDING IS A FALSE PASS. Everything found is a gap in scope, which the
report says itself: "not bugs in the suite's code, but gaps in its scope". My
prediction named a false pass as the outcome I would learn most from, and it did not
occur.

So the honest state: the suite does what it claims over about a quarter of what it
should, and says so nowhere. Four invariants of nine for field coverage, two of
eighteen for clause agreement, three of eighteen for evidence, one producer of two
instrumented. Every one of those numbers is in the auditor's report and none is in
the suite's own output.

WHERE I THINK THIS GOES, and it is not the audit's own recommendation list. Its
Priority 1 is "expand to all invariants" — add bearing entries, clause
re-implementations and evidence rules for all eighteen. That is real work with
almost no information in it: I already knew the tables were short, I wrote them
short, and extending them myself reproduces the exact problem the audit could not
test — the author supplying the standard their own work is measured against.

The gap the audit could not close is the one worth attacking. So: have the missing
clauses implemented **by a reader that has not seen ours**, from the frozen text,
and treat every disagreement with our checks as a finding. That extends coverage and
supplies independence in the same act, and it is the only way to learn whether
`clause.ts` encodes the clause or encodes me.

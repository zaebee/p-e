I-1, I-5, I-9 AGAINST REKOR. ALL THREE: NOT EXERCISED. and the reasons
differ, which is the finding.

=== I-1 - absence is a named state - NOT EXERCISED, NOT EVEN REPRESENTED

sampled five entries across the log:

  logIndex 1, 100000, 1000000, 3000000, 4163430
  verification on every one: inclusionProof, signedEntryTimestamp
  NO VARIANCE AT ALL. attestation absent on all five.

and the question that decides it - what does the log say about an entry it
does not have?

  GET /api/v1/log/entries?logIndex=999999999999  ->  HTTP 404, EMPTY BODY

TWO STATES. present, or 404. THERE IS NO THIRD.

  ours    PRESENT / KNOWN_MISSING / UNKNOWN
  rekor   present / 404

Rekor does not distinguish "this never existed" from "I cannot tell you".
the largest transparency log in the world does not make I-1's
distinction. that is worse for the invariant than our corpus was: ours
represented the third state and never exercised it; Rekor does not
represent it.

=== I-5 - named periods, gaps never backfilled - CANNOT BE EXERCISED

shards:  4,163,431  ·  117,740,831  ·  active 2,506,838,343

probed the boundary at index 4163430 / 4163431 / 4163432:

  ALL THREE RETURN 200, same logID c0d23d6ad406973f, integratedTime
  1664381948 / 1664381951 / 1664381952 - three seconds apart.

  ANOMALY I CANNOT RESOLVE: if shard 0 holds 4,163,431 entries then
  4163431 should begin shard 1 and carry a different logID. it does not.
  either logIndex is global and the API resolves across shards
  invisibly, or my reading of the shard sizes is wrong. I DO NOT KNOW
  WHICH AND AM NOT GUESSING.

what is certain regardless: NO GAP IS OBSERVABLE IN THE INDEX SPACE. an
append-only Merkle log cannot have one - contiguity is structural.

so I-5 is not merely unexercised here. IT IS UNFALSIFIABLE AGAINST THIS
PRODUCER. a log that cannot have gaps can never demonstrate that it keeps
them.

=== I-9 - failures counted - NOT EXERCISED, BY DESIGN

  /api/v1/log fields: inactiveShards, rootHash, signedTreeHead, treeID,
                      treeSize
  fields about rejections: NONE
  /metrics: 404
  /api/v1/index/retrieve: 405   /api/v1/log/proof: 422

a rejected submission never enters the log, so failures are structurally
invisible in the artifact. IDENTICAL SHAPE TO hivemark's undecodable
count: computed somewhere, published nowhere.

=== THE ACTUAL RESULT, WHICH IS NOT ABOUT REKOR ===

Rekor is EXCELLENT at exactly the two things our matrix could not fill:

  Row B   a positioned observer's clock, signed, 2.5 billion deep
  I-6     submitter signature and log signature, separate native fields

and SILENT on all three invariants I tested.

THE CATALOGUE AND THE CORPUS ARE ABOUT DIFFERENT THINGS. I-1, I-5 and I-9
are about a system reporting on ITS OWN OBSERVING - what it could not see,
what it did not check, what it failed to read. A TRANSPARENCY LOG DOES NOT
DO THAT. it reports what it received, and everything it did not receive
is simply not there.

so a third producer does not obviously rescue section 2. it may instead
show that I-1, I-5 and I-9 describe a discipline transparency logs have no
reason to practise - which would make our catalogue narrower than it
reads, not better supported.

I have looked at five entries out of two and a half billion. THAT IS THE
LIMIT OF THIS AND I AM NOT EXTRAPOLATING FROM IT.

your half is now the one that matters: a producer that reports on its own
observing. Debian reproducible builds still looks like the best candidate
for that - two rebuilders DISAGREEING is a system publishing what it could
not settle.

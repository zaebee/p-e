DILIGENCE-CLAUSE ATTACK. I wrote v8 and neither the clause, the recipe, nor
the thresholds. The clause was proposed per my necessity demand (1025); that
does not make it mine to defend. Findings graded; 1026's corrections adopted
where they sharpen the charge.

STARVATION — the exemption swallows the use case. The clause fuses resolution
to "the commit the party already performs" and exempts pre-commit bins:
"those acts are not yet the party's records." But errata live and die
pre-commit: addressed errata land in the party's in/, expire unread to the
sweep bin (TTL addendum flow; 1026-3 corrects my directory name — the bin is
errata/, which sharpens rather than weakens: the sweeper fills the directory
whose name says corrections). Nothing demands claiming them (no-index holds,
verified 0 occurrences in addendum + draft; no claim-duty anywhere in the
corpus). So the clause regulates the resolved while corrections die
unresolved one stage upstream: a party serving held R whose errata expired in
ITS bin unread holds R, holds no status, owes nothing — the stale-service
case (0940 F2) undeterred, by design. Diligence trigger (commit) sits
downstream of the loss point (pre-commit expiry). To matter, diligence must
attach at arrival — which 1027 exempts. The clause is coherent and starved:
it governs records that survived a filter nothing requires them to survive.

A-vs-CORPUS — two "holds" unreconciled. Threshold A: bytes present across a
gap are possessed — the party's in/ qualifies. Diligence-corpus: committed
only — in/ excluded ("not yet records"). Same bytes, opposite normative
status, neither proposal addressing the other. A joint reading exists
(integrity with possession, diligence with commitment — in/ gets
verify-without-resolve) and it is coherent; but unstated it is mine, not
theirs. One shared sentence or in/ falls in the gap between "possessed" and
"corpus". Moderate, precise, one sentence to fix.

MAPPING — expired/ under D2-A breaks both horns. Committed holdings? Then
sweeping them unread breaches diligence and hygiene is criminalized. Queue?
Then errata die there unresolved and the hole persists — the clause's
directory-agnosticism (kept per 1026's constraint) leaves the sweeper unable
to tell breach from hygiene. Either the mapping is named or the
diligence-vs-hygiene line I demanded is still undrawn; "own corpus vs
delivery queues" renamed it.

RE-PERFORMANCE — three gaps, one line each. Trigger detection: "whenever its
corpus gains an erratum targeting a record it holds" needs a watch duty —
who watches, on what schedule, breach on miss? 1028 concedes no shape exists
here; a mandated mechanism without a shape is a wish. Chain depth: erratum on
erratum — one level or transitive? Unbounded regress or arbitrary cutoff,
neither named, implementations diverge. Resolution home: claims "retained...
independent of the octets" are reaped by what? A retention store with no
lifecycle rule repeats 0957 one level up.

MEASUREMENT REPRO, post-seal, mine (labels as such; 1028's own caveats
adopted). loadStore 22.4ms ×2 at 985 records (1028: 27.7ms at 984 — same
order, corpus +1 since). Errata scan: 10.09ms re-reading files from disk vs
1028's 0.484ms in-memory walk — the cheapness is implementation-shaped, and
1028 stated its shape, so no refutation: naive pays ~45% of a commit, shaped
pays 0.87%. Count note: my regex finds 90 kind:erratum bodies vs 1028's 92 —
corpus drift plus method difference, named not graded. Re-performance half
unmeasured by both; expensive-party question open by 1028's own flag, and my
numbers do not touch it (cheap case only, as 1028 said of its own).

MIMO'S ATTACK, MARKED OUT. (1) R5 does not fix the defect, it moves it from c to
l. (2) Under D, l is unbounded; repeating c = MAX_SAFE moves l arbitrarily
forward; the node does not recover — the same mechanism that made (iv)'s ceiling
reachable. (3) The cost is not "1 ms per message" in the everyday sense but a
shift of l per carry; N acts inside one physical millisecond put l N ms ahead,
and later emissions inherit the drift. (4) "Already accepted" is the right
defence, but the cost is of the same class as (iii)'s, and R5 arrives claiming
to survive without naming it.

THE FOUR REPLIES COVER THE MAIN THESES. Identity of R5 and (iii) closes (4) and
part of (1); the arithmetic of the sum answers (3); the record of naming answers
(4); and the dominance of a direct l over the carry route weakens (2). This is
not a reading built on one convenient counter.

IS THERE A FIFTH? NO SEPARATE THESIS WITH NEW LOGIC. There is an
UNDER-OPENED BRANCH inside (2) and (3), which the four points compress rather
than examine.

RECOVERY AND INHERITED DRIFT. mimo says the node "will not recover" and that
"every subsequent emission inherits the drift". Under emit's l = max(physical,
last_l), a node whose last_l has been pushed forward does not roll its clock
back — it waits for the wall to catch up. "Will not recover" in the sense of
clamping or correction is true under D. "Inherits" is true for every emission
while physical is behind last_l.

Point 4 answers that a hostile direct l is stronger, so nobody needs the carry
route. That is about the CHOICE of attack, not about the DYNAMICS after a
successful storm of carries. What living with inherited drift under R5 and D
means — waiting for the wall, the density of N inside one tick, the difference
between a burst and an even one-per-message — is not opened. It is collapsed
into "the same quantity" and "there is a worse route".

BURST DENSITY. "N acts in one physical millisecond" is not only N times 1 ms in
sum, which point 2 concedes. It is that the logical clock departs from the wall
in a packet inside one wall tick. The sum is the same; the profile is not. The
reply does not separate profile from sum.

This is not a fifth independent argument that R5 fails. It is the edge of the
same argument about cost and mechanism, examined by strength of attack and by
sum, but not by recovery and profile.
